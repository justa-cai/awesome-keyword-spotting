## Problem Statement

With the widespread application of humanoid robots in home and service scenarios, Keyword Spotting (KWS) and Sound Source Localization (SSL) have become crucial for robot interaction. However, humanoid robots face unique acoustic challenges:

1. **Echo Interference**: The content played by the robot's own speakers generates echoes, which severely interfere with the user speech signals received by the microphones.
2. **Mechanical Noise**: The mechanical noise generated by motors and joints during robot movement is non-stationary, making it difficult for traditional noise reduction methods to handle.
3. **Indoor Reverberation**: Room reverberation in home environments varies with factors such as room size and furniture layout.
4. **Superposition of Multiple Noise Sources**: The aforementioned noise sources coexist, forming complex acoustic scenes.

Previously, there was a lack of large-scale open-source datasets and standardized evaluation platforms specifically for humanoid robot scenarios, leading to slow progress in related research. Existing KWS and SSL studies were mostly based on clean or simple noise conditions, failing to reflect the complex acoustic environments encountered in real-world robot deployments.

## Methodology

### Challenge Design

This paper organized a speech challenge centered around UBTECH's Alpha-mini humanoid robot. The robot is equipped with a 4-microphone array, providing researchers with a standardized evaluation platform.

### Dataset Construction

The challenge provides a rich variety of open-source datasets:

1. **Keyword-Train**: Single-channel keyword and speech data, used to train the basic acoustic features of KWS models.
2. **Noise-Train**: Indoor noise recordings, covering various types of indoor environmental noise (air conditioning, television, conversation, etc.).
3. **Echo-Train**: Simulated echo data, generated by simulating echoes produced by the robot's speaker playback.
4. **Echo-Record**: Real-recorded echo data, actually collected on the Alpha-mini robot.
5. **Noise-Mech**: Mechanical noise data, recording the noise generated when various joints of the robot move.
6. **KWS-Dev/Eval**: Development and evaluation sets recorded in real robot scenarios.

### Two Challenge Tracks

**KWS Track (Keyword Spotting)**:
- **Objective**: Detect predefined keywords in complex acoustic environments.
- **Evaluation Metric**: Detection Error Rate (DER), which comprehensively considers false alarms and missed detections.
- **Data Conditions**: Includes various interference sources such as noise, echo, and mechanical noise.

**SSL Track (Sound Source Localization)**:
- **Objective**: Estimate the Direction of Arrival (DOA) of the speaker relative to the robot.
- **Evaluation Metric**: Angular Error.
- **Method**: Utilizes spatial information from the 4-microphone array for direction estimation.

### Baseline Systems

- **KWS Baseline**: An end-to-end model based on attention mechanisms, which directly predicts keywords from audio features.
- **SSL Baseline**: A sound source localization method based on SRP-PHAT (Steered Response Power with Phase Transform).

## Main Contributions

1. **Large-scale Open-source Dataset**: For the first time, a large-scale dataset containing real noise, echo, and mechanical noise is provided for humanoid robot KWS and SSL tasks. The data is actually recorded on the Alpha-mini robot, possessing high authenticity and practical value.
2. **Standardized Evaluation Platform**: Two challenge tracks and standardized evaluation metrics are defined, providing a foundation for fair comparison of different methods and promoting academic research in this direction.
3. **Baseline Systems**: Baseline systems are provided for both tracks, lowering the research threshold and enabling researchers to quickly get started and improve upon these bases.
4. **Multi-channel Data Support**: The 4-microphone array data supports both KWS and SSL tasks simultaneously, providing a data foundation for research on joint optimization of multiple tasks.
5. **Real-scenario Diversity**: The data covers various room configurations (different sizes, furniture layouts, and reverberation characteristics), enhancing the practicality of the research.

## Experimental Results

### KWS Baseline Results
- On the development and evaluation sets, the KWS baseline performs well under clean conditions.
- Performance drops significantly after adding noise, echo, and mechanical noise, reflecting the challenges of real-world scenarios.
- Different noise sources affect different keywords to varying degrees, pointing out directions for subsequent research.

### SSL Baseline Results
- The SRP-PHAT method achieves high angular estimation accuracy under low-noise conditions.
- Localization accuracy drops significantly under high-noise and strong reverberation conditions.
- Multi-source scenarios are the main challenge for the SSL track.

### Challenge Impact
- The openness of the dataset has promoted research progress in KWS for robot scenarios.
- The design of the two tracks allows researchers to focus separately on their respective technical difficulties.
- The baseline systems provide participants with reasonable performance references.

## Limitations and Future Work

### Methodological Limitations
- **Hardware Specificity**: The dataset is specific to the Alpha-mini robot hardware. Parameters such as microphone array layout and speaker positions differ from other robot platforms, and the generalizability of the data remains to be verified.
- **Limited Room Configurations**: The number of room configurations in the evaluation scenarios is limited and may not cover all real-world deployment environments.
- **Simple Baseline Methods**: The baseline systems adopt relatively simple technical solutions, creating a gap in complexity compared to actual industrial systems.
- **Challenge Format Constraints**: As a challenge paper, methodological details are mainly concentrated on the description of baseline systems.

### Future Directions
- Extend to more robot platforms to establish cross-platform universal datasets.
- Increase the variety of room configurations and noise conditions to enhance the diversity and representativeness of the data.
- Explore joint optimization methods for KWS and SSL, leveraging the complementarity between the two tasks.
- Introduce adaptive noise reduction and echo cancellation techniques as front-end processing modules.
- Research online learning and continuous adaptation methods to enable robots to continuously optimize performance during deployment.
