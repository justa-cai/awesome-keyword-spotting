## Problem Statement

### Problem Background and Domain Pain Points

Keyword Spotting (KWS), also known as Wake-Word Detection (WWD) in engineering, is the task of detecting predefined keywords in real-time from continuous audio streams on devices with strict memory and computational constraints. It serves as the primary entry point for voice interactions in smart speakers, earbuds, and in-car assistants. The paper's introduction points out that with the rapid development of multimodal large models and end-to-end speech foundation models, building more intelligent and personalized voice assistants has become feasible. Consequently, user attention has shifted, making the accuracy and efficiency of the interaction entry point itself a bottleneck component. The engineering constraints for such systems are threefold: they must run continuously in a streaming manner (cannot wait for the entire sentence to end), the false alarm rate must be suppressed to an extremely low level (otherwise the device would be randomly woken up), and the computational and storage overhead must be small enough to fit into edge chips. The goal stated in the abstract is to achieve SOTA on both fixed-keyword and arbitrary-keyword datasets simultaneously, maintain robustness in noisy environments, while the model has only about 3.3M parameters and the decoding speed is 47%–63% faster than the frame-synchronous baseline.

The paper categorizes existing KWS approaches into two main types (in the introduction). The first type is end-to-end pattern matching or multi-label classification methods: the model processes audio in streaming chunks frame-by-frame and directly determines whether the current chunk is activated by a keyword. These methods are simple and direct, but as segment-level classification models, they are highly sensitive to training data—performance is acceptable in quiet, controlled environments but unstable in diverse real-world scenarios, with poor noise robustness. A more fundamental flaw is that the model is coupled with the keywords used during training, making it impossible to naturally support arbitrary keyword (user-defined) detection. The second type is a two-stage framework based on ASR: the acoustic model is first trained using ASR criteria, and then a decoding algorithm processes frame-level posteriors. Sequence-to-sequence training allows the acoustic model to adapt to diverse acoustic conditions, remaining robust in complex environments. Moreover, the acoustic model is decoupled from the keywords, naturally supporting arbitrary keyword detection, offering much higher flexibility than the classification route. Thus, the core challenge of the ASR route lies in the design of the decoding algorithm—how to accurately and quickly determine "a keyword has appeared" under streaming conditions.

### Specific Shortcomings of Existing Methods

The paper lists four specific shortcomings within the ASR route (in the introduction and abstract):

- **Complex WFST Decoding Graph**: The traditional approach constructs a Weighted Finite State Transducer (WFST) decoding graph that integrates keyword paths and filler paths. While functionally complete, it has high implementation and maintenance complexity and a high engineering threshold.
- **General ASR Decoding Not Tailored for Keywords**: Greedy search and prefix beam search for CTC, and greedy or autoregressive beam search for RNN-T, expand transcription hypotheses over the complete search space. They do not explicitly set priorities for keyword detection, often resulting in suboptimal detection performance.
- **Error Accumulation in Single-branch Transducer Systems**: The authors' previous TDT-KWS (ICASSP 2024) was already superior to RNN-T systems, but the posterior prediction of the current frame in Transducer depends on the history of the predicted sequence. Errors accumulate gradually along the decoding process, weakening the model's ability to distinguish positive samples from difficult negative samples. Performance degrades under complex acoustic conditions (noise, confusable negative samples). This is the shortcoming this paper aims to directly address.
- **CTC-AED Joint Framework Incompatible with KWS**: Multi-task joint training is already a standard paradigm in ASR (e.g., joint training of CTC and attention encoder-decoder, CTC/attention multi-head decoding). However, the computational overhead of attention mechanisms and the significant latency introduced by token-level inference make such systems not universally applicable to KWS, as cited in [38][61]. In other words, existing "joint training + multi-head decoding" experiences from the ASR field cannot be directly copied; a lightweight joint framework and frame-level fusion algorithm need to be redesigned for KWS.

### Key Challenges to Be Solved

In summary, this paper faces four interlinked challenges: First, how to introduce CTC-Transducer joint training into streaming KWS, allowing CTC's conditional independence to counteract Transducer's error accumulation, without introducing the overhead of attention. Second, how to design keyword-specific streaming decoding (rather than general transcription decoding) so that the decoding space revolves around keywords. Third, once both branches use frame-skipping mechanisms (TDT on the Transducer side, PSD on the CTC side), the scores of the two branches are no longer aligned on the time axis. "How to fuse" (fusion method, determining system effectiveness) and "when to fuse" (fusion timing, determining efficiency) become two questions that must be answered (as proposed in the original text of Section II-D). Fourth, how to skip frames irrelevant to keywords to accelerate decoding without sacrificing accuracy, and maintain stability in challenging scenarios such as noise, difficult negative samples, and arbitrary keywords.

## Methodology

### Overall Architecture Design and Design Motivation

The overall structure of MFA-KWS is "one shared encoder + two decoding heads + one frame-asynchronous fusion decoder" (Figure 1 left shows the full pipeline for training and inference). The selection and rationale for each component are as follows:

- **Shared Encoder**: 6-layer DFSMN (Deep Feedforward Sequential Memory Network), hidden layer 512, projection 320. The overall architecture and hyperparameters follow the settings of Tiny Transducer. Why DFSMN instead of Conformer or Transformer: The paper explicitly cites [21][39][40][42][73]–[75] to explain that DFSMN is a lightweight, effective, and mainstream encoder for the KWS task—it is a feedforward memory block structure, has no self-attention, is friendly to streaming inference, has high parameter efficiency, and matches edge-side constraints.
- **CTC Auxiliary Encoder**: Two additional DFSMN blocks and a linear projection layer are added on top of the shared encoder, dedicated to the CTC head. Why this design: The bottom-level acoustic representations are shared by both branches (saving parameters), while the top-level branches are private (providing independent capacity for CTC). This is a common practice in joint training frameworks, allowing both losses to be adapted to their respective representations with almost no increase in total model size.
- **Predictor**: Uses a stateless implementation (from NeMo [60]), with context size 2 and embedding dimension 320. Why use a stateless predictor: Removing the predictor's recurrent state makes streaming decoding state maintenance lighter and the inference path simpler.
- **Joiner**: Transforms the 320-dimensional encoder and predictor outputs to a 256-dimensional representation, activates, and projects to the final output.
- **Modeling Units**: English uses 70 monophones (from CMU dictionary cmudict-0.7b), Chinese uses 200 syllables (DaCiDian dictionary), plus respective special blank symbols; the TDT head additionally outputs duration options from 0 to $D_{max}$. Why use small vocabularies at the phone/syllable level instead of subwords or characters: Arbitrary keyword detection requires expanding any user word into a sequence of acoustic units. A small output layer supports out-of-vocabulary keywords and keeps the decoding lattice scale controllable.
- **Parameter Count**: English and Chinese experiments uniformly use approximately 3.3M parameters. The parameters added for TDT duration prediction account for less than 0.1% of the total. Why emphasize this number repeatedly: Frame-asynchronous acceleration comes at almost no parameter cost, which is evidence for this paper's stance that "efficiency improvements come from decoding algorithms, not larger models."
- **Loss Function**: $\mathcal{L}_{MFA} = \mathcal{L}_{TDT}(x,y) + \alpha \mathcal{L}_{CTC}(x,y)$ (Eq. 8), where $\alpha$ is default 0.3 (stated at Eq. 6; the paper does not report ablation for the basis of $\alpha$'s value). Why joint training is effective (three reasons given in Section II-B): First, CTC is conditionally independent along the time axis; each frame's prediction does not depend on historical outputs, naturally avoiding the error accumulation of Transducer, suppressing false alarms, and preventing the model from overfitting to keywords. Second, CTC acts as a regularizer for acoustic modeling in the multi-task framework, improving convergence. Third, the two branches can serve as mutual verification during inference, obtaining more stable confidence through fusion than any single branch.

### Mathematical Principles of Core Algorithms

**Transducer Training and Decoding Lattice**. Given audio $x=\{x_1,\dots,x_T\}$ and transcription $y=\{y_1,\dots,y_U\}$, the RNN-T loss is $L_{RNN\text{-}T} = -\log\sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{B}^{-1}_{RNN\text{-}T}(y)} p(\pi|x)$ (Eq. 1), which sums over all valid augmented alignment paths. Decoding is performed on the $(t,u)$ lattice (Figure 2): $\delta(t,u)$ is defined as the score of the highest-scoring path reaching node $(t,u)$; the probability of emitting the $(u+1)$-th keyword unit to the right at node $(t,u)$ is $y(t,u) = P(y_{u+1}|x_{[1:t]}, y_{[0:u]})$ (Eq. 2), and the probability of staying down (outputting blank) is $\phi(t,u) = P(\varphi|x_{[1:t]}, y_{[0:u]})$ (Eq. 3). The keyword confidence at time $t$ is $S[t] = \delta(t,U)\cdot\phi(t,U)$ (Eq. 4), meaning: after the path completes emitting all $U$ units of the keyword, it is immediately followed by a blank frame to confirm the activation end. Recursion is completed using dynamic programming (Algorithm 1, lines 9–11), with complexity growing linearly with the number of frames and keyword length.

**Three Decoding Enhancements** (key changes relative to the authors' previous work, Section II-A): First, introduce a bonus score $S_{Bonus}$ to sharpen activation time boundaries, improving keyword endpoint determination accuracy. Second, introduce a timeout $T_{out}$, directly zeroing out decoding paths whose length exceeds the threshold, filtering out overly long hypotheses. Third, normalize the optimal path score by length $S[t] = \mathrm{pow}(S_{Bonus}\cdot S[t],\,1/\ell(t))$ (Algorithm 1, line 29), compressing the score into the [0,1] interval. Why normalization is necessary: Paths ending at different times have different lengths, making raw multiplicative scores incomparable. After normalization into confidence, the scores of the CTC branch and Transducer branch have a unified dimension, making subsequent multi-head fusion (Eqs. 14–16) valid.

**TDT (Token-and-Duration Transducer)**. Conventional RNN-T models $P(v|t,u)$, while TDT extends it to a joint distribution of token and duration $P(v,d|t,u)$, decomposing it under the conditional independence assumption as $P(v,d|t,u) = P_T(v|t,u)\cdot P_D(d|t,u)$ (Eq. 7), where $d\in\{0,1,\dots,D_{max}\}$. Why do this: Once the model can predict "how many frames this token will last," the decoder can advance $d$ frames at once instead of frame-by-frame, upgrading the frame-synchronous system to a token-level asynchronous system, skipping many blank stay frames. Algorithm 1, lines 15–21 show that TDT takes $d=\arg\max_d P_D(d|t,G)$ at each step, and the scores of the subsequent $d-1$ skipped frames are directly set to 0 (which become placeholders PH in the multi-head framework).

**PSD (phone-synchronous decoding, frame-skipping on CTC side)**. Utilizing the peaky nature of CTC outputs (peaky: each frame's prediction is very confident, with maximum probability close to 1), decompose the probability of a complete alignment into the product of non-blank frames and blank frames (Eq. 9), and then approximate the blank probability for frames with $p(\varphi|x_t)\ge\lambda_\varphi$ as 1 (Eqs. 10–12), so that the probability of the entire path is approximately equal to the product of only the speech content frames with low blank probabilities (Eq. 13). $\lambda_\varphi$ is a preset threshold, determining the frame-skipping ratio. Why this approximation is safe: The skipped frames are judged by CTC as almost certain blanks, and their contribution to the path probability is already close to 1. Discarding them loses almost no alignment probability but saves decoding computation on those frames.

**CDC Sliding Window Consistency Fusion**. The fusion weight at time $t$ is $w_t^{CDC} = f_{sim}(S^{Trans}_{[t-\ell_{win}:t]}, S^{CTC}_{[t-\ell_{win}:t]})$ (Eq. 15), where $f_{sim}$ is cosine similarity, and the window $\ell_{win}=20$ is fixed for all experiments (to ensure fairness and reproducibility); fusion is performed as $S_t^{Fused} = (S_t^{Trans} + w_t^{CDC}\cdot S_t^{CTC})/(1+w_t^{CDC})$ (Eq. 16). Why use trend consistency for weights: An isolated high score on a single frame is likely a false alarm. If the score trends of two structurally different branches (one with language context, one conditionally independent) are highly consistent within a window, it indicates that the activation in that segment is a real acoustic event. At this point, higher weight is given to the CTC branch; when consistency is low, it automatically degrades to being dominated by the Transducer branch.

### Key Technical Innovation 1: Keyword-Constrained Transducer Streaming Decoding

Unlike ASR decoding where partial hypotheses are fed to the predictor, this paper's streaming decoding only feeds the keyword token sequence to the predictor, which is equivalent to the inference version of RNN-T loss calculation and teacher forcing during training (Section II-A). Simultaneously, set $\delta(t,0)$ to 1 for each time $t$, allowing the keyword to start at any time in the audio stream. Why this design: KWS does not require generating a complete transcription, only determining whether a keyword appears. Hard-constraining the decoding space to the keyword phone sequence eliminates combinatorial explosion in the decoding space and avoids error accumulation after autoregressive hypotheses go astray. This is the foundation for all subsequent work—streaming decoding for the CTC branch (CTC-Streaming-Decoding, idea from team [40]) and multi-head fusion are all built on the abstraction of "keyword lattice + arbitrary start point + frame-level confidence."

### Key Technical Innovation 2: CTC-Transducer Joint Training and Multi-head Frame-synchronous Decoding (MFS-KWS)

MFS-KWS is the first system contribution of this paper: train a dual-head model using the joint loss $\mathcal{L}_{MFS} = \mathcal{L}_{RNN\text{-}T} + \alpha\mathcal{L}_{CTC}$ (Eq. 6, $\alpha=0.3$). During inference, run Transducer streaming decoding and CTC streaming decoding in parallel according to Algorithm 2, gradually merging the frame-level confidence of both branches $S_t^{Fused} = S_t^{Trans} \oplus S_t^{CTC}$ (Eq. 14). Why introduce joint training in KWS: The error accumulation problem of single-branch Transducer (Introduction) is exactly counteracted by the conditional independence of CTC, while the shortcoming of CTC lacking context is compensated by the language modeling capability of Transducer. Mutual regularization during training also improves convergence (in Table III, each single branch head from joint training exceeds the corresponding single-branch training baseline).

### Key Technical Innovation 3: Dual-head Frame-asynchronization (TDT + PSD) and Placeholder PH

MFA-KWS asynchronizes both heads on top of MFS: the Transducer head is replaced with TDT (token-level frame-skipping), and the CTC head replaces frame-synchronous decoding FSD with KWS-customized PSD (threshold-based frame-skipping). The frame-skipping positions of the two branches are different, leading to moments where "one branch has a score, the other is skipped" during fusion. Therefore, a placeholder PH is introduced to represent the decoding score of skipped frames, with its specific value determined by the fusion strategy (Section II-D). Why explicit definition of PH is necessary: The time axis of a frame-asynchronous system is no longer neatly aligned. PH is the semantic convention that pulls asynchronous sequences back to a unified time grid, and it is the direct answer to the "when to fuse" question—on skipped frames, the system uses the fill value of PH for fusion, rather than forcing both branches to compute synchronously.

### Key Technical Innovation 4: Five Fusion Strategies and the Victory of CDC-Last

The paper systematically explores five fusion strategies (right side of Figure 1): **CTC-Dom** (CTC dominates: use CTC head score directly if normal, fill with Transducer head if skipped, set double PH to 0), **Transducer-Dom** (symmetric logic), **Equivalence-Dom** (equal weight for both branches: average if both have scores, use directly if only one has score), **CDC-Zero** (consistency fusion: PH filled with 0, assuming no acoustic event in skipped frames), **CDC-Last** (consistency fusion: PH filled with the most recent non-placeholder value, treating skipped frames as a continuation of the most recent state). The first three are single-frame-level strategies, simple and interpretable but ignoring historical activation states; the latter two are temporal consistency strategies. Experimental conclusions (Table IV) show CDC-Last is comprehensively optimal. Why CDC-Last wins (explanation given in the paper): Frame-skipping by TDT and PSD is asynchronous and locally continuous. The true state of skipped frames is more likely to continue the most recent decoding state rather than reset to zero. Resetting the state to 0 artificially creates score discontinuities, destroying the estimation of sliding window cosine similarity.

### Technical Differences with Existing Methods

- **Vs. Previous Work TDT-KWS**: Training changes from pure TDT loss to CTC+TDT joint loss (Eq. 8); inference changes from single-branch decoding to dual-head asynchronous decoding with fusion. In Table III, under the same decoding conditions, joint training plus multi-head decoding pushes recall at FAR=0.05 from 98.85% to 99.80%.
- **Vs. WFST Route**: Does not construct a decoding graph; the decoding lattice expands only around the keyword phone sequence. Changing keywords only requires changing the phone sequence, with low engineering maintenance cost.
- **Vs. CTC/Attention Multi-head Decoding (ASR Paradigm)**: Retains the idea of "joint training + multi-head inference" but completely avoids attention—frame-level score fusion replaces sequence-level output fusion, with no token-level autoregressive latency.
- **Vs. General Greedy/Beam Search**: Keyword-specific search space, arbitrary start point initialization, bonus boundary sharpening, timeout filtering, and length normalization are all mechanisms customized for "detection" rather than "transcription." Table VIII shows that general decoding (greedy, beam search) significantly lags behind streaming-specific decoding on LibriKWS-20, which is evidence.
- **Vs. Original PSD**: Classic PSD ([63][64]) is phone-synchronous decoding on the global CTC lattice. This paper transforms it into a version for streaming KWS, combines it with CTC-Streaming-Decoding ([40]), and for the first time, coordinates it with TDT's token-level frame-skipping in a multi-head framework.

## Experimental Results

### Datasets Used and Their Scale

Experiments cover three scenarios (Section III-A): English fixed single keyword, Chinese fixed dual keywords, arbitrary keyword continuous speech detection, plus noise robustness evaluation.

- **Hey-Snips** (English, "Hey Snips" single phrase, no pause wake-word): Training/development/test positive samples are 5,799 / 2,484 / 2,529 clips, negative samples are 44,860 / 20,181 / 20,543 clips; negative samples have no complete transcription, used only for false alarm evaluation, not involved in training; all negative samples from the three sets are merged to form an approximately 97-hour false alarm evaluation set.
- **MobvoiHotwords** (Chinese, "Hi Xiaowen" and "Nihao Wenwen" dual keywords): Total approximately 262 hours, 287k sentences; recorded at different distances from smart speakers, mixed with home noise such as music and TV, with varying SNR; positive samples per keyword for training/development/test are approximately 21.8k / 3.7k / 10.6k clips, negative samples are 131k / 31.2k / 52.2k clips; negative test set is approximately 63 hours. Since the original data has no negative sample transcriptions, a powerful Chinese ASR model Paraformer-large is used to generate pseudo-transcriptions, followed by random sampling of common sentences for fine-tuning.
- **LibriSpeech and LibriKWS-20**: LibriSpeech 960 hours of read speech is used for acoustic training; 20 words are selected from its two test sets as keywords (Table I: almost, anything, behind, captain, children, company, continued, country, everything, hardly, himself, husband, moment, morning, necessary, perhaps, silent, something, therefore, together), forming two subsets: test-clean and test-other; test audio without keywords are concatenated to form 3-hour false alarm evaluation sets.
- **AISHELL-2**: 1000 hours of Mandarin, used to train the Chinese seed acoustic model.
- **WHAM!**: Urban environment near-field and far-field noise library (music, instruments, restaurant/bar sounds), used for mixing noise on the test side.
- **Data Strategy**: Arbitrary keyword experiments are trained directly on LibriSpeech-960h without fine-tuning to examine generalization; fixed keyword experiments are first pre-trained on LibriSpeech-960h or AISHELL-2, then fine-tuned using wake-word positive samples plus an equal amount of non-keyword general data; for Mobvoi, due to difficult acoustic conditions, its own non-keyword data (with pseudo-transcriptions) is used as general data. Noise mixing protocol: Negative sample training data is mixed with WHAM! noise with SNR uniformly distributed from 0–20 dB; positive sample testing is mixed at fixed SNR {0, 5, 10, 15, 20} dB; Mobvoi itself contains noise and cannot control SNR, so it is excluded from noise experiments.

**Training Configuration** (Section III-B): Input 40-dimensional FBank (25 ms window, 10 ms frame shift); online speed perturbation {0.9, 1.0, 1.1}; SpecAugment frequency mask F=10, time mask T=50, 2 masks of each type taken per sample; 5 frames concatenated on left and right to form 440-dimensional (40×11) context features; frame skip parameter 3 (3x downsampling). Local batch 64, max 12,288 frames per batch; AdamW, peak learning rate 1e-3, 10k steps warmup; learning rate halved if validation loss does not decrease, training stops if no improvement for 3 consecutive epochs.

### Definition and Rationale for Evaluation Metrics

- **recall and miss rate**: recall is defined as the number of true positives divided by the total number of positive samples, miss rate = 1 − recall; **FAR** (False Alarm Rate) is the number of false alarms divided by the total duration of negative samples (clips/hour). Reporting recall at several fixed FAR levels is the standard evaluation method for KWS—because it directly characterizes the trade-off from a deployment perspective: "how many can be detected under an acceptable false alarm budget."
- **accuracy (= recall when FAR is 0)**: ASR-style two-stage decoding (generate hypothesis first, then match) cannot intrinsically control false alarms, making it difficult to set fixed recall/FAR operating points. For fair comparison, accuracy is additionally reported (Section III-C). This is a pragmatic handling in metric selection: providing a common anchor for methods that cannot tune thresholds.
- **Macro-average metrics**: macro-accuracy and macro-recall for 20 keywords are reported on LibriKWS-20. Rationale: In multi-keyword scenarios, micro-average is hijacked by word frequency and word difficulty structure. Macro-average treats each word equally, which is fairer.

It can also be seen that the benefit of joint training is not only at inference: introducing CTC during training causes each single branch head to exceed the corresponding single-branch training baseline (CTC head 99.05 vs NTC 98.97 @0.05; Transducer head 99.57 vs TDT 98.56 @0.02).

**Chinese Fixed Keywords (Table VII, MobvoiHotwords, FAR=0.5/h, Xiaowen/Wenwen/Average)**: Streaming Transformer 99.30 / 99.50 / 99.40; MDTC 99.50 / 99.57 / 99.54; LF-MMI 99.60 / 99.50 / 99.55; Res2Net-KWS 99.53 / 99.75 / 99.64; MFA-KWS 99.66 / 99.92 / 99.79. Conclusion in Section IV-E: Relative to the strongest baseline for each of the two words, MFA-KWS reduces miss rate by 27.66% and 68% respectively (recall 99.53%→99.66%, 99.75%→99.92%), verifying the generalization of the framework across languages and modeling units (syllables vs. phones).

**Arbitrary Keywords (Table VIII, LibriKWS-20, macro-accuracy@FAR=0, test-clean/test-other/Average)**: CTC Greedy 89.68 / 64.64 / 77.16; CTC Prefix Beam Search (beam=10) 72.47 / 43.52 / 57.60; CTC Streaming Decoding 93.86 / 70.99 / 82.43; Transducer Greedy 83.20 / 51.93 / 67.57; Transducer Beam Search (beam=10) 86.30 / 54.83 / 70.57; Transducer Streaming Decoding 93.18 / 69.71 / 81.45; Joint MFA 94.25 / 72.75 / 83.50, ranking first in both subsets and average. Two noteworthy phenomena: First, CTC's prefix beam search is significantly worse than greedy (average 57.60 vs 77.16), directly confirming that general ASR decoding is not suitable for KWS. Second, streaming-specific decoding is stronger than any general decoding, showing that the value of keyword-specific search space is independent of model structure.

**Noise Robustness (Table IX, FA=2, SNR=0/5/10/15/20 dB)**: On Snips, MFA recall at each SNR is 83.35 / 93.16 / 97.27 / 98.42 / 98.89 (average 94.22), compared to MFS average 93.06, CTC-PSD 90.42, TDT 86.50, CTC-FSD 86.44; on test-clean, MFA average is 94.37 (MFS 93.19); on the hardest test-other, MFA average is 75.15 (TDT 74.60, MFS 72.90, CTC-PSD 70.86, CTC-FSD 70.37). Section IV-G summarizes three points: First, Transducer decoding is more stable than CTC decoding on continuous speech datasets (especially test-other). Second, frame-skipping strategies consistently boost performance under noise conditions (MFA vs MFS, CTC-PSD vs CTC-FSD), possibly because frame-skipping filters out uncorrelated frames polluted by noise, allowing the model to focus on key speech frames. Third, multi-head decoding is significantly superior to single-branch under noise, and is stably effective across all SNR levels.

**Inference Efficiency (Table X, using MFS decoding speed as 1.0× baseline)**: MFA accelerates by 1.63× / 1.55× / 1.53× / 1.52× / 1.47× on Snips/test-clean/test-other/Xiaowen/Wenwen respectively (i.e., the 47%–63% acceleration mentioned in the abstract); single-branch comparisons: CTC-PSD 2.03× / 1.87× / 1.77× / 1.93× / 2.09×, TDT-4 1.64× / 1.59× / 1.60× / 1.57× / 1.62×, RNN-T 1.19× / 1.33× / 1.10× / 1.26× / 1.23×. MFA is faster than single-branch RNN-T, comparable to TDT-4, and slower than pure CTC-PSD—considering that MFA's detection accuracy is comprehensively higher than all single-branch systems, this speed-accuracy position is the basis for the paper's claim of "better performance and efficiency balance."

### Findings from Ablation Experiments

- **PSD Frame-skipping Threshold (Table II, Snips, FAR=0.02)**: Frame-skipping ratios 0% / 20% / 30% / 35% / 40% / 45% / 50% correspond to $\lambda_\varphi$ = 1.0 / 0.9999 / 0.9997 / 0.9993 / 0.9986 / 0.9971 / 0.9946, with recallrespectively, 98.89 / 98.54 / 98.46 / 98.10 / 97.51 / 94.35 / 88.41. The pattern is clear: as the frame loss rate rises from 0 to 35%, recall drops slowly by only 0.79 percentage points; after 35%, the decline turns from slow to rapid, dropping to 94.35 at 45% and collapsing to 88.41 at 50%. Based on this, 35% is selected as the efficiency-accuracy sweet spot, and $\lambda_\varphi$ is searched on the development sets of each dataset. This also reveals the practical value of CTC's peaky distribution: frames truly carrying speech content account for only a minority.
- **Fusion Strategies (Table IV, FA=2, three English test sets)**: When RNN-T does not open PSD (pure MFS), CDC-Zero and CDC-Last results are identical (99.60 / 99.26 / 91.22, average 96.69), because there is no frame-skipping, so there is no difference in PH filling—this itself is a clean verification of the PH semantics; once PSD is opened (RNN-T+PSD), CDC-Last average 96.75 leads CDC-Zero's 96.38; when TDT-4 does not open PSD, CDC-Last average 97.11 is already the best within the block; when TDT-4+PSD, CDC-Last reaches the overall best average 97.45 (Snips 99.60, test-clean 99.11, test-other 93.63), 1.12 percentage points higher than Equivalence-Dom (96.33) in the same block. Conclusion: Consistency-based methods are comprehensively superior to single-frame methods, and CDC-Last stably wins over CDC-Zero. CDC-Last is uniformly adopted for all subsequent multi-head experiments.
- **D_max Scan and MFS/MFA Comparison (Table V, FA=2)**: After replacing the Transducer branch from RNN-T to TDT-2/4/6/8/10, D_max=4 is comprehensively best (TDT-4+PSD average 97.45); D_max too small (TDT-2 average 96.80) means insufficient duration modeling, too large (TDT-8 average 96.20, TDT-10 average 96.02) means single-step frame-skipping is too aggressive, easily skipping key frames. The overall account in Section IV-D: MFA-KWS (TDT-4+PSD) reduces relative miss rate by 23.0% on average compared to MFS-KWS (RNN-T+FSD) (2.55% vs 3.31%), while (Table X) it is also faster—accuracy and speed improve in the same direction, which is the core evidence that "frame-asynchronization is not a trade-off of accuracy for speed, but a win-win."
- **Ablations Not Performed**: Sensitivity of $\alpha$ (CTC loss weight), $\ell_{win}$ (CDC window length), $S_{Bonus}$, and $T_{out}$ values are not reported in the paper ($\ell_{win}$ fixed at 20 is only a statement for reproducibility).

## Main Contributions

1. **Propose MFS-KWS**: Extend Transducer-based KWS to a multi-task system with CTC-Transducer joint training framework and multi-head frame-synchronous streaming decoding, achieving robust performance in challenging scenarios such as difficult negative samples and noise (Introduction Contribution List Item 1; Table III shows that joint training alone can reduce miss rate by up to 72.17% relative to TDT).
2. **Propose MFA-KWS**: A multi-head frame-asynchronous decoding framework introducing PSD to the CTC branch and TDT to the Transducer branch, for which various novel fusion strategies are designed and explored (Contribution Item 2); CDC-Last becomes the most stable choice.
3. **Comprehensive SOTA Across Scenarios**: Surpass ASR-style and end-to-end baselines comprehensively on English fixed keywords (Snips, Tables III/VI), Chinese fixed keywords (MobvoiHotwords, Table VII), and arbitrary keywords (LibriKWS-20, Table VIII); multi-SNR noise experiments (Table IX) show the dual-head system is significantly superior to single-branch, with clear robustness advantages.
4. **Efficiency Leap**: Accelerate by 47%–63% on all test sets relative to the frame-synchronous MFS framework (Table X: 1.47×–1.63×), with approximately 3.3M parameters (TDT duration head overhead <0.1%), suitable for on-device deployment.
5. **Open-source Decoding Code**: Release the full implementation of MFA, Transducer streaming decoding, CTC streaming decoding, and various fusion strategies (GitHub X-LANCE/KWStreamingSearch), directly responding to the scarcity of open-source decoding research in KWS.

## Limitations and Future Work

### Technical Limitations of the Method

- **State and Implementation Cost of Dual-branch Decoding**: Algorithm 2 requires maintaining decoding lattice states for both Transducer and CTC simultaneously, plus CDC sliding window similarity calculation. Implementation complexity and memory overhead are higher than any single-branch system; the paper only reports relative acceleration ratios, not memory usage or state maintenance costs.
- **Global Fixed Hyperparameters, Lack of Adaptivity**: $\alpha=0.3$, $\ell_{win}=20$, frame-skipping ratio 35%, D_max=4 are all global constants, not adaptive across datasets. Table II shows the safety margin for frame-skipping is narrow—recall is 98.10 at 35% and has collapsed to 88.41 at 50%—meaning fixed thresholds risk failure when deployed to data with more skewed distributions.
- **Response Latency of CDC Sliding Window**: Cosine similarity is estimated over a 20-frame window. Estimating with 10 ms frame shift and 3x downsampling (output frame step approx. 30 ms), the window length is approx. 0.6 seconds (this is a derivation from the notes, not reported in the paper), causing natural latency in consistency judgment for sudden short events; moreover, fusion weights are entirely determined by manual rules, with no learnable parameters.
- **CTC Conditional Independence is a Double-edged Sword**: It brings anti-error-accumulation and regularization benefits, but the CTC head itself lacks language context. In arbitrary keyword scenarios, the Transducer branch is still needed as a fallback (Table VIII: CTC streaming average 82.43 vs Transducer streaming 81.45, they complement each other rather than one being redundant); looking at the CTC head alone, after joint training, the 0.05 level is 99.05, still lower than single-branch CDC-KWS's 99.80 (Table III), indicating that joint training does not monotonically benefit all heads.

### Shortcomings in Experimental Design

- **Incomplete Efficiency Metrics**: Only relative acceleration multiples vs. MFS are reported (Table X). Absolute RTF, algorithm latency, peak memory are not reported, nor are any real-world edge hardware (ARM/DSP/NPU) measurements; FLOPs corresponding to 3.3M parameters and the computational split of the two encoders (overhead of the 2 layers of CTC auxiliary encoder) are not reported in the paper.
- **Missing Ablations for Key Hyperparameters**: Sensitivity analysis for $\alpha$, $\ell_{win}$, $S_{Bonus}$, $T_{out}$ is missing, so readers cannot judge the robustness of conclusions to these choices.
- **Uneven Coverage of Noise Experiments**: MobvoiHotwords is excluded from noise mixing experiments (original data contains noise, SNR cannot be controlled), resulting in no controlled noise robustness data on the Chinese side; fusion strategy ablation (Table IV) is also only done on the three English test sets, not covered on Chinese.
- **Limited Confidence in False Alarm Statistics**: The false alarm evaluation audio for each subset of LibriKWS-20 is only 3 hours (Section III-A). The sample size of false alarm counts at low FAR levels is small, with high risk of metric variance; there are only 20 keywords (Table I).
- **Gaps in Comparison Scope**: No direct comparison with WFST-style methods in the main tables (Introduction only qualitatively mentions their complexity); beam search baseline fixes beam=10, width is not scanned; concurrent keyword-biased streaming ASR (e.g., two-pass methods like U2-KWS) are only cited in the Introduction, not competing in experiments.
- **Training Costs Not Disclosed**: The increase in training time and memory overhead brought by dual-task loss and dual-decoding heads relative to single-branch is not reported in the paper.

### Possible Directions for Future Improvement

- **Learnable Fusion**: Replace CDC's cosine weights with a small trainable gate (e.g., a lightweight network taking the sliding window features of both branches as input), allowing "how to fuse, when to fuse" to be learned end-to-end by data, potentially surpassing the ceiling of manual rules.
- **Adaptive Frame-skipping**: Dynamically adjust $\lambda_\varphi$ and D_max upper limit based on online SNR estimation or branch confidence, replacing the global fixed 35%, to exchange deployment safety under narrow margins.
- **Dual-head Distillation to Single-head**: Distill the multi-head teacher from joint training into a single-head student, retaining the benefits of joint training while cutting the dual-decoding overhead during inference, further reducing edge-side costs.
- **Real-device Benchmark**: Measure latency and power consumption on ARM/DSP to verify the benefits of 3.3M parameters plus 1.5× acceleration in a real wake-up link, and provide memory usage reports.
- **Task Expansion**: Expand to larger keyword sets, unified multi-language models, multi-speaker overlap scenarios (the team has text-aware speech separation work that can be combined), and combine with keyword-biased/context-biased techniques to support dynamic registration of user-defined words at runtime.
- **Evaluation Completion**: Add Chinese noise mixing, longer false alarm corpora, ablation for $\alpha$ and window length, and in-field comparison with WFST and two-pass streaming ASR baselines, solidifying the "comprehensive SOTA" claim to a wider comparison scope.
