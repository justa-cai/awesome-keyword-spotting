# End-to-End Efficiency in Keyword Spotting: A System-Level Approach for Embedded Microcontrollers

- **Authors/Affiliations**: Pietro Bartoli, Tommaso Bondini, Christian Veronesi, Andrea Giudici, Franco Zappa (Department of Electronics, Information and Bioengineering, Politecnico di Milano); Niccolò Antonello (Smart Eyewear Laboratory, EssilorLuxottica, Milan)
- **Date**: September 8, 2025 (arXiv:2509.07051v1, cs.SD, IEEE Conference Paper Format)
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07051
- **Keywords**: keyword spotting, edge AI, MFCC, MCU benchmarking, energy-delay product, lightweight neural network, hardware-software co-design

## Problem Statement

### Problem Background and Domain Pain Points

Keyword Spotting (KWS) refers to the technology of detecting predefined words within a continuous audio stream, serving as a foundational component for hands-free voice interaction and modern Internet of Things (IoT) applications. Widely deployed consumer platforms such as Amazon Alexa, Google Assistant, and Apple Siri rely on KWS to recognize specific wake words ("Alexa", "Hey Google", "Hey Siri"), thereby providing intuitive hands-free voice interfaces. These systems share a common architectural decision: the KWS module must run locally to reduce response latency and protect user privacy—only activating cloud-based processing for advanced tasks like speech recognition after a wake word is detected. This two-stage architecture, characterized by "local small models for always-on listening and cloud large models for on-demand wake-up," places all the power consumption and memory pressure of always-on operations on the edge device.

The core contradiction lies in the fact that while these KWS algorithms perform well on consumer devices with strong processing capabilities, such as smart speakers and smartphones, they face severe challenges when deployed on battery-powered microcontrollers (MCUs). MCUs operate under strict memory and computational constraints, requiring KWS solutions to balance response speed and accuracy while minimizing energy consumption—the critical bottleneck for battery-powered devices. The paper explicitly highlights this trade-off triangle of "latency, accuracy, and energy efficiency," noting that this trade-off has become more complex in recent years: although new deep learning methods continuously improve accuracy, they often exceed the computational and memory budgets of embedded hardware.

Beyond the model itself, the audio front-end processing chain introduces an often-overlooked layer of additional overhead, most notably the Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC)—one of the most widely adopted feature extraction methods in automatic speech processing. MFCCs are acoustic features that simulate human auditory perception, reflecting the non-linear frequency sensitivity of the human ear. The computation process involves converting audio into a Mel-scale spectrogram and then performing dimensionality reduction via Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT). This front-end chain consists of a series of floating-point-intensive operations, including FFT, Mel-filter bank weighting, logarithmic compression, and DCT. On MCUs with clock speeds ranging from tens to hundreds of MHz, the energy and latency overhead of this chain cannot be ignored as a trivial matter. An important contextual detail is that the author team includes researchers from the Smart Eyewear Laboratory of EssilorLuxottica (the world's largest eyewear group). Based on the institutional composition, it is reasonable to infer that the true target scenario for this work is wearable devices like smart glasses, which have extremely small battery capacities and require always-on voice wake-up. Although the paper does not explicitly describe specific product scenarios, the conclusion repeatedly emphasizes that the TKWS series is "very suitable for always-on applications," which corroborates this background.

### Specific Shortcomings of Existing Methods

The paper’s critique of existing work focuses on a systemic lack of evaluation scope, which can be summarized in four points:

- **Evaluation scope covers only model inference, ignoring front-end processing.** Most existing work focuses on optimizing the KWS model architecture itself (citing [12]–[14], i.e., DS-CNN from Hello Edge, hardware-aware perceptual training, sinc-convolution raw audio schemes, etc.), but lacks comprehensive studies that simultaneously evaluate the energy and memory costs of audio front-end processing and neural inference on real embedded platforms. In other words, the academic community is accustomed to optimizing only one segment of the pipeline, without accounting for the entire system pipeline.
- **High-accuracy models are disconnected from hardware budgets.** Recent deep architectures (citing [8], such as convolutional recurrent networks) have made continuous progress in accuracy, but "often exceed the computational and memory budgets of embedded hardware." This reflects a structural misalignment between academic evaluation (focusing only on accuracy leaderboards) and engineering deployment (focusing on flash usage, SRAM, and millijoule-level energy consumption).
- **Lack of cross-platform horizontal evidence.** The significant differences in computational levels between different MCUs (this paper involves three tiers: Cortex-M33/M7/M55) mean that the optimality of the same model on different platforms may completely flip, yet existing research rarely conducts horizontal comparisons across multiple platforms under the same methodology.
- **The impact of feature extraction parameters on system resources has never been systematically quantified.** Front-end hyperparameters such as the number of MFCC time windows and the number of Mel-filter banks affect both accuracy and the computational load of front-end processing, but no previous work has scanned the design space of "feature resolution × model architecture × hardware platform" as a whole.

### Key Challenges Addressed by This Paper

The core question the paper aims to answer is: On battery-powered MCUs, how to evaluate and design a "truly efficient" KWS deployment scheme. The definition of "truly efficient" is deliberately expanded by the paper from "high model accuracy" to "system-level end-to-end efficiency," specifically broken down into four sub-challenges: First, how to incorporate the complete pipeline from raw audio to MFCC features to neural inference into a unified energy and latency measurement framework, rather than measuring only the inference segment; Second, how to design a lightweight architecture with parameters pushed to the limit without a collapse in accuracy (this is the task of TKWS); Third, how to balance the trade-off between accuracy gains and system resource costs for feature extraction parameters (time resolution, frequency resolution); Fourth, what is the interaction effect between model architecture and hardware capabilities (especially NPU adaptability)—the paper’s answer is that this interaction directly颠覆s the conclusion of "which model is more efficient." The methodological stance of the entire paper can be summarized in one sentence: Model accuracy alone does not determine real-world effectiveness.

## Methodology

### Overall Architecture Design and Design Motivation

The entire processing pipeline (Fig. 1) is divided into four stages: the raw audio waveform is first transformed into time-frequency features (MFCC), then fed into a neural network, and finally, a post-processing stage extracts the most likely prediction. This seemingly trivial pipeline diagram is actually the program for the entire paper—it explicitly includes MFCC front-end processing and post-processing within the measurement scope, and the measurement framework covers both front-end processing and inference stages (post-processing is excluded from EDP calculation due to negligible overhead).

The experiments span three STMicroelectronics STM32 platforms, representing three different computational tiers: N6 (Cortex-M55 core, running at a maximum frequency of 800 MHz), H7 (Cortex-M7, 480 MHz), and U5 (Cortex-M33, 160 MHz). The motivation for this selection is fully unfolded in the results section: N6 possesses advanced DSP capabilities for fast MFCC extraction and integrates a Neural-ART-like neural acceleration unit (referred to as integrated NPU in the paper) to support low-power, high-speed inference; H7 follows a low-latency route; and U5 follows an ultra-low-power route. All CPUs are configured to run at their maximum frequencies, all models are uniformly quantized to 8-bit integers, and MFCC submission is handled by the DSPs built into each platform—these two uniform processing steps ensure cross-model and cross-platform comparability: if quantization methods and accelerators are inconsistent, the comparison loses its meaning.

A total of four families and six instances of models were evaluated. The first three are reference models from the literature, and the fourth is the TKWS proposed by the paper:

- **DS-CNN**: Implemented according to the Hello Edge framework, using depthwise separable convolution to factorize standard convolution into two steps: depthwise and pointwise, achieving a good balance between accuracy and resource efficiency. It has been the de facto standard baseline for MCU-based KWS since 2018.
- **LicoNet-S**: Takes the small configuration of LiCoNet. The original design was aimed at streaming inference (causal convolution); this paper transforms it into one-shot inference—by modifying the padding strategy, it processes complete samples without maintaining internal state. Why the change: One-shot inference allows for fair comparison of latency and energy with other models under the same measurement scope.
- **TENet6 and TENet6-N (Narrow version)**: A multi-branch time convolution architecture that efficiently aggregates multi-scale time features using kernel fusion during inference while controlling runtime complexity.
- **TKWS (Typman-KWS)**: A self-developed architecture of the paper, with design motivation derived from the inverted bottleneck design of MobileNetV2. It consists of stacked residual blocks, each combining pointwise up-projection, dual 1D depthwise convolutions, and a projection layer, acting directly on the MFCC time series. Two configurations are provided: 2 residual blocks (TKWS-2) and 3 residual blocks (TKWS-3).

All models share the same classification head: Global Average Pooling (GAP) followed by a fully connected layer with softmax output for class probabilities. Why use GAP instead of flattening into a large fully connected layer: Global average pooling directly averages the time dimension of each channel, introducing almost no additional parameters, which is a critical constraint for MCUs with only hundreds of KB of flash memory. It also brings stability to time translation, allowing consistent criteria to be aggregated regardless of where the keyword appears in the 1-second window.

The "why" behind the TKWS architecture choice is worth expanding. The original design motivation of the inverted bottleneck block (pointwise up-projection → depthwise convolution → pointwise projection) is to expand the capacity for inter-channel information mixing with low computational cost: first cheaply increase the number of channels, allowing the depthwise convolution to perform spatial/temporal modeling in a high-dimensional space, and then project back to low dimensions to compress the memory footprint of activations. TKWS migrates this idea to 1D MFCC time series and uses "dual 1D depthwise convolutions"—two depthwise branches can further reduce the parameters and computational load per layer while maintaining the temporal receptive field. Compared to 2D convolution schemes (such as DS-CNN treating MFCC as a 2D time-frequency graph for convolution), the benefits of pure 1D design are confirmed by the paper's EDP data on platforms without NPU: 1D convolution models systematically outperform 2D models in energy efficiency (see Experimental Results section).

### Mathematical Principles of Core Algorithms

This is a system-level benchmarking paper rather than a pure algorithm paper, with little emphasis on mathematics, but the mathematical definition of its measurement framework is worth梳理ing completely.

**MFCC Feature Extraction Chain**: The description given in the paper is conceptual—first converting audio into a Mel-scale spectrogram, then performing dimensionality reduction via DCT. Following signal processing conventions, its mathematical process can be expanded as: performing Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) on frame-wise windowed audio to obtain the magnitude spectrum, weighting and summing the power spectrum using a set of triangular Mel filters (Mel scale simulates the human ear's non-linear perception of frequency, with high resolution at low frequencies and low resolution at high frequencies), taking the logarithm to compress the dynamic range, and finally using DCT to decorrelate the filter bank energies and compress them into fewer cepstral coefficients. DCT is used for dimensionality reduction because the adjacent dimensions of the log-Mel filter bank energy vector are highly correlated, and the basis vectors of DCT happen to concentrate the energy into the first few coefficients. The paper does not report specific frame lengths, frame shifts, or the number of DCT coefficients.

**Energy-Delay Product (EDP)**: Defined as the product of latency and energy consumption, i.e., EDP = E × L (unit mJ·ms, see Fig. 2 annotation). Why choose the product rather than reporting latency or energy separately: Optimizing latency alone tends to select high-frequency, high-power platforms (such as H7), while optimizing energy alone tends to select low-frequency platforms but prolongs response time (such as U5), both of which may violate user experience. The product form penalizes extremes at either end and is a classic comprehensive metric for system-level efficiency (supported by citations [19][20] in the paper). The EDP in this paper covers the merged measurement values of the front-end processing and inference stages, excluding negligible post-processing.

**Operational Structure of Inverted Bottleneck Residual Block**: Let the input channel number be C, the expansion factor be t, and the data flow within the block be C-dimensional input → pointwise convolution (1×1, parameter count C×tC) up to tC dimensions → dual 1D depthwise convolution (parameter count of each branch is only tC×k, where k is the kernel length, convolving each channel independently) → pointwise projection (tC×C') down to C' dimensions. Compared to standard convolution (C×C'×k), the reduction in parameters and multiply-accumulate operations comes from two factorizations: depthwise decouples the channel dimension from the spatial dimension, and pointwise uses 1×1 kernels to handle cross-channel mixing. Residual connections provide an identity shortcut when input and output channels match, alleviating the expressiveness bottleneck of lightweight networks. The paper does not report the specific expansion factors, kernel lengths, or channel numbers for TKWS.

**Depthwise Separable Convolution (DS-CNN Baseline)**: Standard convolution C_in×C_out×k is decomposed into depthwise (independent convolution per channel, C_in×k) plus pointwise (1×1 cross-channel mixing, C_in×C_out), with a reduction ratio in multiply-accumulate operations of approximately 1/C_out + 1/k. This is the convolution paradigm established for MCUs by the Hello Edge framework.

**Multi-branch and Kernel Fusion of TENet**: Multiple time convolution branches with different kernel lengths parallelly extract multi-scale time patterns, fusing the convolution kernels of each branch into a single execution during inference to reduce runtime complexity. The paper does not provide the mathematical details of the fusion (see their original paper [17]).

**Linearized Convolution of LiCoNet**: LiCoNet uses a linearization design that is approximately identity to allow efficient execution of convolution on hardware; the original form is streaming inference of causal convolution; this paper changes it to one-shot inference by modifying the padding strategy. The mathematical expansion of linearization is not reported in the paper (see [16]).

### Key Technical Innovation 1: Typman-KWS (TKWS) Architecture

The innovation positioning of TKWS is not to invent new operators, but to completely transplant the mature efficiency design language of mobile vision (MobileNetV2 inverted bottleneck) to 1D MFCC time series, and make three specific trade-offs for MCU constraints: First, 1D convolution throughout, avoiding the energy efficiency loss caused by poor memory access patterns of 2D convolution on Cortex-M series without NPU (this choice is verified by EDP data on H7/U5); Second, using "dual 1D depthwise" instead of a single large kernel to reduce parameters while maintaining the temporal receptive field; Third, providing two tiers of 2-block and 3-block configurations to cover deployment needs for "extreme memory saving" (TKWS-2, 4.6k–5.1k parameters) and "near-SOTA accuracy" (TKWS-3, 14.4k–14.9k parameters). In terms of results, TKWS-3 achieves 92.4% weighted F1 with 14.4k parameters (Table I), reducing parameters by 17% compared to LicoNet-S and lowering F1 by only 1.2 percentage points, while reducing memory usage by more than three times compared to DS-CNN and TENet6 with持平 or better accuracy.

### Key Technical Innovation 2: End-to-End System-Level Benchmarking Methodology

The second contribution of the paper is the evaluation methodology itself: expanding the measurement boundary from "model inference" to the complete pipeline of "MFCC front-end processing + neural inference," with latency and energy measurements following the TinyML benchmarking methodology previously proposed by the author team (Reference [21], Bartoli et al., arXiv:2505.15622). The significance of this methodology lies in elevating "feature extraction parameters" from fixed pre-processing defaults to explicit design variables: the paper scanned a 2×2 MFCC configuration grid—32 or 63 time windows, and 15 or 30 Mel filter banks. The reasons for choosing these four values are clearly stated in the paper: 32 and 63 frames align with SFFT sizes of 1024 and 512, respectively, avoiding zero-padding and ensuring computational efficiency; 15 and 30 Mel filters represent two tiers of lightweight and finer frequency resolution, respectively. Thus, the experiment constitutes a full-factorial design space of "feature configuration (4) × model (6) × platform (3)," with five types of measurements for each cell: parameter count, F1, latency, energy, and EDP.

### Key Technical Innovation 3: Empirical Revelation of Architecture-Hardware Interaction Effects

The most engineering-valuable finding of the paper is not TKWS itself, but the use of system-level data to prove that model rankings flip with hardware: on H7 and U5 without NPU, 1D convolution models systematically outperform 2D models in energy efficiency, and DS-CNN (2D) ranks last in EDP on both platforms due to higher computational complexity and poor hardware compatibility; but the trend reverses on N6 with NPU—the TENet series suffers because its kernel sizes exceed the optimal execution range of the NPU, causing related operators to be offloaded to the CPU, and the叠加 of data transfer overhead and decreased operator execution efficiency actually increases EDP. This directly leads to the paper's co-design proposition: neural architecture and hardware platforms must be co-designed, because the optimal balance of accuracy and efficiency is ultimately determined by the combined characteristics of the two, not by either one alone.

### Technical Differences from Existing Methods

Compared to Hello Edge (DS-CNN paper): Hello Edge pioneered KWS deployment research on MCUs, but its evaluation was centered on model inference; this paper incorporates front-end processing into the same measurement framework and spans three tiers of hardware. Compared to architecture papers like LiCoNet and TENet: They each propose a single architecture and prove efficiency on GPUs or specific hardware; this paper conducts controlled horizontal comparisons under a unified methodology, revealing their adaptability issues on the N6 NPU (TENet kernel size problem)—a blind spot that original architecture papers would not expose themselves. Compared to the author team's own benchmarking methodology paper [21]: [21] provides a general measurement method, while this paper is the first systematic application and design space scanning of this method on the full KWS pipeline. Compared to general model compression routes (quantization, pruning, distillation): This paper does not compete with compression technologies but assumes 8-bit integer quantization as a common base, focusing innovation on architecture form and system-level trade-offs.

## Experimental Results

### Dataset Used and Its Scale

All models were trained and evaluated on the Google Speech Commands Dataset (GSCD) v0.02, with the classification task using a subset of 10 keyword classes: yes, no, up, down, left, right, on, off, stop, go. To improve robustness to real-world conditions, training samples were augmented with noise from GSCD (including synthetic and real background sounds), with mixed signal-to-noise ratios sampled from a normal distribution N(10 dB, 5 dB)—i.e., random SNR noise added with an average of 10 dB and a standard deviation of 5 dB, which is closer to the diversity of real noise conditions than augmentation with a fixed single SNR. It should be noted that the paper does not report the specific sample quantity division of the training/validation/test sets, number of training epochs, optimizer, learning rate, and other training details; the choice of 10 classes instead of the dataset's full 35 words also means that its F1 values cannot be directly horizontally compared with other papers using 12-class (including unknown and silence) or 35-class settings.

### Definition and Rationale for Evaluation Metrics

The paper adopts three metrics, each with a clear definition and rationale (Section C of Experimental Setup):

- **Number of Parameters**: Quantifies static memory usage on the MCU, as model weights must reside permanently in flash storage. In the MCU context, this is a hard constraint metric—if flash is exceeded, the model simply cannot fit, unlike server scenarios where expansion is infinite.
- **Weighted F1-score**: Used as the primary classification performance metric, with the rationale that the test set may have class imbalance. Compared to overall accuracy, weighted F1 comprehensively combines precision and recall weighted by the number of samples per class, preventing dominance by large classes, which is fairer for wake word scenarios where sample sizes for different keywords are uneven.
- **Energy-Delay Product (EDP)**: The product of latency and energy consumption, used to balance latency and energy and evaluate system-level efficiency (supported by citations [19][20] in the paper). The measurement program follows the methodology of Reference [21], with latency and energy measured separately for the front-end processing and inference stages, and EDP calculated by merging the two stages, excluding negligible post-processing.

### Detailed Comparison with Baseline Methods and SOTA

**Model Accuracy and Parameter Count (Table I)**: Table I provides the parameter counts and test-set weighted F1 for six models under four MFCC configurations (15×32, 30×32, 15×63, 30×63, filter banks × time steps). Key readings are as follows—

- TKWS-3 achieves 92.4% F1 under the 15×63 configuration with only 14.4k parameters, representing the paper's optimal point for accuracy-memory trade-off. Compared to LicoNet-S (93.6% F1, 17.4k parameters), it has 17% fewer parameters and F1 is lower by 1.2 percentage points; compared to DS-CNN (91.2% F1, 46.5k parameters) and TENet6 (92.9% F1, 54.2k parameters), it has持平 or better accuracy while reducing memory usage by more than three times.
- The most compact TKWS-2 has only 4.6k–5.1k parameters, achieving 88.8% F1 under 15×63, which is about 2.4 percentage points lower than the same configuration of DS-CNN (91.2%) with three times the parameters, but its parameter count is only one-tenth of DS-CNN's—this characterizes the cost of the "extreme memory saving" tier.
- The parameter count of the same model varies slightly under different MFCC configurations (e.g., TENet6 from 54.2k to 55.7k), indicating that the model input dimension and network width are co-designed; but time resolution is the dominant variable for accuracy.
- The 63 time window configuration consistently outperforms the 32-window configuration across all models (e.g., DS-CNN from 86.0% to 91.2%, LicoNet-S from 90.1% to 93.6%, TKWS-2 from 86.5% to 88.8%, all seen in Table I), proving that higher time resolution significantly improves accuracy, a factor that system designers under resource constraints must take into account.
- From the data in Table I, another pattern not expanded in the paper text can be observed: under the 63-window configuration, increasing the Mel filter banks from 15 to 30 causes the F1 of most models to drop (LicoNet-S 93.6%→92.5%, TKWS-3 92.4%→91.2%, TKWS-2 88.8%→87.3%, DS-CNN 91.2%→91.0%), with TENet6 being the exception (92.9%→93.2%). This suggests that when time resolution is sufficiently high, the capacity gains from finer frequency resolution may be offset by overfitting or feature redundancy—this is an inference based on Table I, for which the paper provides no explanation.

**System-Level EDP Comparison (Fig. 2)**: Fig. 2 presents the total EDP of the complete KWS pipeline under different MFCC sizes, model architectures, and MCU platform combinations in the form of a heatmap (unit mJ·ms), with each cell reporting both latency L (ms) and energy E (mJ), with color mapping for EDP values (lower is better). The conclusions in the text are as follows—

- **N6 is comprehensively optimal**: Leveraging advanced DSP capabilities for fast MFCC extraction and integrating NPU support for low-power, high-speed inference, N6 has the lowest EDP, and even with high time resolution inputs like 63 time steps, it does not incur significant energy costs—in other words, N6 makes "trading feature resolution for accuracy" almost free, which is not the case on H7/U5.
- **H7 and U5 have similar EDPs but opposite mechanisms**: Both have EDPs inferior to N6 and are close to each other; H7 achieves lower latency through higher clock speeds but consumes more energy, while U5 prioritizes energy efficiency at the expense of speed. This leads to a platform selection guide: H7 is suitable for applications sensitive to response speed (such as real-time command recognition), while U5 is suitable for ultra-low-power scenarios insensitive to latency. The product metric EDP exactly reveals the equivalence of these two platforms at the system level—"different paths leading to the same result"—a conclusion that cannot be obtained by looking at latency or energy alone.
- **Interaction between architecture and hardware**: On MCUs without NPU, 1D convolution models consistently outperform 2D models in energy efficiency, and DS-CNN ranks highest in EDP on H7 and U5 due to higher computational complexity and poor hardware compatibility; on N6, the trend reverses, and the TENet series suffers because its kernel sizes exceed the optimal execution range of the NPU, leading to offloading to the CPU, where data transfer overhead叠加 with decreased execution efficiency results in higher EDP.
- It should be noted that the specific absolute values of latency/energy/EDP for each cell in Fig. 2 are presented only in the form of a heatmap within the figure; the paper text does not cite any specific absolute values, and this note does not fabricate them; interested readers need to consult the original figure.

### Findings from Ablation Experiments

The paper does not have traditional ablation tables for each module (e.g., gradually removing a layer of TKWS to measure accuracy changes); its "ablation" role is undertaken by controlled scanning across three dimensions, which can be summarized into four findings:

- **Time resolution scanning**: Fixing other variables, the comparison between 32 windows and 63 windows across all six models (Table I) consistently shows that 63 windows are better, with the maximum gain appearing in DS-CNN (86.0%→91.2%, +5.2 percentage points under 15 filter banks) and TKWS-2 (86.5%→88.8%, +2.3 percentage points). Conclusion: Time context length is the primary feature dimension for GSCD ten-word classification.
- **Frequency resolution scanning**: The comparison between 15 and 30 Mel filter banks (Table I) shows weak gains and is often negative under high time resolution (see numbers in the previous section), indicating that 15 filter banks are sufficient to cover the discriminative information for these ten short words, while 30 may introduce redundancy.
- **Model depth/width scanning (Internal ablation of TKWS)**: The comparison between TKWS-2 and TKWS-3 constitutes the scale ablation of TKWS—from 2 blocks to 3 blocks, parameters increase from 4.6k to 14.4k (15×63 configuration), and F1 increases from 88.8% to 92.4%, meaning that an accuracy gain of about +3.6 percentage points requires three times the parameters and corresponding inference overhead. The two tiers correspond to the deployment sweet spots of "extreme memory saving" and "cost-performance," respectively.
- **Platform scanning**: The EDP comparison of the same model on three MCUs (Fig. 2) shows an inseparable interaction effect between platform and architecture (the relative superiority of 1D/2D convolution flips with the presence of NPU, and TENet kernel size mismatches with the NPU adaptation domain). This is the most important ablation finding of the entire paper: any assertion that "Model A is better than Model B" must come with a hardware premise.

It must also be pointed out that key engineering metrics such as quantization method (post-training quantization vs. quantization-aware training), accuracy loss due to quantization, peak RAM usage during inference, activation memory, and model MACs per second are not reported.

## Main Contributions

- **Expands the efficiency evaluation scope of KWS from "model inference" to the end-to-end pipeline of "MFCC front-end processing + inference,"** and for the first time completes a system-level benchmark scan of "feature configuration × model × platform" on three STM32 MCUs (N6/M55, H7/M7, U5/M33) representing different computational tiers using a unified methodology (their previous work [21]).
- **Proposes the Typman-KWS (TKWS) lightweight architecture**: Based on the 1D residual design of MobileNetV2 inverted bottleneck, TKWS-3 achieves 92.4% weighted F1 with 14.4k parameters (Table I), reducing parameters by 17% compared to LicoNet-S, and reducing memory usage by more than three times compared to DS-CNN and TENet6 with持平 or better accuracy; TKWS-2 provides an ultra-compact tier with 88.8% F1 using 4.6k parameters.
- **Empirically quantifies the system-level cost of feature extraction parameters**: Proves that 63 time windows have consistent accuracy gains relative to 32 windows (Table I), and that adopting high time resolution features on N6 incurs almost no energy cost (Fig. 2), while 15 Mel filter banks are sufficient for the ten-word task.
- **Reveals architecture-hardware interaction effects**: On platforms without NPU, 1D convolution models systematically outperform 2D models in energy efficiency (DS-CNN ranks last), while on N6 with NPU, TENet suffers because its kernel sizes exceed the optimal execution range of the NPU, proving that the optimal solution is determined by the combined characteristics of architecture and platform, thereby proposing a deployment proposition for hardware-software co-design.
- **Provides a practical guide for platform selection**: N6 is comprehensively optimal and suitable for scenarios requiring high-resolution features with low latency and low energy consumption; H7 is suitable for latency-sensitive applications; U5 is suitable for ultra-low-power scenarios insensitive to latency. The core assertion that "model accuracy alone does not determine real-world effectiveness" has universal engineering warning value for edge AI deployment.

## Limitations and Future Work

### Technical Limitations of the Method

- **TKWS accuracy is not lossless**: TKWS-3's 92.4% is still 1.2 percentage points away from the optimal LicoNet-S (93.6%) under the same configuration (Table I), and TKWS-2's 88.8% is about 3–5 percentage points away from large models; the paper's claim of "minimal accuracy loss" is actually quite勉强 for the TKWS-2 tier.
- **Architecture is sensitive to feature resolution**: Table I shows that for the TKWS series, increasing the filter banks from 15 to 30 under 63 windows causes a drop in points (TKWS-3 from 92.4% to 91.2%), indicating that the capacity or regularization of the inverted bottleneck design has not been tuned for higher-dimensional inputs, and the model and feature configuration are not independently combinable.
- **NPU benefits are not automatically obtained**: The phenomenon of TENet being offloaded to the CPU on N6 due to kernel size mismatch shows that architectures not screened by NPU operator domain constraints may completely fail to enjoy accelerator dividends, and the paper does not provide the specific range of optimal NPU kernel sizes, nor has it verified whether TKWS's own kernel sizes fall within the optimal NPU domain.
- **One-shot inference is disconnected from real streaming scenarios**: All measurements are based on one-shot inference (LiCoNet even abandoned its native streaming design for this), while real wake word detection is a sliding-window streaming task on continuous audio streams; the pipeline overlap between front-end processing and inference, and the overhead of state maintenance, are not reflected in the measurement framework of this paper.
- **Missing key engineering metrics**: Peak RAM (activation memory), MACs per model, quantization method, and quantization accuracy loss are not reported, while RAM is exactly a hard constraint on MCUs alongside flash, making the equivalence relationship "parameter count = memory usage" cover only half of the static picture.

### Shortcomings in Experimental Design

- **Limited coverage of data and augmentation**: Only the ten-word subset of GSCD v0.02 is used, and the augmentation noise is also taken from the synthetic and real background sounds built into GSCD (SNR sampled from N(10 dB, 5 dB)), without involving key acoustic conditions for wearable scenarios such as real far-field, reverberation, and device self-noise (such as bone conduction/wind noise from glasses frames); the test class setting (10 classes, no unknown/silence rejection classes) avoids the evaluation of false wake-up rates, which is exactly the first indicator for wake word products.
- **Lack of statistical robustness**: The paper does not report variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests for multiple runs, making it impossible to judge whether the F1 difference of less than 1 percentage point in ranking (e.g., TKWS-3's 92.4% vs. TENet6-N's 91.8%) is significant.
- **Key values are presented only in figure form**: The absolute values of latency, energy, and EDP for each platform/model/feature combination in the heatmap of Fig. 2 are not cited in the text, nor is there a supporting numerical table, so readers cannot directly reuse these benchmark data and can only visually estimate from the figure, which weakens the core value of a benchmark paper.
- **Opaque training details**: Optimizer, learning rate, number of training epochs, early stopping strategy, and whether quantization is aware training are all not reported, so architecture comparisons may be interfered with by differences in training recipes.
- **Single platform configuration**: The three MCUs are fixed at their maximum clock speeds (800/480/160 MHz) for operation, without scanning DVFS frequency points; the conclusion that "U5 saves power, H7 is fast" is derived under asymmetric frequency settings, and it is unknown whether the EDP ranking remains unchanged after frequency adjustment.

### Possible Directions for Future Improvement

- **Incorporate NPU operator constraints into architecture search**: Incorporate the optimal kernel sizes and supported operator sets of the target accelerator as hard constraints during neural architecture search or manual design, to avoid implicit failures like "offloading to CPU" in the case of TENet; sensitivity verification of kernel size and NPU adaptation domain should also be added for TKWS.
- **Streaming and full-duplex evaluation**: Extend the end-to-end measurement framework to sliding-window streaming inference, incorporate the benefits of state maintenance memory and pipeline overlap, and introduce product-level metrics such as false wake-ups per second and detection latency (from word start to trigger).
- **Further compression of feature extraction**: This paper only scanned two coarse-grained tiers of window count and filter bank count; finer design spaces include the number of retained DCT coefficients, low-bit features (8-bit Mel), and even raw waveform end-to-end schemes that bypass MFCC (such as sinc-convolution) and learnable front-ends, the comparison of which with this paper's framework is a natural extension.
- **Orthogonal superposition with compression technologies**: Superimpose pruning, distillation (e.g., distilling TKWS-3 using LicoNet-S), and lower-bit representations on top of 8-bit quantization to test the combined benefits of lightweight architectures and compression technologies.
- **Validation in real wearable scenarios**: Leveraging the author team's smart glasses background, re-validate on far-field noisy data collected from real devices, and conduct system-level comparisons with VAD/two-stage wake-up pipelines (ultra-low-power detector + high-accuracy KWS), which is a key step from benchmark research to product deployment.
