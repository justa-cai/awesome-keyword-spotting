## Problem Statement

### Problem Background and Domain Pain Points

Keyword Spotting (KWS) is the first-level component of always-on voice interfaces, performing wake-word and command detection on continuous audio streams. The paper opens by listing four practical deployment challenges: (i) handling keywords unseen during training (open-set scenario); (ii) significant inter-speaker variability; (iii) domain shift from training environments to live acoustic conditions; (iv) strict budgets for on-device memory, compute, and energy. These four points collectively point to a requirement: KWS systems must continue to self-improve on the target domain after deployment, without relying on large amounts of labeled data—i.e., personalization and adaptation.

Previous SOTA solutions (Baseline [3], Rusci et al., IEEE IoT Journal 2024) have already validated a "self-learning" pipeline on ultra-low-power audio sensors: constructing keyword prototypes from a small number of user recordings, assigning pseudo-labels to live speech based on embedding distance, and performing incremental gradient fine-tuning. However, it has a structural blind spot: **the adaptation process only adjusts weights; the network architecture is frozen at the moment of deployment**. The efficiency problem in personalized KWS is essentially a dual-cost problem—fine-tuning itself occurs on resource-constrained devices (training is also a cost item), while inference happens continuously 7×24 (the major cost). Compressing only inference but not fine-tuning, or vice versa, is incomplete.

Let us use a specific scenario to clarify the pain point: the same wake-word model deployed to a smart speaker in a quiet study, an industrial gateway in a noisy factory workshop, and a user with a low-pitched voice will clearly have different optimal architectural redundancies—in a quiet environment, pruning 70% of channels might not hurt accuracy, while in a noisy environment or for a specific voice, pruning 30% might start to degrade performance. Offline pruning can only set a compromise ratio based on an "average scenario," leaving waste or risk for each specific deployment. Another unavoidable constraint is pseudo-label noise: live data lacks ground-truth labels, and prototype distances generate both labels and label errors. Any online architectural decision must be made on this data where "labels themselves are unreliable," imposing additional requirements on the robustness of the decision criteria.

### Specific Shortcomings of Existing Methods

- **Weight adaptation and architecture optimization are disjointed.** Online learning methods like [3] only fine-tune weights; whereas architecture optimization techniques such as pruning and Neural Architecture Search (NAS) are entirely offline steps in traditional workflows. The two worlds do not communicate.
- **The implicit assumptions of offline architecture selection distort in the field.** The paper's core insight is that the distribution shifts that make on-device fine-tuning beneficial also affect "what architecture is a good architecture." As stated in the paper, how aggressively one needs to prune to achieve a certain accuracy depends on the target keyword, the speaker's voice, and the live acoustic conditions—information unknown during the offline phase. The pruning rate selected based on pre-training distributions is likely suboptimal for specific deployment contexts.
- **The intuitive path of "prune smaller offline then fine-tune" is suboptimal.** An apparently equivalent alternative is to prune a smaller network offline and fine-tune directly on-site; the paper's empirical results show that online decision-making on what to prune using in-domain (pseudo-labeled) data outperforms directly fine-tuning a network pruned offline.
- **The data dilemma of online architecture optimization has not been studied.** There are no ground-truth labels on-site, only pseudo-labels generated by prototype distances; the sample size is much smaller than the pre-training set, and labels are noisy. Any solution aiming to move pruning to the device must answer "what data, at what timing, and under what criteria to make pruning decisions"—previous literature provides no answer.

### Key Challenges Addressed by This Paper

The paper precisely formulates the problem into a non-trivial research question (paraphrased from the original): **Is it "prune before fine-tuning using a data-aware criterion," or "prune after initial fine-tuning using a data-agnostic criterion"?** The non-triviality lies in the trade-off between data quality and quantity: data-aware criteria align with the deployment distribution and make decisions based on loss surface curvature, but are computationally more expensive and can only access a small number of pseudo-labeled samples; data-agnostic criteria have almost zero cost, but one must first wait for weights to absorb in-domain information so that their statistics reflect the deployment context. The constraints are dual: under iso-task performance (accuracy at the false alarm rate of 0.5 per hour working point does not drop), simultaneously reduce the latency/energy of on-device fine-tuning and the latency/energy of inference.

## Methodology

### Overall Architecture Design and Design Motivation

The entire system is built upon the three-stage self-learning pipeline of baseline [3]:

- **B1 Offline Pre-training**: Train the ProtoNet embedding network $f_\theta:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^d$ using triplet loss on a large-scale multi-class, multi-speaker labeled dataset $\mathcal{D}_{pre}=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ ($y_i\in\{1,\dots,K\}$). The motivation for choosing metric learning over a fixed classification head is open-set personalization: new keywords need to be expressible as prototypes in the embedding space, rather than adding a new output dimension.
- **B2 Calibration and Pseudo-labeling**: After deployment, a user provides $M$ positive examples of unseen keywords $E=\{x_j^+\}_{j=1}^M$ not present in the training set, from which keyword prototypes are constructed; subsequently, continuous incoming audio segments are pseudo-labeled based on embedding distance, forming the fine-tuning set $\mathcal{D}_{ft}$.
- **B3 On-device Training**: Fine-tune $f_\theta$ on $\mathcal{D}_{ft}$ using the same triplet objective as B1 (triplets are sampled from pseudo-labeled positive/negative examples).

OnDA's approach is to insert a structured channel pruning operator $A'=\mathcal{P}^{(\cdot)}_\rho(A;B)$ (where $A$ is the architecture defined by the number of channels per layer, $\rho$ is the pruning ratio, and $B$ is an optional data batch, with the superscript representing the criterion) into this pipeline, and systematically study the **insertion timing**: P (offline, before deployment), O1 (online, before B3), O2 (online, after B3). This yields two complete pipelines: OnDA-1 = B1 (P) B2 O1 B3; OnDA-2 = B1 (P) B2 B3 O2 (B3 must be repeated after pruning). O1/O2 are orthogonal to P—both can be added to an unpruned baseline or stacked on a model already compressed offline.

The compression form chosen is **structured pruning of convolutional layer output channels**, due to engineering compatibility: the pruned model is simply a "dense sub-network with fewer channels," requiring no sparse operators or specialized hardware, and can achieve both training and inference acceleration and energy savings in existing training/inference frameworks [18]. This is doubly important in online scenarios—after pruning, fine-tuning must immediately follow on the device, and training dense small networks also benefits from general infrastructure. The paper explicitly states that OnDA is theoretically scalable to unstructured/semi-structured pruning (provided hardware support exists), but this is not experimented with in this paper.

### Mathematical Principles of Core Algorithms

**Triplet Loss** (shared by B1/B3):

$$\mathcal{L}_{tri}(\theta)=\max\left(\|z_{p1}-z_{p2}\|_2^2-\|z_{p1}-z_n\|_2^2+\alpha,\ 0\right)$$

where $z_{p1}=f_\theta(x_{p1})$ etc. are embeddings, the triplet $(x_{p1},x_{p2},x_n)$ satisfies $y_{p1}=y_{p2}\neq y_n$, and $\alpha>0$ is the margin. In plain terms: pull similar embeddings closer, push dissimilar embeddings apart, and stop caring once the gap exceeds $\alpha$.

**Prototypes and Pseudo-labels** (B2):

$$p=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}f_\theta(x_j^+),\qquad d(x)=\|f_\theta(x)-p\|_2$$

The distance is compared against dual thresholds $\gamma^+<\gamma^-$ for a three-way decision: $d(x)\leq\gamma^+$ is classified as positive, $d(x)\geq\gamma^-$ as negative, and those falling in $(\gamma^+,\gamma^-)$ are discarded and not used—this "uncertainty band" is the key gate controlling pseudo-label noise entering the fine-tuning set, with thresholds calibrated according to the method in [3].

The **pruning ratio** $\rho$ is defined as the ratio of total weights in the pruned model to the unpruned model; the scoring is **global**—all channels across the entire network are jointly ranked by the same importance metric, and pruning proceeds until $\rho$ is reached. Therefore, the number of channels pruned per layer can vary, and the pruning budget automatically flows to the least important channels, rather than being manually allocated per layer.

**Data-agnostic Criterion (Global L1, Eq. 3)**: Let $W_c$ be the convolutional kernel slice producing output channel $c$,

$$s_c^{L1}=\|W_c\|_1=\sum_i |(W_c)_i|$$

Small magnitude $\rightarrow$ unimportant $\rightarrow$ prune first.

**Data-aware Criterion (HAP, Eq. 4)**: Let $H_c$ be the block-diagonal Hessian approximation for the weight group of channel $c$, and $n_c$ be the total number of parameters in that group,

$$s_c^{HAP}=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(H_c)}{n_c}\,\|W_c\|_2^2$$

Second-order intuition: The local loss increase caused by zeroing out a group of weights is approximately $\Delta\mathcal{L}\approx\frac{1}{2}\Delta w^\top H\, \Delta w$. HAP approximates this quadratic form using "average eigenvalue ($\mathrm{Tr}(H_c)/n_c$) $\times$ squared weight magnitude." In plain terms: **HAP is magnitude pruning weighted by curvature**—channels with large magnitude located on a steep loss surface are the least likely to be pruned; channels with large magnitude but located in flat regions can actually be safely deleted, which is information invisible to pure L1. The significance of approximating the Hessian as block-diagonal grouped by channels is to split the impossible-to-store full second-order matrix into small blocks that can be processed channel-by-channel; the Hutchinson method estimates the trace using random vectors (standard form $\mathrm{Tr}(H)\approx\mathbb{E}_v[v^\top Hv]$), requiring only Hessian-vector products throughout without explicitly constructing $H$, with a single estimation computational cost comparable to one backward pass of gradients [6]—this is precisely the premise for HAP being portable to devices: it requires no massive data and no storage super-linear in the number of parameters.

### Key Technical Innovation 1: Pipeline Design of Pruning Timing × Data Awareness (OnDA-1 / OnDA-2)

This is the paper's main innovation: for the first time, it couples architecture adaptation (online structured channel pruning) with weight adaptation (on-device training) into the same on-device flow, and makes "when to prune" an explicit design variable. The internal logic is the **structural binding** of criterion and timing:

- **Data-agnostic criterion (L1) can only go via O2.** It only looks at weight statistics (channel norms), and the norms of pre-trained weights reflect the pre-training distribution; one must first complete a round of B3 to write in-domain information into the weights, so that the statistics represent the deployment context, allowing pruning to preserve parts "important for this live site." The cost is that accuracy drops after pruning, requiring B3 to be repeated.
- **Data-aware criterion (HAP) can go via O1.** It makes decisions based on loss surface curvature measured by (pseudo-labeled) data, independent of whether weights have been updated—pruning can be completed in one shot before fine-tuning begins, after which **the entire fine-tuning process runs on the small network**, simultaneously delivering training acceleration and inference acceleration. The savings here are compound: suppose fine-tuning requires $E$ epochs; O1 pays the HAP scoring cost only once at the beginning, and every subsequent step calculates gradients and activations on the shrunk network; O2 must first run the first round of fine-tuning completely on the original large network, and after pruning, pay for a second round of fine-tuning—the training cost of the large network is saved not at all.
- **Orthogonal combination with offline P**: O1/O2 can be stacked on an unpruned baseline or a pre-compressed model with offline $\rho=25\%/50\%$, forming a two-stage compression of "offline coarse pruning + online fine pruning." This orthogonality allows OnDA to seamlessly integrate into any existing model that has already undergone offline compression, without retraining.

### Key Technical Innovation 2: Criterion Selection for Low-Data In-Domain Pruning (Division of Labor between L1 and HAP)

The paper does not invent a new pruning criterion, but rather selects two representative endpoints for the online scenario and systematically compares them: L1 (zero data cost, pure magnitude) and HAP (second-order curvature, requires data but is data-efficient). The reason for choosing HAP for the data-aware end is pointed out by the paper as being in the **low-data regime**: Hessian-based importance estimation is inherently data-efficient (few samples can characterize local curvature), and it has hardly been explored on KWS previously. This selection makes the comparison on the two axes of "data-aware vs. data-agnostic" and "before vs. after fine-tuning" clean and straightforward—each pipeline binds to one criterion, making the outcome attributable.

### Key Technical Innovation 3: Judging Pipelines by Total Deployment Cost (Dual-Objective Evaluation of Fine-tuning and Inference)

The third innovation is the evaluation methodology: accounting for online pruning simultaneously as a **training accelerator** and an **inference accelerator**. In addition to the latency and energy of a single step of fine-tuning/inference, it provides the cumulative energy difference $\Delta E$ over $n$ inference steps and its break-even point. The reason for accumulating by inference steps rather than wall-clock time is practical: real always-on systems directly skip inference when input audio energy is below a threshold, so inference steps are closer to real workloads than the time axis. Power is measured in real-time using current sensors on the Jetson board (SoC current), not simulated estimates. This accounting method brings to light the key finding that "two pipelines produce models with similar accuracy/size, yet deployment costs differ significantly."

### Technical Differences from Existing Methods

- **To Baseline [3]**: Upgraded from "weight adaptation" to "weight + architecture collaborative adaptation," compressing both fine-tuning and inference cost items simultaneously.
- **To Classic Offline Pruning (L1 filter pruning [5], HAP [6])**: The criteria are reused as-is; the novelty lies in the decision data (in-domain pseudo-labels rather than pre-training set) and decision timing (on-device, within the adaptation flow). The innovation focus is on the pipeline and empirical results, not the pruning mathematics.
- **To Domain Learning [4]** (Cioflan et al., AICAS 2024): [4] adapts to acoustic domains (environmental noise), while OnDA adapts to users/keywords (open-set new words); the two routes are orthogonal.
- **To NAS [16][17]**: No search required, one-time scoring and pruning, executable instantly on-device, avoiding the unbearable search cost of NAS on devices.

## Experimental Results

### Datasets Used and Their Scale

The pre-training set $\mathcal{D}_{pre}$ is the large-scale multilingual spoken word corpus **MSWC** [19] (this paper does not restate its vocabulary/sample scale, following the protocol of [3]; the paper does not report specific numbers). Evaluation is performed on **HeySnips** [12] and **HeySnapdragon** [20]: each is split into an adaptation set (used to construct the pseudo-labeled set $\mathcal{D}_{ft}$) and a disjoint test set (evaluation only before and after adaptation); subjects are the same as in [3], with each pipeline executed independently for each subject and then averaged across subjects (the number of subjects and set sizes are not reported in this paper and require checking [3]). A methodological improvement over [3]: **all experiments are repeated with 3 random seeds**, with error bars in figures representing the min/max across seeds and markers representing the mean—addressing the randomness introduced by pseudo-label sampling and triplet sampling. Model side: using all models reported in [3] as baselines (ResNet15, DSCNN-L, DSCNN-M, DSCNN-S four families), OnDA is applied to the largest and best-performing two (ResNet15 and DS-CNN-L) and their offline $\rho=25\%/50\%$ versions; online pruning ratios take three levels: 25%, 50%, 75%; training/adaptation hyperparameters remain unchanged throughout.

### Definition and Rationale for Evaluation Metrics

- **Task Metric: Accuracy at the False Alarm Rate of 0.5 per hour (Acc w/ FARh=0.5)**, aligned with [3]. Chosen instead of general accuracy or AUC because the usability of always-on wake-up is determined by the "hourly false wake-up budget"—comparing accuracy at a fixed false alarm rate is a fair comparison at the same working point, which is the conventional metric in the KWS domain close to product constraints.
- **Efficiency Metrics**: Model parameter count (x-axis 0–500k in Fig. 2); latency and energy of single-step fine-tuning and single-step inference on Jetson Orin Nano; measured SoC power (W) using on-board current sensors; and cumulative energy difference $\Delta E$ (J) over inference steps.

### Detailed Comparison with Baseline Methods and SOTA

**Accuracy-Size Pareto Frontier (Fig. 2)**: Both OnDA pipelines push the frontier beyond the baseline and "offline pruning only" models, especially in the small-size region. The Pareto optima mostly come from the ResNet family—the paper's explanation is that starting from a larger DNN leaves more freedom for subsequent pruning/adaptation optimization. Specific numbers (HeySnips): at the same accuracy as the most accurate baseline ResNet15, the best OnDA configuration compresses by **3.33×**; relative to the DS-CNN-L baseline, the highest same-performance compression reaches **9.63×**, and this point is smaller and more accurate. Abstract wording: up to **9.63×** model size compression relative to the unpruned baseline. On HeySnapdragon, the best compression at the same performance as ResNet15 is **1.7×**. Additionally, the paper indeed confirms: **pruning using in-domain data outperforms directly fine-tuning a network pruned offline**. In the accuracy-size dimension, the two pipelines tie (each producing multiple Pareto optima), and the deciding factor shifts to deployment cost.

**Jetson Orin Nano Deployment Measurements (Table 1, Fig. 3, GPU and Multi-core CPU)**: Measurements are performed on two points circled in the left graph of Fig. 2 (best compression at same accuracy, similar accuracy/size, belonging to OnDA-1 and OnDA-2 respectively), with hyperparameters and total training epochs kept consistent, so differences in duration/power at each step are purely explained by architecture differences induced by pruning. Gains relative to baseline [3]:

| Compute Unit | Pipeline | Fine-tuning Latency/Energy | Inference Latency/Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPU | OnDA-1 | 1.52× / 1.64× | 1.57× / 1.77× |
| GPU | OnDA-2 | 1.29× / 1.36× | 1.91× / 2.55× |
| CPU | OnDA-1 | 1.86× / 1.94× | 1.93× / 2.07× |
| CPU | OnDA-2 | 1.8× / 1.87× | 2.34× / 2.53× |

Abstract and conclusion wording: relative to weight-only adaptation, the latency and energy improvements for on-device training/inference are up to 1.52×/1.57× and 1.64×/1.77× (GPU, OnDA-1); contribution point wording: relative to [3] baseline DNN, inference latency is reduced to a minimum of 1.57×/1.93×, and inference energy to 1.77×/2.07× (GPU/CPU). The power trajectory in Fig. 3 tells the deployment story intuitively: the x-axis sequentially lists the stages of the pipeline (OnDA-1: Pruning $\rightarrow$ Fine-tuning $\rightarrow$ Standby $\rightarrow$ Inference; OnDA-2: Fine-tuning $\rightarrow$ Pruning + Re-fine-tuning $\rightarrow$ Standby $\rightarrow$ Inference), and the y-axis is the measured SoC power (W). On the time axis, OnDA-1's online pruning + fine-tuning completes in about **93 s**, while OnDA-2 drags to about **157 s** due to two rounds of fine-tuning; afterwards, it enters the standby phase, then any number $n$ of inference steps. The energy account (Fig. 3 bottom row, $\Delta E$ positive is green, negative is red): OnDA-1 pays a brief initial negative $\Delta E$ (HAP scoring cost) but quickly turns positive thanks to shorter fine-tuning and inference; OnDA-2's second round of fine-tuning introduces considerable energy overhead, delaying the break-even point to **after more than 10⁵ inference steps**—for an always-on scenario with roughly one inference per second, this means the device must run continuously for over twenty hours before it starts to "break even." A counter-intuitive data point worth noting: OnDA-2's single-step inference gain is actually larger (GPU 1.91×/2.55× vs OnDA-1's 1.57×/1.77×)—the two evaluation points have similar model sizes, and the paper does not analyze the cause (a reasonable explanation is that the two criteria retain dense sub-networks of different shapes with different hardware efficiencies, but this is the author's inference, not the paper's conclusion). Final verdict: **Data-aware online pruning before fine-tuning (OnDA-1) is the superior solution**—HAP alone is more expensive than L1, but placed at the beginning of adaptation, it allows the subsequent entire fine-tuning to proceed on the small model, resulting in a significantly better overall account.

### Findings from Ablation Studies

The paper does not have traditional module-by-module ablation, but instead a controlled design space scan: offline $\rho \in \{0, 25\%, 50\%\}$ $\times$ online $\rho \in \{25\%, 50\%, 75\%\}$ $\times$ pipeline $\{$OnDA-1, OnDA-2$\}$ $\times$ architecture $\{$ResNet15, DS-CNN-L$\}$ $\times$ dataset $\{$HeySnips, HeySnapdragon$\}$, with hyperparameters unchanged and three seed repetitions. Four findings:

1. **Online pruning stacked on top of offline pruning is still effective**, with the greatest gains in the small model interval—offline compression does not squeeze out all redundancy, and in-domain data can still prune further.
2. **In-domain data-driven pruning outperforms "offline prune small + direct fine-tune"**—proving that the information source for pruning decisions (in-domain vs. pre-training distribution) is more critical than "whether pruning occurred."
3. **Larger starting point means more freedom**: Pareto optima concentrate in the ResNet family, indicating that large models + online pruning outperform directly choosing small models.
4. **Criterion $\times$ Timing is structurally bound**: Data-agnostic can only go via O2 (fine-tune first, then prune, then fine-tune again); data-aware can go via O1 (prune first, then fine-tune); accuracy-wise they tie, but in deployment cost, O1 wins completely.

Uncovered ablation dimensions (not reported by the paper): The impact of the number of Hutchinson samples, the sensitivity of threshold $\gamma^\pm$ calibration, and the degree of interference of pseudo-label noise on pruning scoring and fine-tuning.

Expanding on Finding 2 alone, because it best illustrates the weight of the word "online": Offline pruning of a small network followed by in-domain data fine-tuning looks like a different path to the same destination as OnDA—both result in "small model + in-domain adaptation." The difference lies in the distribution upon which the pruning decision is based: the offline version decides who to keep and who to delete based on the pre-training distribution, where channel importance is an estimate of the "average world"; OnDA makes decisions based on the loss surface curvature estimated from pseudo-labeled data (even if few and noisy), where importance is an estimate of "this deployment site." The redundancy deleted by the former might happen to be key features in the target domain (e.g., frequency bands dominated by live noise); the latter, by scoring based on live data, naturally avoids such mis-deletion. Experimentally, this gap ultimately manifests as a clear separation in the small-size interval on the Pareto frontier, which is the fundamental reason why 9.63× compression can maintain "smaller and more accurate." Finding 3 hints from another direction to practitioners: rather than struggling offline to choose the skeleton size, it is better to choose a slightly larger skeleton and leave the pruning to the online stage—redundancy here is not waste, but freedom for adaptation.

## Main Contributions

1. **First to couple architecture adaptation (online structured channel pruning) with weight adaptation (on-device training)** into the self-learning pipeline for personalized KWS, providing two insertion timings O1/O2 and a complete pipeline family orthogonal and combinable with offline pruning P.
2. **Provides the Accuracy (at FARh=0.5 working point) vs. Model Size Pareto Frontier on HeySnips and HeySnapdragon**: Using [3]'s ResNet15 and DS-CNN-L as baselines, achieving up to 3.33× and 9.63× compression under the same task performance; and empirically proving that "pruning using in-domain data outperforms directly fine-tuning a network pruned offline."
3. **First to provide a real deployment account for such pipelines**: Measured on Jetson Orin Nano GPU/CPU, inference latency reduced to a minimum of 1.57×/1.93×, inference energy to 1.77×/2.07×; and judging "data-aware pruning before fine-tuning" as the preferred option via energy break-even analysis.
4. **Methodological Contribution**: All experiments repeated with three seeds + error bars, and a deployment-level evaluation metric accumulating energy by inference steps (rather than wall-clock time).

One-sentence positioning: The pruning criteria are not new (L1 [5], HAP [6] are both existing methods); the novelty lies in moving architectural decisions into the online, pseudo-labeled, few-shot scenario, and providing the final answer for criterion-timing binding using deployment cost.

## Limitations and Future Work

### Technical Limitations of the Method

- **One-shot pruning, fixed levels**: Pruning is one-shot (original text in conclusion chapter: "one-shot data-aware pruning"), and the ratio can only be selected from offline preset levels of 25%/50%/75%. The most critical decision itself—"how aggressively to prune"—is still made offline, creating tension with the paper's premise that "architecture should be online adaptive."
- **HAP's compute threshold is only verified on watt-level platforms**: The cost of Hutchinson trace estimation is comparable to one backward pass, but baseline [3] targets milliwatt-level ultra-low-power audio sensors; whether such targets can afford online HAP scoring is not verified by the paper (the paper does not report the absolute latency/energy of the pruning step itself, Table 1 only lists fine-tuning and inference steps).
- **Single compression form**: Only structured channel pruning was experimented with; scalability to unstructured/semi-structured pruning is only declared, with no data.
- **Dual role of pseudo-label noise is not quantified**: Noise both pollutes HAP's curvature estimation (the basis for scoring) and fine-tuning supervision (B3 objective); how errors propagate through these two paths and the sensitivity to final accuracy are not reported by the paper.
- **Peak memory during on-device training is not reported**: For MCU-class platforms, RAM budget during training is often a harder constraint than compute.

### Shortcomings in Experimental Design

- **Incomplete architecture coverage**: Only ResNet15 and DS-CNN-L from the four baseline families were integrated with OnDA; DSCNN-M/S served only as offline reference points, raising doubts about whether the conclusions hold for small architectures.
- **Dataset dependency of gains is not explained**: Compression at the same performance on HeySnapdragon is only 1.7×, far lower than the 3.33× on HeySnips, and the paper does not analyze the reason.
- **Narrow comparison scope**: No head-to-head deployment comparison with other efficiency/adaptation routes such as quantization ([14] route), NAS ([16][17]), or domain learning ([4]); other works in the personalization direction ([2][8][9], including ProtoNet variants and multi-task personalization) are also not directly compared—the only baseline is [3].
- **Absolute accuracy values are not in the main text**: All accuracies are presented only as curves in Fig. 2 (y-axis range approx. 70–95%), requiring a return to [3] for reproduction and horizontal citation.
- **Single hardware platform**: Only Jetson Orin Nano (GPU + multi-core CPU), lacking data support for extrapolating conclusions to lower power levels.

### Possible Directions for Future Improvement

- **Composite with quantization**: Structured pruning and low-bit quantization (QAT) are orthogonal; their superposition is expected to compress on-device fine-tuning and inference costs by another order of magnitude, and test the sensitivity of pruned networks to quantization.
- **Iterative / ratio-adaptive online pruning**: Move the selection of $\rho$ into online decision-making as well (e.g., automatically setting levels based on validation-style pseudo-label feedback), or even introduce pruning-re-growth dynamics, completing the last piece of "fully online adaptive architecture."
- **Downward migration to milliwatt-level platforms**: Measure HAP overhead on MCU/NPU targets, or find cheaper second-order approximations/first-order alternative criteria, making the O1 pipeline viable on the ultra-low-power sensors originally set by [3].
- **Unstructured/Semi-structured pruning + sparse operators**: Re-run the same timing-criterion matrix on hardware supporting sparsity to verify the paper's claim of "principally scalable."
- **Joint adaptation of user and acoustic domain**: Merge with the domain learning route of [4] to simultaneously adapt "who is speaking/what is being said" and "in what environment it is being said."
- **Noise-aware pruning criteria**: Explicitly model pseudo-label confidence in HAP scoring (e.g., weighting by $d(x)$ distance) to mitigate the pollution of curvature estimation by pseudo-label noise.
