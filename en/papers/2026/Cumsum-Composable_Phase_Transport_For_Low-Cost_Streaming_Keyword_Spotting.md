# Cumsum-Composable Phase Transport for Low-Cost Streaming Keyword Spotting

- **Authors/Affiliations**: Mahesh Godavarti (A Carrot, Inc., listed as an independent researcher; affiliation confirmed from the paper’s first page)
- **Date**: July 22, 2026 (arXiv:2607.20086v1 [cs.SD], metadata date 2026.07)
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20086
- **Keywords**: keyword spotting, phase transport, cumulative sum, streaming inference, unitary rotation, state-space model, prefix difference

## Problem Statement

### Problem Background and Domain Pain Points

The core contradiction in streaming speech systems is the need to continuously retain temporal evidence under strict latency and cost constraints. Keyword Spotting (KWS) is the most acute scenario for this contradiction: KWS systems on smart speakers, earbuds, and wearable devices operate in continuous listening mode, making them extremely sensitive to per-frame inference latency, memory footprint, and energy consumption, while simultaneously requiring zero miss rate for wake words.

Modern State Space Models (SSMs, including the S4, S5, and Mamba lineages) are viewed as strong candidates for streaming speech: they replace the pairwise attention of all historical frames in attention mechanisms with a small, compact recursive state, and selective variants can decide what to retain or forget as input arrives. This narrative holds for long-context tasks. However, the authors of this paper keenly point out that for short-command keyword detection (around one second), the actual trade-offs are completely different: a one-second speech command is a finite local pattern, and compact CNNs (such as DS-CNN, TC-ResNet, BC-ResNet) have long been well-mapped to commercial hardware. If an SSM-like model must rely on scan kernels, repeated state updates, and non-trivial backpropagation, its asymptotically linear complexity may not translate to low training costs on small tasks—the asymptotic $O$ advantage of linear complexity is completely eaten up by constant-factor disadvantages at the implementation level when sequence lengths are only a few hundred frames. This is the pain point pointed to by the word "low-cost" in the title: the problem is not model expressivity, but the real overhead of training and inference on commercial GPUs (the paper measures on a Tesla T4, an entry-level GPU oriented towards inference).

### Specific Deficiencies of Existing Methods

The paper analyzes the deficiencies of three existing paths one by one:

First, **the constant factor disadvantage of SSM scan kernels**. Selective scan introduces parallel prefix scan kernels to break the convolutional form, typically requiring custom Triton or CUDA implementations. The paper’s primitive-level measurements under matched conditions (Table 5, mel layer sequence size $T=200$, $N=40$, Tesla T4) provide hard numbers: with a batch size of 1, `torch.cumsum` takes 84.9 microseconds, while Triton scan takes 270.6 microseconds, a 3.2x difference; with a batch size of 32, the gap widens to 4.5x (73.4 microseconds vs. 331.8 microseconds); with a batch size of 128, it is 3.7x (140.5 microseconds vs. 514.5 microseconds). That is, at the level of the most common sequence primitives, the scan kernel incurs a wall-clock cost more than three times higher, and requires maintaining non-standard custom kernel code.

Second, **decaying recursion destroys the numerical conditioning of prefix representations**. This is a deep deficiency at the methodological level. If classic exponential decay memory (the decaying linear state of S4/S5) is to be rewritten in prefix sum form, the prefix accumulator will involve division by the decay factor (detailed in Section 2.5 below), causing scale separation, overflow risks, and loss of low-bit information under finite precision. This makes the most convenient path of "parallel batch computation for training, streaming recursion for inference" naturally unfriendly to decaying models, creating a numerical crack between the training and streaming inference implementations.

Third, **fixed frontends and learned frontends each have shortcomings**. The FFT + log-mel frontend provides strong inductive bias on short commands (the MelCNNMaxPool with FFT+log-mel frontend reached 97.1% in Table 3 of the paper, the highest among all frontend experiments), but it is not a learnable module designed for streaming low-latency; learned frontends like LEAF can be trained end-to-end but usually rely on convolutions and non-instantaneous filter designs. The raw waveform Conv1d baseline (RawCNN) in this paper’s setting only achieved 91.0% (Table 3), indicating that the path of learning directly from raw samples is significantly disadvantaged under the same parameter budget.

### Key Challenges This Paper Aims to Solve

The challenges set by the paper can be summarized in four points: (1) Design a temporal aggregation primitive whose batch training form and streaming inference form are **exactly equivalent** (not approximately equivalent), so that training and deployment share the same representation; (2) This equivalence must be built on a **numerically well-conditioned** structural assumption, where the prefix accumulator does not amplify numerical scales due to inverse operations; (3) Accuracy on Google Speech Commands v2 must be **competitive** with controlled compact CNN baselines, with the parameter count kept to a similar magnitude (in the range of 20k to 30k); (4) In system-level comparisons under matched conditions, achieve **lower latency** without custom scan kernels. Taken together, these four points answer one question: for short-duration speech tasks, is there a temporal layer cheaper than scan that retains the advantages of the SSM-style "compact streaming state"?

## Methodology

### Overall Architecture Design and Design Motivation

The main architecture (Table 1 in the paper) is a minimalist streaming pipeline where every step from waveform to trigger score can be executed incrementally frame by frame:

1. **Acoustic Frontend**: 40-dimensional log-mel spectrogram, 16 kHz audio, 400-sample FFT window, hop of 80 samples (5 ms) or 160 samples (10 ms); SpecAugment applied during training. Why use a fixed mel instead of a learned frontend? Because the appendix frontend ablation (Tables 11–14) proves that the fixed FFT/mel/log pipeline still provides the strongest inductive bias on short commands, with all learned frontends lagging behind. Thus, the strongest model places cumsum after mel features rather than replacing mel.
2. **Embedding Layer**: A linear mapping projects the 40-dimensional mel frame $m_t$ to a $d$-dimensional real vector $h_t$.
3. **Value Projection**: A linear mapping per layer transforms $h_t$ into real and imaginary pairs $(a_t, b_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d/2} \times \mathbb{R}^{d/2}$, interpreted as complex numbers $z_t = a_t + i b_t$. Why project to the complex domain first? Because the subsequent phase transport operator acts on complex channels, and complex coordinates are the natural carrier for rotation.
4. **Fixed Phase Bank**: Each layer learns a frequency $\omega_{\ell,k}$ per complex channel, with phase $\phi_{t,k} = t\omega_{\ell,k}$, and the transport operator is a diagonal unitary matrix $U_t = \mathrm{diag}(e^{i\phi_{t,1}}, \dots, e^{i\phi_{t,n}})$. Why is the phase fixed as a linear function of time? Because this makes the phase a deterministic function of time within the layer, allowing the entire sequence to be processed via prefix operations at once during training, and requiring only a single prefix update step during streaming inference—one of the prerequisites for the "exact isomorphism between batch and streaming" to hold.
5. **Cumsum Transport**: Maintain prefix state $p_t = \sum_{\tau \le t} U_\tau^{-1} z_\tau$, read out $y_t = U_t(p_t - p_{t-W})$.
6. **Layer Update**: Concatenate the real and imaginary parts of $y_t$, pass through BatchNorm and GLU gating, then add residual: $h_t \leftarrow h_t + \mathrm{GLU}(\mathrm{BN}([\Re y_t, \Im y_t]))$ (Eq. 5). Why use gated residual instead of direct replacement? Because the output of the transport layer is window-aggregated evidence; gating allows the network to decide layer by layer how much aggregated information to retain versus how much of the original $h_t$ to keep, while the residual ensures gradient pathways during deep stacking.
7. **Classifier**: Take the modulus of the complex channels of the last layer, apply temporal max pooling, and a linear 12-class classifier. Why take the modulus? Because the modulus is invariant to phase rotation, freeing the discrimination basis from absolute phase to energy envelopes, which naturally suits command patterns with indefinite alignment when combined with max pooling.
8. **Tied Variant**: All layers share the value projection and GLU, with only frequencies and BatchNorm independent per layer. Why tie? Table 8 shows that tying reduces parameters from 82,252 to 24,012 (approx. 3.4x reduction) in the W=20, 4-layer setting, with test accuracy dropping only from 96.3% to 95.2%, indicating that "repeated local transport plus shared projection" itself is a compact temporal mixer that does not rely on stacking parameters to win.

The streaming form of the entire frontend is: waveform enters a short analysis buffer, one frame is output per hop, and frames sequentially pass through $x_{1:t} \to m_t \to z_t \to p_t = p_{t-1} + U_{t-1}^{-1} z_t \to y_t = U_t(p_t - p_{t-W}) \to s_t$. The model does not need to wait for a full one-second crop; instead, it updates scores rolling at the frame rate, triggering when exceeding the task threshold. Memory per layer only requires the current prefix $p_t$ plus a circular buffer storing the old prefix (for the hard window case) or block summaries (for the block decay case).

### Mathematical Principles of the Core Algorithm

The entire mathematical skeleton of the method is condensed into three equations.

**Transport Window Sum (Eq. 1)**: Given complex-valued channels $z_t \in \mathbb{C}^n$ and diagonal unitary transport operators $U_t \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}$,

$$y_t = U_t \sum_{\tau=\max(1, t-W+1)}^{t} U_\tau^{-1} z_\tau$$

Intuitive interpretation: Evidence from time $\tau$ is first transformed back to a common coordinate system by inverse rotation $U_\tau^{-1}$ for accumulation, and then rotated to the current frame's coordinate system by $U_t$ upon reading out. This is an analogy of "local phase transport for recursive memory"—information from time $\tau$ is transported to the coordinate framework of time $t$ before aggregation.

**Prefix Accumulator (Eq. 2) and Window Difference Readout (Eq. 3)**: Let $p_t = \sum_{\tau=1}^{t} U_\tau^{-1} z_\tau$ ($p_s = 0$ for $s \le 0$), then

$$y_t = U_t (p_t - p_{t-W})$$

This is the meaning of "cumsum-composable" in the title: the entire layer reduces to the cumulative sum of transported values, a single delayed prefix subtraction, and a single element-wise rotation back to the current frame. These three operations are all standard primitives of parallel sequence computation (Blelloch 1990's prefix sum system).

**Proposition 1 (Exact Equivalence of Batch and Streaming)**: For fixed parameters and fixed input sequences, whether $p_t$ is obtained via batch-wise cumsum of the entire sequence or recursively updated via streaming $p_t = p_{t-1} + U_{t-1}^{-1} z_t$, the $y_t$ output by Eq. 3 is exactly the same. The proof is only one line: expanding the streaming recursion yields $\sum_{\tau=1}^t U_\tau^{-1} z_\tau$, which is term-by-term equal to the batch definition. This proposition is the fulcrum of the entire paper—the training graph and deployment graph are mathematically the same object, eliminating the most common hidden danger of streaming models: "training-inference inconsistency."

**Why Transport Before Accumulation (Section 2.2)**: Ordinary cumsum aggregates in a fixed coordinate system, where temporal order enters the model only through window membership and subsequent non-linearities; phase transport makes the contribution of time $\tau$ to time $t$ output become $U_t U_\tau^{-1} z_\tau$. Under fixed learned phases $\phi_{t,k} = t\omega_k$, the $k$-th complex coordinate is multiplied by $e^{i(t-\tau)\omega_k}$—that is, this layer implements a **learned oscillatory kernel with relative lag as the independent variable**, where aggregated evidence depends on relative phase rather than unordered sums. This uses the same family of mathematical tools as sinusoidal positional encoding, RoPE (Rotary Positional Encoding), and diagonal complex SSMs (S4D/DSS), but the narrowing point of this paper is: finite window and unitary transport parameterization make online recursion and batch training computation exactly the same prefix representation.

**Fixed Learned Phase (Eq. 4, Section 2.4)**: $\phi_{t,k} = t\omega_k$ gives each complex channel a learned eigen-oscillator. The rotation is unitary, so the transport part preserves norm; finite memory is explicitly supplied by window or block decay readout, rather than relying on continuously decaying oscillators. This is the key design of "forgetting mechanism externalized."

**Block Decay (Eq. 6, Section 2.7)**: Hard windows are efficient but have risks—discontinuous jumps in representation when old items exit the window (Section 2.7 admits this). Block decay replaces a single hard window with a geometrically weighted block sum:

$$y_t = U_t \sum_{b=0}^{K-1} \lambda^b \left( p_{t-bW} - p_{t-(b+1)W} \right)$$

It keeps the computation cumsum-style (each block is still a single prefix difference) while introducing smooth forgetting. The main experiments use hard windows, with block decay as an optional alternative for scenarios requiring smooth forgetting—this trade-off itself shows that the authors are aware of the edge mutation risks of hard windows, but they do not become bottlenecks in this task.

### Key Technical Innovation 1: Unitary Transport as a Structural Guarantee for Numerical Well-Conditioning of Prefix Factorization

This is the section with the highest density of thought in the entire paper (Section 2.5). Prefix construction requires applying inverse transport to each input value before accumulation. For unitary rotation, $U_t^{-1} = U_t^*$ and $\|U_t^{-1} z\| = \|z\|$, so the prefix terms maintain the same numerical scale as the projected values—this is the algebraic reason why both batch cumsum and streaming prefix updates are simple and stable.

The authors further argue via counterexamples why decaying transport cannot be used. If one takes $A_t = \rho^t U_t$ ($0 < \rho < 1$) and applies the same prefix form, the prefix accumulator will contain $A_\tau^{-1} z_\tau = \rho^{-\tau} U_\tau^{-1} z_\tau$. Although the final readout can multiply a small forward factor to cancel the scaling in exact arithmetic, the prefix state itself involves division by increasingly small numbers; under finite precision, this leads to large scale separation, overflow risks, and loss of low-bit information. Unitary transport avoids this class of conditioning number problems from the root, because the norm of the inverse operator used to build the prefix is always one. **Forgetting is thus moved outside the transport inversion**, introduced via hard window subtraction or block-level weighting—this is the complete answer to the design decision of "where to implement forgetting": do not decay in the transport, but decay at the readout end.

### Key Technical Innovation 2: Exact Isomorphism Pipeline Between Training and Streaming Inference

The engineering value of Proposition 1 lies in: during training, the entire sequence uses a single `torch.cumsum` (library function, zero custom kernels, auto-diff friendly); during deployment, the same state $p_t$ is updated frame by frame in one step, and readout uses a single subtraction. In contrast, scan-like models require parallel scan kernels for training and another set of recursive implementations for inference, with inconsistent numerical behaviors and code stacks for the two paths. The paper quantifies this advantage in the system-level comparison in Section 4.5: under matched architectures, the cumsum model trains in 16.2 seconds per epoch vs. 17.4 seconds for scan (1.07x faster), with single-example inference latency of 5.01 ms vs. 7.09 ms for batch size 1 (1.42x lower, Table 4), while accuracy is actually higher (94.82% vs. 94.33%). At the primitive level (Table 5), `torch.cumsum` is 3.2 to 4.5 times faster than Triton scan.

### Key Technical Innovation 3: Cumsum Acoustic Frontend and Phase-Carrying Features

The same prefix sum primitive can be moved before mel: maintaining a sliding sum of complex sinusoids on raw waveform samples, directly outputting streaming acoustic frames, equivalent to a filter bank implemented with rectangular window cumsum (echoing learned frontends like LEAF, but maintaining cumsum composability). Frontend ablation (Tables 11, 12) reveals an informative pattern: under non-overlapping hop 400 settings, adding real and imaginary channels with learned phase improves accuracy from 90.8% to 93.6%, and with frozen mel phase from 92.0% to 94.0%—because the amplitude of sliding cumsum is a noisier spectral estimate, and the phase channel hands over phase information within the window to the next layer. Engineering-wise, this frontend, after memory layout optimization (contiguous (B, N, T) layout, indexing step positions first before downstream operations), reduces the first layer's single-example latency from 5.8 ms to 284 microseconds, and finally to 337 microseconds with batch size 1, matching the 359 microseconds of FFT+log+embedding (Section 4.6).

### Key Technical Innovation 4: Compact Recipe for Small Window Deep Stacking with Weight Tying

Ablation (Table 7) shows that under the same nominal span of 40 frames ($L \times W$), switching from two layers W=20 to eight layers W=5 increases test accuracy from 94.4% to 96.2%—repeated tight windows with heavy non-linear gating are significantly better than a single large, undifferentiated accumulator. Tying (Table 8) saves another 3.4x parameters. The combination of both yields the cleanest efficiency point of 24.8K parameters and 96.8% accuracy (Table 2), and the strongest tied model of 51.6K parameters and 97.3% accuracy.

### Technical Differences with Existing Methods

Table 6 in the paper provides a positioning coordinate system: CNN/TCN are finite local receptive fields, convolution training, causal buffer streaming; S4/S5 are long-range or decaying linear states, scan or structured convolution, recursive streaming; Mamba is input-selective states, selective scan, recursive streaming; this paper is **finite unitary transport, training via cumulative sum plus prefix difference, streaming via prefix state plus circular buffer**. Compared to the closest diagonal complex SSM, this paper restricts transport to unit modulus rotation and explicitly provides memory length via prefix difference, thereby keeping the inverse transport inside the accumulator bounded. Compared to the MelCNNMaxPool baseline in the style of TC-ResNet, this paper replaces the entire temporal convolution after mel with cumsum-composable phase transport, with similar parameter counts (24,812 vs. 25,628) and close accuracy (96.8% vs. 97.1%, Table 2).

## Experimental Results

### Datasets Used and Their Scales

All experiments use Google Speech Commands v2 (Warden 2018), cropped to one second at 16 kHz. The label set has 12 classes: ten command words (yes, no, up, down, left, right, on, off, stop, go) plus unknown and silence. The unknown class is downsampled to match the average sample count of target commands; silence is generated from dataset background noise crops. The full training set uses the local full training set of approximately 36K samples, for 40 or 80 epochs (depending on the experiment); the smoke tests for architecture deconstruction use approximately 4K samples for two epochs and do not enter the formal results. It is worth noting: 36K is the scale of the paper's local setting, which is less than the common 85K context scale of the official GSC v2 training set. This is a premise that must be remembered when evaluating external comparability.

### Definition and Rationale for Evaluation Metrics

The main metric is **clip-level test accuracy** (single seed). The rationale and limitations are intertwined: the clip classifier metric allows models with "exact streaming implementation" to align directly with standard baselines, but the paper itself admits in Section 2.3 that continuous streaming KWS usually also requires trigger-based metrics such as false acceptance rate, false rejection rate, and detection delay (citing Rybakov 2020, Jose 2022), which this paper does not evaluate—this is an honestly annotated evaluation gap. Auxiliary metrics include: parameter count (measuring compactness), single-example inference latency with batch size 1 (measuring edge-side cost), wall-clock time per epoch for training (measuring training overhead), and primitive-level micro-benchmarks (Table 5, isolating implementation differences). Hardware and software stack: Tesla T4, CUDA 12.4, PyTorch 2.6, Torchaudio 2.6, training batch size 128; optimizer Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$.

### Detailed Comparison with Baseline Methods and SOTA

**Main Comparison (Table 2, single-seed test accuracy)**: The strongest mel+cumsum single-seed reached 97.3% (MelCumsumFixed, W=5, 8 layers, n=80, hop=160, untied, 40 epochs, 160,892 parameters); the tied variant with 51.6K parameters (W=10, 8 layers, n=120, hop=80, 80 epochs) also reached 97.3%; the MelCNNMaxPool baseline was 97.1% (25,628 parameters); the 24.8K tied model was 96.8% (W=10, 8 layers, n=80, hop=80, 80 epochs); the bidirectional tied variant was 96.8% (31,612 parameters); the 37,012 parameter tied model was 97.0%. The conclusion is: cumsum-composable temporal primitives, under exact streaming prefix representation, achieve accuracy comparable to controlled CNN baselines.

**Frontend Comparison (Table 3)**: FFT+log-mel MelCNNMaxPool 97.1% (25,628 parameters) ranks first; frozen mel phase cumsum spectrum frontend plus log amplitude plus real/imaginary parts 95.2% (46,828 parameters); learned phase version 93.9% (30,508 parameters); learned spectrum using only log amplitude 94.1% (25,668 parameters); raw waveform Conv1d only 91.0% (25,228 parameters). This set of numbers supports the decision that "the strongest architecture places cumsum after mel": the inductive bias of the fixed mel pipeline still dominates on short commands.

**Matched Condition Cumsum vs. Scan (Table 4, Section 4.5)**: This is the core of the system-level argument. The two models share 40-dimensional log-mel, hop 80, d=80, eight-layer tied, same projection, BatchNorm, GLU residual, max pooling readout; the only substantial difference is the temporal primitive—MelCumsumFixed uses `torch.cumsum` plus prefix subtraction to implement W=10 hard window, while MelScanFixed uses custom Triton scan to implement learned per-channel exponential decay (320 more parameters, one decay scalar per layer per frequency). Results: cumsum test 94.82% vs. scan 94.33%, validation 94.8 vs. 94.2, single-example latency 5.01 ms vs. 7.09 ms, training per epoch 16.2 s vs. 17.4 s. Particularly explanatory is: the decay values learned by the scan model imply an effective window of 3 to 21 frames—learned recursive memory naturally converges to short local filters in this task, and the hard window cumsum directly supplies this short memory. This constitutes direct evidence for the "finite memory hypothesis."

**Primitive Timing (Table 5) and Frontend Latency (Section 4.6)**: As mentioned above, `torch.cumsum` is 3.2 to 4.5 times faster than Triton scan; the optimized raw audio cumsum frontend single-example latency is 337 microseconds, matching the 359 microseconds of the mel frontend. It is worth noting that the absolute accuracy of the matched baseline (Table 4, 94.8%) is significantly lower than the 97.3% in the main table (Table 2), because the matched baseline adopted different training and architectural details from the optimal row in Table 2 (hop 80, d=80 tied small model). The paper does not explain the sources of the gap item by item, only emphasizing the validity of the comparison under the same conditions.

### Findings from Ablation Experiments

The appendix provides five groups of ablations, all single-seed:

1. **Window and Depth (Table 7)**: Under the same nominal span of 40 frames, W=20 two layers 94.4%, W=10 four layers 95.8%, W=5 eight layers 96.2%; increasing nominal span to 80 frames (W=20 four layers) 95.2%, 160 frames (W=20 eight layers) 95.5%. Conclusion: small window deep stacking is superior to shallow wide aggregation; cumsum should be used as a local temporal component with non-linear gating, rather than as a large accumulator.
2. **Weight Tying (Table 8)**: Under W=20 four layers, untied 82,252 parameters, test 96.3%; tied 24,012 parameters, test 95.2% (approx. 3.4x parameter reduction, drop of 1.1 percentage points); MelCNNMaxPool reference 25,628 parameters, 95.4%. The tied model matches the CNN reference with almost the same parameter count.
3. **Fixed Frequency Scan (Table 9)**: The optimal tied model 97.3% (51,612 parameters) equals the larger untied model (160,892 parameters), saving approx. 3.1x parameters; the worst configuration (W=40 two layers) 94.5% again confirms the direction of deep stacking with small windows.
4. **Architecture Alternatives (Table 10)**: To answer the reviewer-style question "is the benefit just from stacking many small non-linear stages," the paper compares progressive cumsum-ResNet variants: the equal-width fixed cumsum tied model 96.8% is significantly higher than the ResNet variant with progressive channels [24,24,32,48] 94.9% (similar parameters, 25,380 vs. 24,812), and also higher than the modulus deep variant 93.0%. This shows that the fixed phase transport design itself contributes beyond "generic compact stacking."
5. **Cumsum Frontend Ablation (Tables 11–14)**: Under hop 160, frozen phase plus real/imaginary 95.2%, learned phase plus real/imaginary 93.9%, log amplitude only 93.4%; under hop 400 non-overlapping settings, the benefit of phase channels is amplified (learned phase 90.8% to 93.6%, frozen 92.0% to 94.0%). The comparison in Table 13 is clever: in the Hann-windowed sin-cos convolution filter bank, the frozen mel spacing log amplitude filter bank is actually the strongest (95.7%), without needing real/imaginary parts—because the amplitude spectral estimate of the convolution filter bank is cleaner than the sliding rectangular cumsum, contrasting that the cumsum frontend amplitude is noisier and thus needs phase channels to supplement information. The end-to-end cumsum stacking in Table 14 (frontend and temporal both using cumsum) reaches a maximum of 93.5%, confirming that the pure cumsum path is feasible but lags behind the mel-posterior path.

## Main Contributions

The paper explicitly lists six contributions in the introduction (self-limited by the authors to local experiments):

1. **Formalization of the Phase Transport Layer**: Provides a phase transport layer where finite window representation can be computed by cumulative sum and has an exact streaming equivalent form (Eqs. 1–3 and Proposition 1).
2. **Clarification of the Structural Role of Unitary Transport**: Argues that unitary transport is the structural assumption that makes prefix factorization numerically well-conditioned—inverse transport remains bounded, and forgetting is externalized to the readout end (Section 2.5).
3. **Accompanying Streaming Frontend**: Audio to low-latency mel or learned spectrum frames, with one cumsum state update per frame, emitting rolling keyword scores from finite window states.
4. **Evidence of Accuracy Competitiveness**: Stacking local mel+cumsum is comparable to compact CNN baselines under local Speech Commands settings, reaching a maximum of 97.3% (Table 2).
5. **Evidence of Parameter Efficiency**: Tied local cumsum layers maintain strong accuracy under small parameter counts, with a 24.8K parameter model achieving 96.8%.
6. **Matched System Baseline**: Matched comparison against learned decay scan shows lower latency and comparable accuracy, without requiring custom scan kernels (Tables 4, 5).

From a broader perspective, the true contribution of this paper is welding the "SSM-style compact streaming state" with the "cheapest primitive (prefix sum)" and pointing out that the numerical conditioning at the weld is guaranteed by unitarity—providing a third choice between CNN and SSM for short-duration streaming speech tasks.

## Limitations and Future Work

### Technical Limitations of the Method

- **Applicability Boundary of the Short Memory Hypothesis**: The entire design presupposes that keyword detection only requires finite local memory. In the matched experiments, the effective window learned by scan is only 3 to 21 frames, and hard windows are sufficient; once the task requires longer-range dependencies (long keywords, context adaptation, continuous large vocabulary recognition), it becomes questionable whether W-limited hard windows or block decay can cover it. The paper also does not conduct systematic exploration beyond nominal spans of window lengths exceeding 40 frames (in Table 7, the W=20 eight-layer 160-frame span is actually lower than the small window stacking of the 40-frame span).
- **Discontinuity of Hard Windows**: Representation jumps when old items exit the window (admitted in Section 2.7); block decay is a theoretical alternative but not adopted in the main experiments, and its stability benefits lack data.
- **Expression Upper Bound of Fixed Frequency Oscillators**: Each channel having one learned frequency constitutes a fixed relative-lag oscillatory kernel, unable to select memory based on input content like Mamba. Whether the trade-off of expressivity for implementation simplicity is worthwhile on harder tasks has not been tested.
- **VRAM Pressure for Large Batch Training**: The cumsum frontend is still VRAM-heavy under large training batches because it materializes full-resolution complex rotation tensors; fusing rotation, cumsum, window subtraction, and step operations is listed as a next-step implementation direction (Section 4.6), indicating that the current implementation is not yet in place.
- **Backpropagation Path Not Analyzed**: The paper emphasizes the forward batch/streaming equivalence but does not provide memory and stability analysis for the cumsum path backpropagation on longer sequences (e.g., full ASR).

### Deficiencies in Experimental Design

The paper's self-statement (Section 6) is worth recording item by item: all results are single-seed, variance is not reported; the SSM comparison uses local research implementations rather than optimized official implementations, and the scan baseline's Triton kernel may not represent the optimal level; the system baseline is limited to one GPU environment (Tesla T4) and one matched architecture; Speech Commands is a clip-level benchmark, lacking perturbation suites such as pitch shift, reverberation, codec distortion, speaker holdout, and event-triggered start metrics. Adding two points that are not explicitly stated by the paper but are equally valid: first, the training set is the local setting of approximately 36K samples, inconsistent with the larger training splits commonly used in the community. Absolute numbers like 97.3% are not directly horizontally comparable with external literature (the paper only compares with local controlled MelCNNMaxPool, and does not compare with standard optimized baselines such as DS-CNN, TC-ResNet, BC-ResNet, CRNN/TCN, small Conformer, etc.); second, the source of the gap between the absolute accuracy of the matched baseline (Table 4, 94.8%) and the main table (97.3%)—how much each of hop, width, and epoch count contributes—is not decomposed, and the 0.49 percentage point advantage of cumsum over scan under single-seed also lacks significance support. Additionally, the text refers to itself as a "draft," indicating it is aphase report.

### Possible Directions for Future Improvement

The six next steps listed by the paper (Section 6): (1) Re-run at least three seeds for the optimal tied mel+cumsum and MelCNNMaxPool; (2) Extend wall-clock, VRAM, throughput, and profiling logs to CPU, Apple Silicon, and embedded mobile targets; (3) Compare with standard optimized baselines such as DS-CNN, TC-ResNet, BC-ResNet, CRNN/TCN, small Conformer, etc.; (4) Cleanly separate the three families—mel+cumsum, learned spectrum cumsum, raw sample rate cumsum; (5) Add event-triggered command start detection, so that the model is also tested on latency and sparse triggering; (6) Extend perturbation tests to pitch shift, reverberation, background noise, codec artifacts, speaker holdout, and controlled phase amplitude perturbations.

Beyond the paper's设想 (vision), from the perspective of this deep reading, it is worth pointing out: promoting block decay from an alternative to the main method and quantifying its smooth forgetting benefits in streaming trigger scenarios; testing the composability of the phase kernel on custom keyword entries (different keywords sharing the frequency bank, only adjusting the readout layer); and promoting "unitary transport plus prefix difference" to a multi-band hierarchical structure (different layers with different hops, forming a multi-resolution memory pyramid) may be a scaling path more consistent with the architecture's nature than simply widening and deepening. Overall judgment: this is an honest architectural exploration paper, the value of which lies in proposing a mathematically clean, engineeringly minimalist streaming temporal primitive, and proving its cost-effectiveness under the short memory regime with matched experiments; its conclusion boundaries are equally clear—single-seed, single dataset, single hardware, all extrapolations must wait for the six subsequent experiments listed by the paper itself.
