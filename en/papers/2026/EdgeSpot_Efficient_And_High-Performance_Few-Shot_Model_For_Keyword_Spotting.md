# EdgeSpot: Efficient and High-Performance Few-Shot Model for Keyword Spotting

- **Authors/Affiliations**: Oguzhan Buyuksolak, Alican Gok, Osman Erman Okman (Analog Devices, Istanbul, Turkey)
- **Date**: January 2026 (arXiv:2601.16316v1, submitted Jan 22, 2026; Accepted at ICASSP 2026)
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16316
- **Keywords**: few-shot keyword spotting, edge computing, knowledge distillation, BC-ResNet backbone, trainable PCEN frontend, temporal self-attention, self-supervised speech model

## Problem Statement

### Problem Background and Domain Pain Points
Keyword Spotting (KWS) serves as the voice interface for hands-free interaction on edge devices such as smart speakers, wireless earbuds, and wearables, which require always-on listening for wake words. Traditional neural network-based KWS solutions suffer from two structural pain points: first, training requires massive amounts of data and significant computational resources, adhering to a "heavy retraining" paradigm; second, whenever the keyword set changes (e.g., switching a wake word or adding a custom command), the entire training pipeline must be rerun from scratch. The paper explicitly states in the introduction that this retraining process is "not feasible to adapt the models on edge hardware"—end devices lack both the data and the compute power for full retraining.

Few-Shot Keyword Spotting (FS-KWS) was born to break this deadlock: it uses compact deep network architectures combined with metric learning techniques (such as Prototypical Networks) to register new keywords with extremely few pronunciation samples. Its mechanism involves learning an embedding space where similar keywords cluster together; during user registration, a few spoken samples are provided, and their embeddings are averaged to form a class prototype (prototype). During inference, discrimination is performed by measuring the distance between the test sample's embedding and the prototype. In open-set scenarios, this means terminal users can customize wake words without any retraining. However, the cost of this paradigm is that embedding quality is everything, while edge hardware imposes strict limits on model size and computational load. Thus, "embedding discriminability" and "edge-friendliness" constitute a core contradiction.

### Specific Shortcomings of Existing Methods
The paper categorizes existing FS-KWS works into three lines and points out their shortcomings one by one:
- **Prototypical Network Route** (Mazumder et al., Interspeech 2021, Ref [1]) and **Fully Unsupervised Training Route** (Lee et al., Ref [2]): These established a prototype inference framework, but the backbone networks themselves were not optimized for efficiency in edge deployment.
- **ResNet + Metric Loss Route** (Rusci and Tuytelaars, Refs [3][4]): Trained using a ResNet backbone with triplet loss, achieving good results on FS-KWS tasks, but similarly using a generic backbone without adaptation for low-compute scenarios.
- **Self-Supervised Teacher Distillation Route** (the authors' previous work, Gok et al., arXiv:2506.17686, Ref [6]): Uses pre-trained Wav2Vec2.0 to provide robust features, distilling down-projected embeddings into a student model of the same ResNet architecture via a teacher-student knowledge distillation framework. While effective, the paper explicitly states that "this model architecture is not optimized enough for edge deployment"—the previous ResNet15 student network requires 235M MACs and 480k parameters (Table 2), which remains a burden for many MCU-class devices.

Meanwhile, the fixed-vocabulary KWS domain has accumulated a batch of efficient architectures (Refs [7][8][9], such as MatchboxNet). Among them, BC-ResNet (Ref [10], Interspeech 2021) is a state-of-the-art model on the Google Speech Commands (GSC) benchmark, possessing both computational efficiency and scalability. The problem is that these efficient architectures were designed for "fixed-vocabulary classification" and have never been systematically adapted to the few-shot, prototype-inference-based embedding learning paradigm. In other words, FS-KWS literature lacks a truly edge-friendly backbone, while efficient backbone literature lacks adaptation for FS-KWS—EdgeSpot fills this cross-domain gap.

### Key Challenges to Be Solved by This Paper
How to transform the efficiency champion of fixed-vocabulary KWS, BC-ResNet, into a high-quality few-shot embedding extractor, satisfying three conditions simultaneously: (1) Detection accuracy at fixed low False Alarm Rate (FAR) consistently outperforms the same-scale BC-ResNet baseline, even surpassing ResNet15 at a much lower cost; (2) Strong cross-domain generalization capability—performance must not collapse when trained on the MSWC corpus and tested on the distributionally distinct GSC; (3) Strictly maintain an edge-deployable resource footprint (the largest variant requires only 29.4M MACs and 128.3k parameters, Table 2). Every modified module must answer "why this change and not that," with minimal overhead.

## Methodology

### Overall Architecture Design and Design Motivation
The overall design philosophy of EdgeSpot is "performing three targeted minor surgeries on a strong and small backbone," rather than inventing a new backbone from scratch. The motivation for choosing BC-ResNet as the acoustic backbone is clear: it is state-of-the-art on the GSC fixed-vocabulary benchmark while providing efficiency and scalability (the width multiplier $\tau$ allows smooth scaling of the model), which are exactly the attributes needed for edge deployment; in contrast, the ResNet15 backbone used in previous FS-KWS works is inherently inefficient.

The three surgeries are (Fig. 1, Table 1):
- **(a) Trainable PCEN Frontend**: Inserted before the acoustic stack, replacing static log compression with learnable Per-Channel Energy Normalization, motivated by the desire to enhance cross-domain generalization (see below).
- **(b) Early Block Temporal Path Fusion (Fused BC-ResBlock)**: Only in the first two stages, replacing the depthwise separable structure of "temporal depthwise convolution + 1×1 pointwise convolution" with a single standard temporal convolution, motivated by the early fusion principles of EfficientNetV2.
- **(c) Lightweight Temporal Self-Attention Head**: Performs single-head Scaled Dot-Product Self-Attention (SDPA) along the time axis at the end of the network, modeling long-range temporal dependencies within short speech clips at minimal cost, outputting a 64-dimensional sentence-level embedding.

According to Table 1 (configuration at $\tau=1$), the complete data flow is: Input 1×40×101 Mel spectrogram → PCEN → conv2d(5×5)-BN-ReLU (16 channels, stride (2,1)) → Fused BC-ResBlock ×2 (n=2, c=8, s=1, d=1) → Fused BC-ResBlock ×2 (n=2, c=12, s=(2,1), d=(1,2)) → BC-ResBlock ×4 (n=4, c=16, s=(2,1), d=(1,4)) → BC-ResBlock ×4 (n=4, c=20, s=1, d=(1,8)) → Depthwise Convolution DW conv2d(5×5) (20 channels) → conv2d(1×1)-BN-ReLU (32 channels, compressing the frequency dimension to 1) → Depthwise Convolution DW conv1d(16) relative position encoding → SDPA-PReLU → conv1d(1) outputting a 64-dimensional embedding. It can be seen that the network only downsamples along the frequency dimension (40→20→10→5→1), while the time dimension maintains 101 frames without downsampling, instead using temporal dilated convolutions with strides (1,2)/(1,4)/(1,8) to expand the receptive field—this is to preserve full temporal resolution for the subsequent temporal attention. Note that Table 1 specifically states: regardless of $\tau$, the output channels of the final conv1d layer are always 1.

### Mathematical Principles of Core Algorithms
**PCEN Formula** (Eq. (1)(2)): For Mel filterbank energy $E(t,f)$, a causal IIR smoothing is first performed to obtain the gain baseline:

$M(t,f) = (1-s)\cdot M(t-1,f) + s\cdot E(t,f), \quad s \in (0,1),$

followed by automatic gain control and stabilized root compression:

$PCEN(t,f) = \left( \frac{E(t,f)}{(\epsilon + M(t,f))^\alpha} \right)^r + \delta - \delta^r,$

where $\epsilon>0$ is a small constant, $\alpha \in [0,1]$ controls the strength of automatic gain, and $\delta>0$ and $r \in (0,1]$ define the stabilized root compression. All operations are differentiable, allowing end-to-end joint training with the entire network. EdgeSpot's specific configuration is to learn channel-shared scalar parameters $\alpha, r, \delta$ and channel-shared smoothing coefficient $s$, with $\epsilon$ fixed at 1e-6—choosing channel sharing over per-channel parameters is to suppress the frontend overhead to nearly zero, a typical edge-side design trade-off.

**Relative Position Encoding** (Eq. (3)(4)): Given channel-prioritized temporal features $X \in \mathbb{R}^{C\tau \times T}$, relative position information is added in a residual manner via per-channel depthwise 1D convolution: $\tilde{X} = X + \phi(X)$. $\phi$ is a depthwise convolution with kernel length $\kappa$ ("same" padding maintains length): $[\phi(X)]_{c,t} = \sum_{\Delta=-\lfloor\kappa/2\rfloor}^{\lfloor\kappa/2\rfloor} K_{c,\Delta}\cdot X_{c,t+\Delta}$, with zero-padding for out-of-bounds indices. EdgeSpot sets $\kappa=16$ and symmetric padding $p=8$.

**Temporal Self-Attention** (Eq. (5)–(8)): The convolution frontend output is reshaped into $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C\tau}$ (a $C\tau$-dimensional feature vector for each time step), which undergoes learnable linear projection to obtain Query, Key, and Value: $Q = XW_Q + 1\cdot b_Q^T$, with $K$ and $V$ similar, where $W_Q, W_K, W_V \in \mathbb{R}^{C\tau \times d}$, and the projection dimension $d=64$ is fixed by design and independent of $\tau$. Attention is computed along the time dimension: $A = \text{Softmax}(QK^T/\sqrt{d}) \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times T}$, $Z = AV \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d}$; $\sqrt{d}$ scaling is used to stabilize gradients under large dimensions, and softmax row-normalization ensures each row of $A$ constitutes a probability distribution over time steps. The paper emphasizes that this attention strictly operates only on the time axis, without any spatial/frequency mixing—consistent with the 1D temporal representation before attention. Subsequently, pointwise non-linearity $\tilde{Z} = \text{PReLU}(Z)$ is applied, followed by a lightweight 1D convolution head for temporal aggregation, yielding a sentence-level 64-dimensional embedding $e = g_\theta(\tilde{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$. The dimension $d=64$ is not chosen arbitrarily: it must match the 64-dimensional embedding space used by the training pipeline and downstream prototype inference.

**Design Logic of Training Objective and Distillation Strategy** (Eq. (9)): $L = L_{KD} + \lambda \cdot L_{SCAF}$, with $\lambda = 5e-5$. Here, $L_{KD}$ is the MSE loss between teacher and student embeddings, and $L_{SCAF}$ is the Sub-center ArcFace loss applied to the student embeddings. The teacher comes from the pipeline in Ref [6]: a pre-trained Wav2Vec2.0 encoder (using only the first 16 Transformer layers, per Table 2 footnote) provides frame-level features, and an attention-based down-projection head maps them to 64-dimensional embeddings, trained with SCAF loss. The two terms of this composite loss serve different purposes: the MSE distillation term transfers the geometric structure of the teacher's embedding space to the student as a whole, equivalent to the student imitating the teacher's "coordinate system" in a continuous space; the SCAF term imposes discriminative constraints with angular margins directly on the student's own output, ensuring the student is not just a dumb regressor of the teacher, but possesses discriminative geometry in the sense of metric learning itself. SCAF originates from the face recognition field; its sub-center design allows a class to have multiple clustering centers, providing natural tolerance for noisy labels and intra-class multimodality (different accents, speeds, and pronunciation styles for the same keyword)—this exactly corresponds to the reality in FS-KWS that "the same word does not have only one form in the embedding space." $\lambda$ is set very small at 5e-5, indicating that the main signal comes from embedding regression, while the discriminative term plays a regularization and shaping role. During inference, the average of $K$ registered sample embeddings forms the keyword prototype, and the test sample embedding is compared with the prototype, with FAR controlled by a threshold—following the prototype inference protocol of the previous work.

### Key Technical Innovation 1: Trainable PCEN Frontend, Fighting for Cross-Domain Generalization
The core reason for replacing static log compression with PCEN is given by the paper with a complete mechanistic chain: PCEN can (1) reduce loudness dependence (automatic gain control); (2) suppress stationary background noise (the gain baseline comes from causal IIR smoothing, removing slowly changing backgrounds); (3) enhance speech onset transients; (4) Gaussianize and decorrelate amplitude distributions—the latter is particularly beneficial for downstream discriminative models and cross-domain transfer. The primary motivation ("primarily") for introducing PCEN in EdgeSpot is to enhance cross-domain generalization: the training domain is MSWC, and the test domain is GSC; the recording conditions, channels, and speaker distributions of the two corpora differ. The frontend "whitens" the spectral magnitude before entering the backbone, effectively performing domain alignment first. The paper also provides initial ablation evidence: adding only PCEN without changing any other structure improves performance on the cross-domain test set (GSC), which the authors attribute to PCEN's Gaussianization and whitening effects (Ref [14]). This is also why PCEN is placed before the backbone rather than after—normalization must occur at the first step of feature extraction to affect the entire downstream process.

### Key Technical Innovation 2: Fused BC-ResBlock, "Inverse Optimization" of Depthwise Separability in Early Stages
This innovation seems counter-intuitive at first glance: depthwise separable convolution (DW+PW) is clearly a standard method for saving compute, yet EdgeSpot replaces it with standard convolution in the early blocks. The motivation is directly borrowed from the findings of EfficientNetV2: in the earliest stages of the network, replacing the depthwise separable stack with a single standard convolution is beneficial. The structure of the original BC-ResBlock (Fig. 2(a)) is: 3×1 frequency depthwise convolution (with sub-spectral normalization, averaging activation along the frequency dimension) → temporal depthwise convolution → 1×1 pointwise convolution (broadcasting temporal features back to 2D) → auxiliary 2D residual connection. The Fused BC-ResBlock (Fig. 2(b)) replaces the last two layers (temporal DW convolution + 1×1 convolution) with a single standard temporal convolution, maintaining the same kernel, stride, and dilation rate in configuration, with output channels aligned with the original 1×1 projection; the frequency branch, frequency averaging and broadcasting, and auxiliary 2D residual connections remain unchanged.

Why dare to do this and only in the early stages? The paper gives three layers of reasons: First, fusing only in the earliest stages captures the accuracy gains observed in EfficientNetV2, while avoiding performance regression when "fusing all stages"; Second, the number of channels in the early stages is small, and the fused operation is a 1D operation on the temporal branch, resulting in minimal incremental parameters and computation; Third, and more engineering-oriented—the "simplification of the separable stack improves optimization in practice." That is, reducing the number of stacked layers is not just for convenience, but also makes training more stable. This is a typical case where "textbook rules (using DW convolution to save compute) do not hold in specific locations": early layers have few channels and shallow representations, and the optimization difficulties brought by stacking exceed the compute saved.

### Key Technical Innovation 3: Lightweight Temporal Self-Attention Head + Convolutional Relative Position Encoding
Adding attention after a CNN backbone, EdgeSpot implements "lightweight" down to every detail. Why does attention only perform temporal single-head? Because the convolution frontend has already compressed the frequency dimension to 1 at the end (conv2d(1×1) outputs 32×101), the natural input to attention is a 1D temporal sequence; KWS speech is very short (1 second, 101 frames), so the T×T attention matrix is only 101×101, with a completely acceptable computational cost, yet it can model long-range temporal dependencies that dilated convolutions struggle to cover. Why use convolutional relative position encoding instead of absolute position encoding or standard learnable position tables? The residual depthwise convolution design in Eq. (3) maintains translation equivariance, allowing each channel to learn compact local temporal cues (such as onset/offset transients) gently, providing conditional information for attention while avoiding the parameter count of an explicit T×T position bias table. Why is the projection dimension $d=64$ decoupled from $\tau$? Because the dimension of the embedding space is locked by the training pipeline (the teacher is also 64-dimensional) and the prototype inference protocol, independent of model width scaling; this means that even the smallest $\tau=1$ model outputs a 64-dimensional embedding of the same specification, making the entire registration/inference protocol completely unified across different model scales. Finally, a shallow Conv1D head (conv1d with kernel length 1 in Table 1) is used for temporal aggregation to obtain the sentence-level embedding, rather than the common mean pooling—retaining a bit of learnable aggregation flexibility.

### Technical Differences with Existing Methods
Compared to the previous work [6] (same team, same teacher, same distillation protocol), the differences in EdgeSpot are entirely on the student side backbone: ResNet15 is replaced by the BC-ResNet base, superimposed with the trio of PCEN, early fusion, and temporal attention, reducing parameters from 480k to 128.3k and MACs from 235M to 29.4M (Table 2), while cross-domain GSC 10-shot accuracy actually increases (75.4% → 82.0%, ACC@1%). Compared to the original BC-ResNet [10], EdgeSpot transforms it from a fixed-vocabulary classifier into an embedding extractor (replacing the end with an SDPA head outputting 64-dimensional embeddings) and trains it using distillation + metric loss. Compared to the generic ResNet backbones in Refs [1]-[4], EdgeSpot is the first work to systematically transplant the "fixed-vocabulary efficient architecture family" into the FS-KWS embedding paradigm. A particularly commendable methodological detail is the fairness design: the paper does not directly compare with FS-KWS numbers from others, but re-trains BC-ResNet using the exact same FS-KWS training method and prototype inference protocol (replacing the end classifier with a 64-dimensional embedding head to be compatible with the distillation setup), ensuring that the gains from the "trio modification" are strictly separated from the gains from the "training protocol."

## Experimental Results

### Datasets Used and Their Scales
Training uses the English training set of MSWC (Multilingual Spoken Words Corpus): 5.5 million 1-second samples, 39,000 different words. Testing uses two sets (Section 2.2 of the paper): the MSWC test set, 680k samples, 38,090 words, belonging to in-domain testing; and GSC (Google Speech Commands), 100k 1-second audio clips, 35 short commands, belonging to cross-domain testing—the training and testing distributions are completely different, specifically used to stress-test generalization. The input for the student model is a 40-band Mel spectrogram converted from 16 kHz waveforms via centered STFT, resulting in a 40×101 frequency-time map. Regarding the evaluation protocol: on GSC, 100 random trials are conducted, each registering 11 target keywords (including silence) with K-shot and adversarial against the remaining 25 words; on MSWC, 3 independent runs are conducted, each containing 100k positive and 100k negative examples. Training details: 40 epochs, Adam optimizer, weight decay 4e-5, cosine learning rate schedule, linear warmup to peak learning rate 1e-3 for the first 5 epochs; SpecAugment is tied to model width—the smallest model with $\tau=1$ uses none (including time warping), while $\tau \in \{2,3,4\}$ enables it, with time warping factors uniformly sampled from [0.9,1.1], frequency mask width $F=6$, and time mask width $T=8$. The motivation for this "augmentation intensity scales with capacity" approach is direct: the smallest model has too little capacity to withstand strong augmentation.

### Definition and Rationale for Evaluation Metrics
On MSWC, DET@X (Detection Rate at FAR X, reporting DET@1% and DET@5%) and AUROC are used; on GSC, ACC@1%, ACC@5% (accuracy at fixed 1%/5% FAR) and AUC are used. Choosing detection rate at fixed low FAR as the primary metric is determined by the nature of the KWS task: devices listen all day long, and every false wake-up has real user experience and power consumption costs, so the engineering-meaningful question is never "how high is the average accuracy," but "how many true wake-ups can be caught when FAR is strictly capped at 1%." AUROC/AUC aggregates ranking quality across all thresholds, supplementing information from a single operating point. All cells report mean and standard deviation for 1-shot/10-shot (Table 2), which is important—performance variance in few-shot registration is extremely large, and reporting only the mean would severely overestimate stability.

### Detailed Comparison with Baseline Methods and SOTA
Table 2 contains all main results. Read it in three layers:

**Head-to-Head at Same Scale (EdgeSpot-k vs BC-ResNet-k, verifying the value of the trio)**: EdgeSpot systematically wins at every scale, and the gains are largest at low FAR and 1-shot—this exactly hits the paper's design goals. Most obvious on cross-domain GSC: 1-shot ACC@1% improvements across scales are 14.1→17.2 (k=1), 25.8→29.2 (k=2), 36.8→41.1 (k=3), 44.5→51.8 (k=4), with the largest scale showing a 7.3 percentage point increase (relative increase of about 16%); 10-shot ACC@1% increases from 73.7→82.0 (k=4, i.e., the number highlighted in the abstract). The trend of larger relative gains for smaller scales is also noteworthy: for k=1, the 1-shot ACC@1% relative increase is about 22% (14.1→17.2), indicating that the trio modification is almost a "lifeline" for capacity-constrained small models—PCEN whitens at the frontend to free up discriminative budget, and attention at the end compensates for the small backbone's lack of long-range modeling capability. Gains on in-domain MSWC are relatively smaller (k=4 1-shot DET@1% 87.1→87.8, 10-shot 95.5→95.7), indicating that the in-domain baseline is already basically saturated by the backbone, and the value of the modification lies mainly in difficult working points (low FAR, few-shot, cross-domain). In terms of cost, compared to BC-ResNet-4, EdgeSpot-4's MACs increase from 24.1M to 29.4M (approx. +22%), and parameters from 96.6k to 128.3k (approx. +33%), trading 20-30% compute for over 7 points of accuracy at cross-domain low FAR, which is a worthwhile transaction within edge budgets. An honest detail: on GSC 1-shot AUC, EdgeSpot-3 (73.2) is slightly lower than BC-ResNet-3 (73.7) by 0.5, the only point in the entire table where the baseline surpasses EdgeSpot; the paper's statement of "improvement at all scales" should be understood as referring to the low-FAR primary metrics.

**Head-to-Head with Previous Student Model (Verifying Edge Efficiency)**: EdgeSpot-4 exceeds ResNet15 in almost all metrics (MSWC 10-shot DET@1% 95.7 vs 96.1 slightly lower; GSC all metrics surpassed, e.g., 10-shot ACC@1% 82.0 vs 75.4), while requiring about 8 times fewer MACs (29.4M vs 235M) and about 3.7 times fewer parameters (128.3k vs 480k).

**Head-to-Head with Teacher (Verifying Distillation Ceiling)**: The teacher (Wav2Vec2.0 first 16 layers + down-projection module) requires 63.3G MACs and 217.8M parameters, approximately 2150 times the compute of EdgeSpot-4. At 10-shot, EdgeSpot-4 is already close to the teacher, even surpassing it at loose working points on GSC (ACC@5% 87.4 vs 85.2, AUC 91.9 vs 90.9); performance at loose working points on MSWC is "substantially saturated" relative to the teacher (DET@5% both 99.1). In the most challenging 1-shot scenario, the teacher is still the strongest (GSC ACC@1% 65.1 vs 51.8), but EdgeSpot-4 narrows the gap relative to BC-ResNet-4 by 7.3 percentage points; the paper's interpretation is that distillation + SCAF allows EdgeSpot to recover most of the teacher's discriminability under strict FAR, while maintaining an edge-friendly resource footprint.

### Findings from Ablation Experiments
This is the weakest part of this paper. The only ablation evidence reported in the main text is qualitative: initial ablation shows that "adding only PCEN without changing other structures" improves performance on the cross-domain test set (GSC), attributed to PCEN's Gaussianization and whitening effects. Apart from this, **the paper does not report** any ablation table for individual modules—the independent contributions of the four components (PCEN, Fused BC-ResBlock, relative position encoding, SDPA) and their combined effects are not given separately; sensitivity analysis for design choices such as different $\kappa$ (position encoding kernel length), different numbers of attention heads, and PCEN parameter sharing vs. per-channel is also not reported. Readers can only indirectly infer the combined contribution of the trio through the overall difference between "EdgeSpot-k vs BC-ResNet-k retrained with the same protocol." Fairly speaking, the authors controlled training pipeline variables by retraining the baseline with the same protocol (which itself is a kind of "training protocol ablation"), but the absence of architecture-level ablation makes it impossible to answer "how many points did each component contribute."

## Main Contributions

1. **Systematically introducing the fixed-vocabulary efficiency champion into FS-KWS**: First transforms the BC-ResNet backbone into a few-shot embedding extractor, and re-trains BC-ResNet using the exact same FS-KWS training method and prototype inference protocol as the baseline for fair comparison, proving that the gains come from architecture modification (rather than training protocol).
2. **Three targeted edge-friendly modifications**: Trainable PCEN frontend (cross-domain whitening), early block temporal fusion (borrowing EfficientNetV2 early fusion principles, improving optimization), and fixed $d=64$ lightweight temporal self-attention head (including convolutional relative position encoding), with the overhead of each module deliberately kept minimal (PCEN uses channel-shared parameters, fusion only in the first two stages, attention only temporal single-head).
3. **Substantial accuracy breakthrough in cross-domain few-shot**: Raises accuracy from 73.7% to 82.0% on GSC cross-domain 10-shot at 1% FAR (Table 2), comprehensively surpassing ResNet15 requiring 235M MACs and 480k parameters with only 29.4M MACs and 128.3k parameters, and approaching or even surpassing the self-supervised teacher requiring 63.3G MACs and 217.8M parameters at loose working points.
4. **Complete reproducible details**: From data scale, training hyperparameters (40 epochs, Adam, cosine schedule, warmup, SpecAugment tied to width configuration) to evaluation protocol (100 trials on GSC, 3 runs × 100k positive/negative examples on MSWC) are all provided, offering a clean comparison baseline for subsequent work.

## Limitations and Future Work

### Technical Limitations of the Method

- **Significant gap with the teacher in 1-shot scenarios**: On GSC 1-shot ACC@1%, EdgeSpot-4 is 51.8%, while the teacher is 65.1%, a difference of 13.3 percentage points (Table 2). When registering with a single sample, embedding quality is extremely sensitive to intra-class variations, exposing the capacity bottleneck of small models most fully—the trio alleviates but does not solve this problem.
- **High variance in cross-domain 1-shot**: The standard deviation for GSC 1-shot is as high as ±8.1 (EdgeSpot-4 ACC@1%, Table 2), meaning performance fluctuations across different registered samples can reach ten-plus points, raising doubts about the stability of actual user experience; this is more a difficulty inherent to the FS-KWS task itself, but the paper does not provide variance control methods (such as multiple registrations taking the best, or prototype calibration).
- **Streaming adaptation of attention and PCEN requires additional work**: SDPA is global T×T attention (101×101), and PCEN contains causal IIR smoothing (naturally causal, but state needs to be maintained across frames); this paper evaluates offline on 1-second full sentences, and does not report performance and overhead for streaming/low-latency versions, whereas real wake-word devices operate in a frame-by-frame streaming manner.
- **PCEN parameter channel sharing is a capacity compromise**: $\alpha, r, \delta, s$ are all channel-shared, saving parameters to the maximum extent, but also abandoning the ability to adapt per-channel to the statistical characteristics of different frequency bands; the noise characteristics of the 40 Mel bands differ significantly, and the cost of this compromise is not quantified by ablation.
- **Boundaries of early fusion rely on heuristics**: Fusing only in the first two stages is a heuristic borrowed from EfficientNetV2; whether fusing in the third and fourth stages would definitely cause regression is not supported by the paper's own data (ablation is missing).

### Shortcomings in Experimental Design

- **No architecture-level ablation table**: As mentioned above, the independent contributions of the four new components are not quantified or reported by the paper; the initial ablation of "adding only PCEN" only gives qualitative conclusions without specific numbers.
- **No real hardware validation**: The paper only reports MACs and parameter counts, and does not report measured latency, memory usage, or power consumption on any MCU/DSP; 29.4M MACs sounds edge-friendly, but the actual cost of SDPA's T×T matrix multiplication and softmax on MCUs without accelerators is heterogeneous to CNN layers, and the lack of real measurements is a hard flaw.
- **Narrow baseline coverage**: Table 2 only compares with the authors' own teacher, their own ResNet15, and the retrained BC-ResNet family, without including same-protocol numbers for third-party FS-KWS methods such as Refs [1][2][3][4]; while understandable (protocols are not unified), this limits external comparability.
- **Single language and domain coverage**: Although MSWC itself is a multilingual corpus, training and testing only use English; cross-lingual generalization (which Ref [1] of FS-KWS literature explicitly aims for as "any language") is not tested.
- **No long speech/multi-word continuous scenarios**: All evaluations are based on 1-second truncated clips; detection in continuous audio streams (concatenation artifacts, keywords crossing window boundaries) is not reported by the paper.

### Possible Directions for Future Improvement

- **Complete architecture ablation and streaming implementation**: Quantify the independent contribution of each component, and provide a streaming alternative for SDPA (restricted window attention or linear attention) and an edge-side deployment scheme for PCEN state maintenance.
- **Strengthen 1-shot registration**: Introduce registration-side augmentation (e.g., multi-prototype modeling of embeddings for registered samples—the sub-center idea of SCAF can be reversed and applied to the prototype side), and test-time adaptation (referencing directions like ImKWS) to reduce single-sample variance.
- **Overlay quantization compression**: Models with 128.3k parameters are naturally suitable for orthogonal overlay with low-bit quantization (int8/int4), further approaching MCU-level budgets; this is not explored in the paper.
- **Multilingual and speaker adaptation**: Utilize the multilingual attributes of MSWC to verify cross-lingual FS-KWS, and combine with speaker verification for personalized registration (there is already related work in the literature pool).
- **Mining the potential of larger teachers**: The teacher only used the first 16 layers of Wav2Vec2.0; whether deeper teachers or multi-teacher distillation continue to raise the student's upper limit is a question worth verifying.
