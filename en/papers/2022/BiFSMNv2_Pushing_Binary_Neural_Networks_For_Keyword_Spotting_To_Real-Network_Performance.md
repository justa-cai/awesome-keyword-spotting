## Problem Statement

### Problem Background and Domain Pain Points
Keyword Spotting (KWS) is a persistent voice entry point for edge devices such as smart speakers and wearables: devices remain in sleep mode, relying on continuous listening for wake-word triggers. This creates a structural contradiction—DNN-based KWS models (FSMN, cFSMN, Deep-FSMN, etc.) offer good accuracy but have high computational and storage overheads, while the edge devices hosting them are resource-constrained and must operate 24/7 in standby mode. Lightweight architectures such as cFSMN, DC-CNN, and BC-ResNet compress parameters and computations but remain floating-point networks, leaving significant room for compression in terms of bit-width (as stated in the paper's introduction).

Network binarization is an extreme form of quantization: both weights and activations are compressed to 1-bit, theoretically achieving a 32x compression in storage and replacing floating-point multiply-accumulate operations with XNOR and popcount bitwise operations. Literature [36] (XNOR-Net) provides an upper limit for binarized convolution units of 32.00x theoretical compression and 62.27x acceleration; this paper further estimates a theoretical reduction of 64x in single-layer FLOPs (Section 3.E, where multiplication of 1-bit weights and activations is折算 to mn/64 FLOPs on a 64-bit CPU). For always-on listening KWS, savings of this magnitude directly determine deployability.

However, binarization has historically performed poorly in KWS. The core pain point is that constraining both weights and activations to the minimal value space of $\{-1, +1\}$ severely limits the model's representational capacity; simultaneously, high discretization makes optimization difficult. Speech time-frequency representations are filled with fine-grained structures—transient bursts of consonants, energy concentration of vowel formants—compressing these continuous values into two discrete values causes information loss that is more fatal than edge contour loss in image tasks. As a result, directly applying existing binarization methods to KWS leads to a significant drop in accuracy (specific numbers see next section). Additionally, there are two engineering pain points: first, existing KWS architectures (whether full-precision or binarized) have fixed model scales and topologies, unable to dynamically adjust the accuracy-efficiency trade-off at runtime based on resource budgets (wake mode vs. power-saving mode), whereas actual device energy budgets drift with modes—high-load devices need KWS to yield for power saving, while idle devices can run more accurately; second, existing binarization deployment frameworks (daBNN, Bolt) achieve overall inference efficiency on real hardware far below theoretical limits, meaning the "64x FLOPs reduction per layer" calculated in the paper often fails to materialize on edge devices.

### Specific Deficiencies of Existing Methods
- **General binarization algorithms suffer accuracy collapse in KWS**. Paper Table III provides systematic evidence: performing 1-bit binarization on 8-block Deep-FSMN, DoReFa achieves only 66.42%, BNN 68.84%, XNOR 82.74%, IR-Net 86.81%, FDA 88.71% on Speech Commands V1-12, while full-precision is 97.93%. The strongest ReActNet achieves only 93.96%. That is, mature binarization methods in the visual domain generally drop 4~31 percentage points when moved to KWS.
- **Directly binarizing existing KWS architectures carries extremely high risk**. Table IV shows: MHAtt-RNN drops to 56.45%~65.53% after binarization, BC-ResNet-8 to 56.45%~66.51%, VGG19bn to 56.45%~66.10%, a huge gap from their full-precision versions (94%~97.8%). This indicates that attention/RNN-type structures are inherently unfriendly to binarization, necessitating architectures specifically designed for binarization.
- **Issues left by the first-generation BiFSMN (same team, IJCAI-22)**. The first-generation method (HED distillation + TBA prunable architecture) was the first KWS-specific binarized network, but the authors themselves noted "existing binarized networks still have a significant accuracy gap compared to their full-precision counterparts" (Introduction), with the best result on V1-12 being 95.03% (Table III), still 2.90 percentage points behind full-precision. The first-generation distillation scheme HED only enhances high frequencies, failing to address the systematic mismatch in the high/low-frequency information ratio between full-precision and binarized representations (visualized for the first time in this paper's Fig. 4, Fig. 8, see Methodology).
- **Forward/Reverse limitations of the binarizer itself** (summarized in Section 3.D of this paper): First, rigid forward parameterization—the sign function uses a fixed threshold of 0 to cut full-precision parameters into $\pm 1$, and the quantizer cannot adapt to the data distribution from a global level, relying only on slow, indirect element-wise updates; Second, reverse error accumulation—STE approximates the derivative of sign using a clip function, passing gradients through with a ratio of 1 within the $[-1,1]$ interval, while the true derivative of sign is 0. The approximation error ratio tends to infinity, and this unadjustable error leads to suboptimal models.
- **Deployment frameworks fail to realize theoretical acceleration**. BGEMM (binarized general matrix multiplication, composed of XNOR and bit-counting instructions) suffers from register allocation and instruction throughput limitations in frameworks like daBNN/Bolt, resulting in efficiency far below theoretical design (Section 3.E), so the "theoretical 64x" often reduces to just a few times on real machines.

### Key Challenges This Paper Aims to Solve
In one sentence: How to push 1-bit KWS networks to a level "almost lossless compared to full-precision networks," while truly realizing theoretical acceleration on real ARMv8 hardware. This breaks down into four sub-problems: (1) Restore the representational capacity of binarized computation units under 1-bit value constraints; (2) Support runtime accuracy-efficiency switching at the architecture level (one training, multiple deployment tiers) without accuracy loss due to prunability; (3) Bypass the frequency information ratio mismatch between full-precision teachers and binarized students at the training level; (4) Design bitwise operation kernels that utilize full registers and instruction throughput at the engineering level. All four are indispensable—focusing only on training algorithms without solving architecture, or only on architecture without deployment optimization, will not reach "real-network performance."

## Methodology

### Overall Architecture Design and Design Motivation
BiFSMNv2 is built upon the binarization of Deep-FSMN (Fig. 1). The reasons for choosing Deep-FSMN as the binarization host are two-fold (Section 3.A): (1) It is a purely feedforward structure, capturing sequence information by stacking memory blocks, without RNN cyclic dependencies, so binarization gradients do not repeatedly pass through quantization functions across time steps; (2) Skip connections between adjacent layers have been proven important for binarized network accuracy (citing [22] Bi-Real), allowing information to bypass blocks weakened by binarization and reach the next layer directly. The basic binarization framework follows convention: the first and last layers remain full-precision, while all intermediate convolution and linear units undergo 1-bit binarization, with non-linearity handled by PReLU.

The overall design is a "compensation chain": unit-level representation enhancement (DTA's dual-scale binarization) brings additional computational overhead, so the overall architecture reduces blocks (from 8 to 4) + skip-block prunable design to release speed potential; training uses FID to solve frequency mismatch; LPB further patches the forward/reverse limitations of the binarizer itself; finally, FBCK realizes acceleration on ARMv8. The four components are independently usable (the paper shows FID alone is effective on Deep-FSMN and first-gen BiFSMN in Table IV), but only combined do they form a complete solution.

### Mathematical Principles of Core Algorithms
**Basic Binarization Framework (Eq. 1-7)**. Forward pass uses deterministic sign function: $w^b = \mathrm{sign}(w)$, weights paired with magnitude factor $\alpha_w = \mathrm{mean}(|w|)$ (Eq. 3), performing $w \approx \alpha_w b_w$ to reduce quantization error; activations are binarized directly without scaling. Convolution/linear unit output is $o = \alpha_w (b_w \otimes b_a)$ (Eq. 4), where $\otimes$ denotes the bitwise inner product composed of XNOR and popcount. Reverse pass uses Straight-Through Estimator: gradients pass through according to the gradient of $\mathrm{sign}(x)$ in the $|x| \le 1$ interval, and are zeroed outside the interval (Eq. 2).

The $\ell$-th binarized memory block performs look-back (order $N1^\ell$, stride $s_1$) and look-ahead (order $N2^\ell$, stride $s_2$) 1D binarized convolutions on the linear projection sequence, then adds cross-block direct connection (Eq. 5):

$$\tilde{p}_t^{\ell} = \sum_{i=0}^{N1^\ell} \alpha_a^i (b_a^i \otimes p_{t-i\cdot s_1}) + \sum_{j=1}^{N2^\ell} \alpha_c^j (b_c^j \otimes p_{t+j\cdot s_2}) + H(\tilde{p}_t^{\ell-1} + p_t^{\ell})$$

The linear projection layer is $p_t^\ell = \alpha_V b_V^\ell \otimes b_{h_t}^\ell + b^\ell$ (Eq. 6), applying $f(\cdot) = \mathrm{BN} \cdot \mathrm{Nonlinear}$ (Eq. 7) when passing to the next hidden layer.

**Frequency Analysis Tools (Eq. 13-15)**. Uses 2D Haar wavelet transform to decompose intermediate representations into low-frequency and high-frequency components (horizontal/vertical edges are grouped into high-frequency), measuring the information content of each component with relative wavelet energy $E_j = \sum_k |C^j(k)|^2$, where $p_H = E_H / E_{\text{total}}$, $p_L = E_L / E_{\text{total}}$. This tool is the starting point for FID: components with larger energy contain more information, so comparing the $p_H$/$p_L$ of full-precision and binarized representations directly reveals frequency structure differences.

### Key Technical Innovation 1: Dual-Scale Prunable 1-bit Architecture (DTA)
**Dual-Scale Activation Binarization (Eq. 8-9)**. First scale proceeds normally $b_a^{\mathrm{1st}} = \mathrm{sign}(a)$; second scale performs another binarization on the residual $a - b_a^{\mathrm{1st}}$ and pairs it with magnitude factor $\alpha_a^{\mathrm{2nd}} = \mathrm{mean}(|a - b_a^{\mathrm{1st}}|)$ (calculated in real-time during inference). The convolution unit output becomes the sum of two terms:

$$o_{\mathrm{dual}} = \alpha_w (b_w \otimes b_a^{\mathrm{1st}}) + \alpha_w \alpha_a (b_w \otimes b_a^{\mathrm{2nd}})$$

The intuition is "coarse value + residual correction": the first scale captures sign information, while the second scale re-encodes the fine-grained information remaining in the quantization residual, significantly increasing the details a single binarized unit can represent. Fig. 2(b) provides a manually calculable example: activation vector $a = [-1.5, 0.7, 2, -0.3]$, first scale $b^{\mathrm{1st}} = [-1, 1, 1, -1]$, quantization residual $a - b^{\mathrm{1st}} = [-0.5, -0.3, 1, 0.7]$, second scale takes the sign of the residual to get $b^{\mathrm{2nd}} = [-1, -1, 1, 1]$; the two paths are respectively XNOR-inner-producted with binarized weights and then merged according to magnitude factors, effectively reconstructing the main structure of the original activation plus correction terms using two-bit resolution. The paper verifies with Fig. 7: sampling activations layer-by-layer on an 8-layer Deep-FSMN to calculate binarized MSE, DTA's quantization error is significantly lower than ordinary units at each layer.

**Why only dual-scale for activations, not weights** (Section 3.B, an important attribution experiment conclusion of this paper): The authors found that the bottleneck of binarized models lies mainly in activations rather than weights—weights already carry the $\alpha_w$ scaling factor, so information degradation is less severe; activations, which data flows through, are the main source of information loss. Therefore, dual-scale is added only to activations, weight storage does not increase by a single bit, and the storage overhead of binarized computation units remains exactly the same as before; magnitude factors are calculated in real-time only during inference and attached only to the second scale (two activations are not rescaled simultaneously), resulting in very small additional computational overhead.

**Why redesign the overall topology**. Dual-scale is good but not free: directly applying dual-scale quantization to an 8-layer Deep-FSMN results in inference FLOPs being approximately 1.5 times that of DoReFa-style binarization (numbers given in Section 3.B). The authors' approach is "stronger units $\rightarrow$ lighter overall": with improved unit representational capacity, fewer blocks are used to achieve equal or better accuracy, releasing acceleration potential from the overall architecture perspective.

**Skip-Block Prunable Design (Eq. 10-12)**. The base architecture $M$ containing $2N$ blocks (default 8) is changed to $M_{\mathrm{DTA}}$ containing $N$ blocks (default 4), skipping blocks by interval $\delta$ during inference: $M_{\mathrm{DTA}}(x;\delta) = \Phi^N_{\mathrm{dual}} \cdot \Phi^{N-1}_{\mathrm{dual}} \cdots \Phi^1_{\mathrm{dual}}(x)$, where the $\ell$-th block performs normal calculation when $\ell \in \{i\delta, i \in [1, N/\delta]\}$, otherwise degrading to an identity mapping (Eq. 12). $N=4$, $\delta=1/2/4$ correspond to three tiers: 1× (4 blocks), 0.5× (2 blocks), 0.25× (1 block) (Fig. 3), with BatchNorm parameters for each tier pre-allocated according to variants. This allows instant tier switching at runtime based on battery/CPU load, covering multi-level deployment with one training. Compared to the first-gen BiFSMN's TBA (truncating channels by width multiplier), DTA replaces "width pruning" with "depth skipping + unit enhancement," achieving higher accuracy even with half the number of blocks (comparison see Experimental Results section).

### Key Technical Innovation 2: Frequency Independent Distillation (FID)
**Motivation comes from a quantified empirical observation**. Fig. 4 visualizes intermediate representations of full-precision and binarized networks side-by-side: after binarization, the relative wavelet energy of high-frequency components rises significantly (values in the bottom right of the figure, high-frequency relative energy for three samples rises from 0.35/0.25/0.18 in full-precision to 0.48/0.38/0.27 in binarization, Fig. 4). Fig. 8 further statistics layer-by-layer, indicating this high/low-frequency mismatch exists universally across layers, not as an偶然 phenomenon. Mechanism explanation: binarized representations are naturally better at capturing high-frequency (edge-like) components, as feature values pinned to $\pm 1$ always fall at extrema on edge maps.

**Why ordinary distillation doesn't work** (key argument in Section 3.C): Directly making binarized features approximate full-precision features is equivalent to constraining two things simultaneously: "content of high/low frequencies individually" and "ratio between high/low frequencies." Since the two types of representations capture frequencies with fundamentally different capabilities, the ratio gap is hard to bridge through training, and this persistent mismatch significantly drags down training performance. First-gen HED only enhances the high-frequency side, similarly failing to decouple the ratio.

**Solution**. Perform Haar wavelet decomposition on intermediate representations of both full-precision teacher and binarized student, then reconstruct pure high-frequency and pure low-frequency representations via inverse wavelet transform (IWT) (Eq. 16), and then independently distill the two components in an attention form (L2 normalized then matched, following [28] Bi-Real) (Eq. 17):

$$L_{\mathrm{FID}} = \sum_{\ell=1}^{N} \left( \left\| \frac{R_H^\ell}{\|R_H^\ell\|_2} - \frac{\hat{R}_H^\ell}{\|\hat{R}_H^\ell\|_2} \right\|^2 + \left\| \frac{R_L^\ell}{\|R_L^\ell\|_2} - \frac{\hat{R}_L^\ell}{\|\hat{R}_L^\ell\|_2} \right\|^2 \right)$$

After frequency decoupling, content matching is no longer polluted by ratio mismatch. The loss adopts an attention form (L2 normalize teacher and student features first, then compare difference) rather than raw L2 distance, for reasons consistent with [28]: binarized student and full-precision teacher feature magnitudes are naturally on different scales; after normalization, matching focuses on directional structure, preventing the student from wasting capacity on matching magnitudes. For the prunable architecture, there is also **Uniform Layer Mapping** (Eq. 18): teacher's $i\delta$-th layer aligns with student's $i\delta$-th layer (after skipping) for distillation, allowing students at each $\delta$ tier to receive layer-by-layer supervision from the teacher; correspondingly, BatchNorm statistics for each tier are pre-allocated according to variants (explanation after Eq. 12), avoiding distribution misalignment caused by the 0.5×/0.25× tiers套用 full-network normalization parameters. Total loss (Eq. 19) is weighted by compression ratio for each tier: $L_{\mathrm{tot}} = \sum_\delta \frac{1}{2^{\delta-1}} (L_{\mathrm{CE}}^\delta + \gamma L_{\mathrm{FID}}^\delta)$, $\gamma=0.01$ (more complete tiers have larger weights: 1× weight 1, 0.5× weight 1/2, 0.25× weight 1/4), gradients for each tier are accumulated and jointly update weights during backpropagation (Algorithm 1 provides the complete training process: forward pass teacher first to get high/low frequency representations, then forward pass student sub-networks for each $\delta \in \{1, 2, \ldots, \log_2 N\}$, calculate distillation loss and cross-entropy loss, finally merge and descend).

### Key Technical Innovation 3: Learnable Propagation Binarizer (LPB)
Addressing the two major limitations of the binarizer summarized in the Problem Statement, LPB makes both the forward threshold and reverse gradient shape learnable parameters (Eq. 20-21):

$$\mathrm{LPB}(x) = \mathrm{sign}(x - \theta), \qquad \frac{\partial\, \mathrm{LPB}(x)}{\partial x} \approx \begin{cases} r \cdot g_x, & |x - \theta| \le r \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

The learnable forward threshold $\theta$ acts as a "mean shift": the quantization cut point is no longer pinned at 0, it can adapt to the distribution from a global level, enriching binarization diversity—solving the rigidity of "only relying on indirect element-wise updates." The reverse introduces proportion parameter $r$ to adjust the gradient proportion of the approximation function, directly compressing the approximation error that tends to infinity between clip and the true derivative of sign. Since the improvement occurs only within the binarizer, LPB can be plugged into any convolution and linear unit indiscriminately, with zero additional computational burden during inference.

### Key Technical Innovation 4: Fast Bitwise Operation Kernel (FBCK)
The theoretical 64x single-layer acceleration hits a bottleneck at the instruction-level execution efficiency of BGEMM on real machines: frameworks like daBNN use EOR (XOR) and CNT (bit counting) instructions on ARMv8, but register utilization and instruction throughput are not fully utilized. The core of FBCK (Fig. 6) is a register five-partition reallocation strategy tailored for ARMv8-A: Partition A occupies 4 registers to store one input (weight or activation), with each register packing the same input 8 times; B occupies 2 registers to store the other input, with each register holding 8 different inputs; C occupies 8 registers to hold intermediate results (INT8) of EOR+CNT; D occupies 8 registers for output accumulation within a single loop (INT8); E occupies 8 registers to hold final results (INT16). Inputs are packed as INT16. Execution first uses A's registers with one of B's registers to perform EOR and CNT, obtaining 32 INT8 results in C, then ADD accumulates into D, repeating for the other register of B; after 16 loops, uses long instruction ADALP to widen INT8 in D and accumulate into INT16 in E. The effect is that registers have almost no idle bits during computation, and instruction throughput is maximized (Section 3.E).

### Technical Differences with Existing Methods
- **Four upgrades over first-gen BiFSMN** (explicitly listed by authors in Introduction): (1) Architecture: first-gen TBA truncates channels by width multiplier, v2's DTA uses dual-scale unit enhancement + skip-block pruning, halving block count, reducing FLOPs from 40.46M to 35.01M (1× tier, Table III) while accuracy increases; (2) Distillation: HED only enhances high frequencies, FID decomposes high/low frequency components and distills them independently, positively addressing ratio mismatch—Table IV shows FID comprehensively beats HED under the same architecture (94.08% vs 93.47% on Deep-FSMN, 95.42% vs 95.03% on first-gen BiFSMN); (3) Adds two components absent in first-gen: LPB binarizer and FBCK kernel; (4) Comparison scope greatly expanded: newly introduces SOTA binarization algorithms like XNOR++, ReActNet, ReCU, FDA, newly introduces binarization architectures like VGG19bn, BC-ResNet, MHAtt-RNN, and provides ultra-small BiFSMNv2S.
- **Against general binarization methods** (element-wise scaling of XNOR++, learnable shift generalized activation of ReActNet, dead weight reuse of ReCU, frequency domain approximation of FDA): These are task-agnostic binarizer or loss improvements; individually, none are sufficient to pull KWS binarization close to full-precision (highest 93.96% in Table III). The difference with BiFSMNv2 lies in the "architecture + training + binarizer + deployment kernel" four-in-one approach, and FID is built on the task-agnostic but previously unexplicitly handled observation that "binarized representations have a systematic frequency structure shift." Like FDA, it involves the frequency domain, but FDA learns frequency domain approximation to modify binarization operators, while FID decomposes representations and aligns them in the distillation target, operating at different levels.
- **Against multi-bit quantization**: Binarization is the extreme point of 1-bit, exchanging for the bitwise operation dividend of XNOR/popcount, at the cost of representational capacity collapse—this paper's entire method is essentially "buying back representational capacity at the minimum cost."

## Experimental Results

### Datasets Used and Their Scales
Google Speech Commands V1 and V2 (citing [51] Warden), evaluated at 12, 20, and 35 vocabulary scales (CLS 12 / CLS 20 / CLS 35), each setting tested once on both V1 and V2 datasets. The paper does not report scale information such as number of audio clips or total duration for the two datasets; this needs to be checked in the original dataset papers. The structure of the ultra-small model BiFSMNv2S is 3×3 convolution kernel, 12 channels at the front end, 32-dimensional backbone memory, 56-dimensional hidden layer.

### Definition and Rationale for Evaluation Metrics
- **Accuracy**: Classification accuracy, i.e., number of correct predictions divided by total predictions (Eq. 22). Rationale: follows the convention of existing KWS works [16][33], and this definition is universal for 12/20/35 classification tasks.
- **Computational Cost FLOPs**: Based on the口径 "multiplication of m-bit and n-bit parameters折算s to mn/64 FLOPs on a 64-bit CPU" (citing [22][78]), the FLOPs of a single binarized computation unit is recorded as 1/64 of its full-precision counterpart.
- **Deployment Metrics**: Parameter count, storage occupancy, single-inference latency, measured on real edge hardware Raspberry Pi 3B+ (1.2GHz 64-bit ARMv8 Cortex-A53); Table III latency uses daBNN deployment, Fig. 10 additionally compares Bolt and self-developed FBCK.
- **Training Configuration**: PyTorch implementation, SGD optimizer + cosine annealing learning rate decay to 0, all binarized models trained for 300 epochs (consistent with full-precision counterparts); comparison methods retain their original paper hyperparameters. BiFSMNv2 experimental configuration is 4 memory blocks, backbone memory size 128, hidden layer size 224.

### Findings from Ablation Experiments
Ablation is performed on SC-V1/V2-12 (Table I), verifying three components one by one:

- **Contribution of DTA**. Pure DTA (vanilla training, no distillation, no LPB) three tiers 95.09/94.75/92.99% (V1), **7.38** percentage points higher than vanilla binarization on Deep-FSMN (87.71%); with ordinary distillation, **5.46** percentage points higher than Deep-FSMN + distillation (90.02%). Fig. 7 shows DTA layer-by-layer activation quantization error (MSE) is significantly lower than ordinary binarized units; Fig. 9 training curves show joint training of multiple $\delta$ tiers facilitates optimization convergence, converging faster and more stably.
- **Contribution of FID**. On Deep-FSMN, FID (94.08%) is **4.06** percentage points higher than ordinary distillation (90.02%) and 0.85 higher than $\ell 2$-norm feature distillation (93.23%); on DTA architecture, FID (96.34%) is **0.86** higher than ordinary distillation (95.48%) and 2.72 higher than $\ell 2$-norm (93.62%), summarized by the paper text as "FID stably improves by approx 3% relative to $\ell 2$-norm distillation on both architectures." A notable failure case: $\ell 2$-norm distillation collapses directly to 56.80/60.89% on DTA's 0.5×/0.25× pruned tiers (Table I), while FID maintains 95.44% on the 0.25× tier thanks to uniform layer mapping—indicating ordinary feature distillation is incompatible with prunable architectures, and FID's layer alignment strategy is not just icing on the cake but a necessity.
- **Contribution of LPB**. Adding LPB on top of DTA+FID: V2-12 from 96.22% to 96.45% (+0.23, this is the number cited in the paper text), V1-12 from 96.34% to 96.42% (+0.08), with zero additional inference overhead. Interestingly, LPB's benefit varies with the strength of the base architecture: on pure Deep-FSMN, FID+LPB (96.21/96.22%) is 2.13/1.85 percentage points higher than FID (94.08/94.37%) (Table I), indicating that binarizer propagation quality is one of the main bottlenecks on weak architectures; on architectures where representational capacity has been strengthened by DTA, the binarizer's短板 is partially masked, and benefits narrow accordingly. This asymmetry also suggests LPB's "generality" is more reflected in its plug-and-play nature for any binarized unit and orthogonal stacking with other components, rather than bringing large improvements in every scenario.
- **Layer-by-layer efficiency anatomy (Table II, 8-layer Deep-FSMN full-precision vs 1-bit)**. Backbone single Block drops from 69.34M FLOPs to 3.82M, **single-block acceleration 18.1x**; Head Layer1.2 drops from 105.64M to 3.47M; while full-precision first layer Layer1.1 (14.08M) and classifier Layer3.1 (1.57M) remain unchanged—the overhead of non-binarized layers accounts for a small proportion, ensuring the feasibility of the overall ultra-high speed ratio. Meanwhile, Fig. 8 layer-by-layer wavelet entropy statistics confirm frequency mismatch exists universally across the network, providing direct evidence for the necessity of FID.

## Main Contributions
1. **DTA (Dual-Scale Prunable 1-bit Architecture)**: Restores the representational capacity of binarized computation units via residual-style second-scale binarization without increasing any weight storage, supporting a lightweight overall architecture of "block reduction + skip-blocks," achieving instant switching of three tiers 1×/0.5×/0.25× at runtime, covering multi-level resource budgets with one training.
2. **FID (Frequency Independent Distillation)**: First explicitly quantifies and handles the high/low-frequency information ratio mismatch between full-precision and binarized representations (Fig. 4/Fig. 8), using Haar wavelet decomposition + component-independent attention distillation + uniform layer mapping, significantly increasing the teacher knowledge utilization rate of binarized perception training, comprehensively outperforming first-gen HED and $\ell 2$-norm distillation under the same architecture (Table I/IV).
3. **LPB (Learnable Propagation Binarizer)**: Parameterizes both forward threshold and reverse gradient proportion, simultaneously fixing the two general defects of sign+STE: forward rigidity and reverse error accumulation, pluggable into any binarized unit with zero inference overhead.
4. **FBCK (Fast Bitwise Operation Kernel)**: Five-partition register allocation and INT16 packing, ADALP long instruction accumulation strategy tailored for ARMv8, fully utilizing BGEMM's registers and instruction throughput, realizing 20.2x storage saving and up to 25.1x acceleration on Raspberry Pi 3B+, significantly faster than daBNN and Bolt.
5. **Systematic Empirical Evidence**: Comprehensively surpasses 11 SOTA binarization algorithms and various binarization architectures on Speech Commands V1/V2 12/20/35-class tasks, reaching 96.42% on V1-12, only 1.51% away from full-precision, being the first work to push KWS binarized networks to near full-precision levels.

## Limitations and Future Work

### Technical Limitations of the Method
- **"Close to full-precision" holds most true only for 12-class tasks**. Calculated from Table III, the gap with full-precision for 20/35-class tasks is 2.06~2.91 percentage points, the 0.25× pruned tier (94.65% on V1-12) is 3.28 percentage points away from full-precision; ultra-small BiFSMNv2S is 5.49 percentage points away from its full-precision version (Table IV). The larger the vocabulary and the smaller the model, the more obvious the binarization gap, indicating that representational capacity recovery is not yet complete.
- **First and last layers are not binarized**. Layer1.1's 14.08M FLOPs are retained as-is (Table II), still occupying a considerable share in extreme compute-saving scenarios; mixed low-bit (2/4-bit) first/last layers are not explored in the paper.
- **Dual-scale activation's additional computation relies on real-time statistics**. $\alpha_a^{\mathrm{2nd}}$ requires per-step calculation during inference; the paper claims overhead is small but does not provide quantitative breakdown; and dual-scale only acts on activations, representational loss on the weight side is not handled.
- **FID requires full-precision teacher and fixed Haar wavelet basis**. Whether the choice of decomposition basis (Haar here) is optimal, and the sensitivity to changing wavelet bases, are not reported by the paper; the impact of teacher quality on final accuracy is also not ablated separately.

### Deficiencies in Experimental Design
- **Narrow data scope**: Only Google Speech Commands V1/V2 (1-second short word classification), dataset scale not reported by the paper; no evaluation on custom wake words, streaming frame-level detection, far-field/noise enhancement, which are closer to real wake scenarios.
- **Single metric**: Only uses classification accuracy (Eq. 22), does not report False Alarm Rate/False Rejection Rate (FAR/FRR) operating points most concerned by KWS deployment, nor measures power consumption/energy—energy consumption is as important as accuracy in always-on listening scenarios.
- **Single hardware**: Only measures speed on one ARMv8 device, Raspberry Pi 3B+; FBCK is deeply bound to ARMv8-A instruction set (EOR/CNT/ADALP), not verified on MCU, DSP, etc., which are more common deployment targets for KWS; no Pareto comparison with INT8 quantization schemes at the same accuracy-efficiency level.
- **Minor inconsistencies between numbers and narrative**: Introduction writes V1-12 as 96.34%, Table I/III and Section 4 text as 96.42%; text claims exceeding FDA by 9.61 percentage points, calculated from table is 7.71; text claims V1-20/35 improvements of 3.87/3.40 percentage points relative to SOTA, which does not align with the maximum gap calculated per competitor from the table (1.02/3.25). Individually, this does not affect the direction of conclusions, but citations should prioritize table numbers.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity not reported**: Distillation weight $\gamma=0.01$, LPB threshold $\theta$ initialization and proportion $r$ ranges, $\delta$ tier distribution, etc., have no sensitivity analysis; the anomalous phenomenon of $\ell 2$-norm collapsing on pruned tiers (56.80%) is only used as a control for FID, without deep attribution.

### Possible Future Improvement Directions
- Generalize the idea of Frequency Independent Distillation to larger vocabularies, streaming KWS, and other speech tasks, to verify whether "binarized representations bias towards high frequencies" is a universal law, and explore adaptive wavelet bases or learnable decomposition.
- Replace manual $\delta$ skip-block distribution with Neural Architecture Search to find better Pareto points on the accuracy-efficiency curve; combine with structured pruning and mixed precision (low-bit quantization of first/last layers) to further compress residual floating-point overhead.
- Port the FBCK approach to mainstream KWS target platforms like MCU/DSP (register-level optimization methodology is transferable, specific instructions need rewriting), and supplement with systematic evaluation of FAR/FRR and energy dimensions.
- Continue improving binarizer propagation quality along the LPB direction (e.g., richer learnable gradient shapes), and research noise-resistant training and domain adaptation for binarized KWS, responding to far-field real scenarios.
