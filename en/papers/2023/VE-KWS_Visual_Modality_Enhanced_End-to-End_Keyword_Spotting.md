## Problem Statement

### Problem Background and Domain Pain Points

Keyword Spotting (KWS, i.e., listening for wake words in continuous audio streams to trigger voice assistants) is the first entry point for voice interaction systems such as smart speakers, TVs, and wearable devices. The paper begins by pointing out: with the popularity of mobile and smart devices, hands-free voice experiences have made KWS a prerequisite component for triggering speech recognition, and its prediction accuracy directly determines the user experience of voice assistants (first paragraph of the original introduction). The engineering fact behind this statement is that KWS is a component that needs to run permanently year-round. Its two types of errors—missed wake-ups (failing to wake when it should) and false wake-ups (waking up when it shouldn't)—if either becomes excessive, will destroy user trust. Therefore, the system must maintain a fine balance between the two types of errors rather than pursuing a single metric unilaterally.

The scenario of this paper is anchored in the wake-word detection task of the MISP2021 challenge (Multimodal Information Based Speech Processing): a real-home living room TV scenario with far-field multi-channel audio and visual data. This scenario exposes three pain points. First, home noise is not traditional stationary background noise—the TV playing in the background is another person's speech, and the air conditioner provides low-frequency continuous interference. The target speaker's wake word and the interference source have highly overlapping spectra, making it difficult for pure audio methods to separate them (the original text points out that real home background noise makes it hard to estimate the speaker's direction of arrival and detect keywords using only the audio modality). Second, far-field conditions introduce reverberation and energy attenuation, so devices are equipped with six-channel far-field microphone arrays, making beamforming a standard solution. However, the prerequisite for beamforming is knowing the target speaker's Direction of Arrival (DOA), and DOA estimation itself is precisely the most unreliable in noise—this is a "chicken or egg" deadlock. Third, theoretically, the visual modality is unaffected by acoustic noise (lip movement information is independent of acoustic SNR), but in real scenarios, visual occlusions (side profiles, looking down, lighting changes) limit the performance of pure visual systems. Furthermore, public audio-visual KWS datasets were long missing; this direction only gained a standard testbed after MISP2021 released approximately 125 hours of data.

It is worth emphasizing the industrial background of this work: the paper is a joint effort between the ASLP Lab at Northwestern Polytechnical University and China Mobile Research Institute, targeting the real product constraints of operator smart hardware (smart TVs, home speakers). This also foreshadows the authors' proactive discussion of real-time rate and model size in the conclusion.

### Specific Deficiencies of Existing Methods

The paper divides existing work into two routes and points out their structural defects one by one.

**Route 1: Noise robustness of audio single-modality.** Existing studies have improved KWS performance in noise by introducing speech enhancement modules and improving network architectures, heavily relying on data augmentation and semi-supervised training. Specific achievements include: multi-look KWS (citing reference [7]) proposed the multi-view enhancement network MLENet to improve performance under noise and far-field conditions; ConvMixer (citing reference [8]) used a new convolutional encoder to focus on effective spatial information and performed curriculum multi-condition training based on Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR). The paper explicitly points out: despite these advances, **KWS in realistic environments with low SNR remains challenging** (original text: "KWS in realistic environments with a low SNR is still challenging"). Why does this route have a ceiling? Because TV noise and human speech are acoustically same-spectrum and same-source. The audio modality can only rely on statistical differences rather than physical differences to distinguish the target from interference. When the interference itself is speech, this distinction systematically fails at low SNR.

**Route 2: Rough fusion methods in audio-visual multi-modal KWS.** The visual modality was first introduced to KWS by Momeni et al. (reference [11], BMVC 2020 "Seeing wake words"), where the approach was to calculate similarity maps between features of different modalities and feed them into the network for prediction. After MISP2021, participating systems adopted two types of fusion: Cheng et al. (reference [13], DKU system) proposed Hierarchical Modality Aggregation (HMA), performing feature-level fusion of intermediate feature maps from different levels of single-modality encoders; Xu et al. (reference [14]) performed feature-level concatenation fusion and decision-level score averaging fusion. The common criticism of these methods in the paper is: **they all only combine information after "each modality has finished learning representations using its own exclusive network," meaning the relationships between modalities do not participate in their respective modeling processes** (original text: "mainly focus on learning multi-modal information by simply concatenating different modal features from their exclusive networks"). Why is this a substantive defect rather than just a wording issue? Because the reliability of the two modalities switches dynamically with the environment—audio is strong when quiet, vision is stable when noisy. Exclusive encoders for each modality "cannot see" the other during training, so the learned representations are not friendly to subsequent fusion. The fusion layer can only perform post-hoc remediation on representations that are already fixed and mutually unaware.

**The third completely ignored gap: The multi-channel speech enhancement stage does not use the visual modality.** The original text directly points out that "the use of the visual modality in the multi-channel speech enhancement process has not yet been explored." This is a more fundamental gap than backend fusion: the position of the lips in the frame is the spatial position of the sound source, which is exactly the physical prior needed most for beamforming. However, all previous audio-visual KWS systems treated visual information only as "another feature," and no one used it as a "spatial sensor" at the front end.

Additionally, there are practical difficulties in training: the MISP2021 training set has a severe imbalance between positive and negative samples (over 47,000 negative samples vs. over 5,000 positive samples, approximately 9:1, see Section 3.1 Data Composition), and there is an audio-video frame rate mismatch where video frame length is about 4 times that of audio frames (Section 3.3). These impose additional requirements for end-to-end joint training.

### Key Challenges to be Solved by This Paper

In summary, this paper aims to solve challenges at three levels simultaneously. First, **how to provide reliable directional priors for beamforming when audio DOA is unreliable**—the answer is lip region coordinates, but the granularity of visual coordinates is coarse (a far-field wide-angle camera with a 120-degree field of view can only be divided into 6 regions of 20 degrees each). How to convert this low-precision information into a feature form that is usable by the enhancement network and can be vetoed by the network if erroneous, rather than an irreversible hard decision. Second, **how to make the two modalities interact during their respective representation learning stages**, so that the complementary performance of relying on audio when quiet and vision as a fallback when noisy is internalized by the encoder, while using learnable second-order interactions (bilinear) to replace the limited expressive power of first-order concatenation. Third, **how to stably train randomly initialized fusion modules on top of pre-trained single-modality models**, and complete joint optimization under conditions of positive-negative sample imbalance and scarce audio-video pairing (via same-category video replacement augmentation). These three matters correspond to the paper's Visual-Assisted MVDR (Section 2.1), Cross-Attention plus Bilinear Fusion (Section 2.2), and Two-Stage Training Strategy (Sections 3.3, 3.4), respectively.

## Methodology

### Overall Architecture Design and Design Motivation

VE-KWS is essentially a two-layer architecture of "front-end physical-level enhancement + back-end semantic-level fusion," where the visual modality is injected into the system at two completely different levels.

**Front-end: Visual-Assisted MVDR (Original Fig. 1).** The pipeline is: extract lip ROI coordinates from video signals → Angle Feature Extractor (Beam-ID + STFT + Steering Vector Calculation + IPD Calculation) → Concatenate with the magnitude spectrum of the first channel along the frequency axis → Speech Enhancement Network (three-layer 256-dim LSTM + Fully Connected + Sigmoid, estimating masks) → Mask-based MVDR Beamformer (Covariance Estimation + Filter Coefficient Solving + iSTFT Reconstruction) → Output enhanced audio for subsequent KWS use. The six-channel far-field audio signal is the main data for this pipeline, with vision providing only one prior: "which 20-degree region the speaker is in."

**Back-end: Cross-Modal Attention Fusion Network (Original Fig. 2).** Dual-tower structure: Audio features (FBank blocks of 80 frames concatenated) and Visual features (112×112×3 lip region sequences) enter two **structurally identical** ResNet3D encoders (five blocks, channels sequentially 32, 64, 64, 128, 256), each followed by a Transformer layer (containing both self-attention and cross-attention modules), outputting enhanced single-modality representations Xa and Xv; the two are concatenated along the last dimension to obtain the joint representation J, which undergoes bilinear pooling to produce audio attention map Ca and visual attention map Cv, respectively. These are then fused via a residual-style fusion of "projection addition + ReLU + projection addition" to obtain X_j,a and X_j,v, which are finally concatenated into a fully connected layer to output a 0/1 decision on keyword existence.

The motivation behind this two-layer design is worth digging into. **Why is vision used twice, rather than just in the backend fusion?** Because the two levels of problems cannot be solved simultaneously by one module: the front end needs to solve "the signal itself is polluted" (noise and reverberation pollute the spectrum at the physical level, and no backend classifier can recover physically lost information from already polluted representations), while the back end needs to solve "representations are not complementary" (two clean modality representations also need an interaction mechanism to be dynamically weighted according to the environment). The front-end visual injection utilizes the **physical attribute** of vision (lip position is the sound source position), while the back end utilizes the **semantic attribute** of vision (lip movement content and speech content are redundant encodings of each other). The same camera data is squeezed for two orthogonal values in the system, which is the core design idea distinguishing this paper from all previous works.

Another architectural decision is **isomorphic encoders for both modalities** (original text: to maintain feature dimension consistency, different modalities use the same ResNet3D architecture). This is not laziness but a necessity: cross-attention requires the representations of the two modalities to be mutually usable as query/key, and bilinear pooling requires the two matrices to be multiplicable. Isomorphism ensures these interaction operators do not require additional adaptation layers. The choice of ResNet3D is based on its verified spatiotemporal dynamic modeling capabilities (proposed in reference [19], used for video action recognition in reference [20] and speaker recognition in reference [21]); lip movement is precisely a typical spatiotemporal pattern. For the audio side, the 80-frame concatenated FBank time-frequency block can naturally be fed into the same network as a single-channel "image," unifying audio and video inputs into "3D convolutions on time-block sequences."

### Mathematical Principles of Core Algorithms

**Angle Feature (AF).** Let i, j be microphone indices, and y_{t,f} be the frequency-domain observed signal. First, calculate the inter-channel phase difference IPD and the phase of the steering vector corresponding to the region center angle, then perform cosine consistency measurement (original formulas 1, 2):

$$IPD^{ij} = \angle y^{i}_{t,f} - \angle y^{j}_{t,f}, \qquad AF^{ij,\theta} = \cos\left(IPD^{ij} - \Delta\theta\right)$$

In plain language: IPD is the "phase difference of sound arriving at two microphones," physically determined by the sound source direction; Δθ is the "theoretically expected phase difference if the speaker were in the center direction of the θ-th region"; taking the cosine of the difference between the two yields a scalar in [-1, 1]—the closer to 1, the more consistent the observed phase difference is with the hypothesis "the speaker is in this region." The brilliance of this design lies in its being **soft evidence rather than hard decision**: the AF values for the six regions can be non-zero simultaneously, allowing the network to learn how to weigh them, rather than being forced to accept a potentially wrong beam.

**Mask Estimation and MVDR.** The enhancement network uses IRM (Ideal Ratio Mask, i.e., the ratio mask of clean speech to mixed signal spectrum) as the training target and Mean Squared Error as the loss. After obtaining speech/noise masks, the mask-weighted speech and noise covariance matrices are calculated according to formulas 3 and 4:

$$R^{S}_{f} = \frac{\sum_{t} m^{S}_{t,f}\, y_{t,f} y^{H}_{t,f}}{\sum_{t} m^{S}_{t,f}}, \qquad R^{N}_{f} = \frac{\sum_{t} m^{N}_{t,f}\, y_{t,f} y^{H}_{t,f}}{\sum_{t} m^{N}_{t,f}}$$

The MVDR filter coefficients are solved by formula 5: $w_f = \dfrac{(R^{N}_{f})^{-1} R^{S}_{f}}{\mathrm{tr}\left[(R^{N}_{f})^{-1} R^{S}_{f}\right]} u_f$, where tr(·) is the matrix trace, and u_f is the one-hot vector indicating the reference microphone; the final enhanced signal is $\hat{y}_{t,f} = w_f^{H} y_{t,f}$ (formula 6). Why use MVDR instead of directly using the masked spectrum as output? The distortionless constraint of MVDR (Minimum Variance Distortionless Response Beamformer) ensures that the gain of the signal in the target direction is always 1, suppressing interference without introducing spectral distortion—KWS is sensitive to spectral distortion (formant positions and consonant transients are key discriminators), and a "clean but distorted" signal is not necessarily better than a "slightly dirty but faithful" signal.

**Cross-Modal Fusion.** Let Xa, Xv be the enhanced single-modality representations output by the Transformer layer. First, concatenate them to get the joint representation J, then perform bilinear pooling for each modality (original formulas 7, 8):

$$C_a = \tanh\left(\frac{X_a^{T} W^{ja} J}{\sqrt{d}}\right), \qquad C_v = \tanh\left(\frac{X_v^{T} W^{jv} J}{\sqrt{d}}\right)$$

where W^{ja}, W^{jv} are the learnable projection matrices for each modality, √d scaling (borrowing the magnitude stabilization technique from scaled dot-product attention) prevents the inner product from exploding as dimensions increase, and tanh compresses the output to a bounded interval. The paper explicitly points out that Ca, Cv measure correlations that are "not only cross-modal but also include intra-modal" correlations—because J itself is a concatenation of two modalities, the inner product of the audio position with both the audio segment and the visual segment in J is performed simultaneously. Subsequently, using audio as an example, residual fusion is performed (formulas 9, 10):

$$H_a = \mathrm{ReLU}\left(W_a X_a + W_{ca} C_a^{T}\right), \qquad X_{j,a} = W_{ha} H_a + X_a$$

The bilinear term captures **second-order multiplicative interactions**: concatenation fusion can only allow subsequent fully connected layers to learn additive combinations themselves, whereas bilinear explicitly constructs "the product weighting of the i-th position of audio and the j-th position of the joint representation," offering stronger expressive power (bilinear models can be traced back to the style-content decomposition work by Tenenbaum and Freeman in 2000, reference [23]). The final residual connection X_j,a = W_ha H_a + X_a means that in the worst case, the fusion layer can degrade back to the single-modality representation itself—the identity mapping ensures training stability and guarantees that the system still retains single-modality performance if fusion fails. The visual side obtains X_j,v isomorphically, and the two are concatenated into a fully connected layer for keyword existence prediction.

### Key Technical Innovation 1: Visual-Assisted MVDR Beamforming

This innovation solves the "chicken or egg" deadlock mentioned in the introduction: beamforming requires DOA, but audio DOA estimation is unreliable in home noise (especially TV playing human speech). The answer here is to bypass audio estimation and read the speaker's position directly from the video's lip ROI coordinates.

Specific implementation (Section 3.2): The far-field wide-angle camera has a 120-degree field of view, dividing video frames horizontally into 6 regions, each corresponding to a 20-degree beam; based on the ROI provided with the MISP dataset (cropped by internal tools of the challenge organizers), obtain the region number to which the lip coordinates belong, then calculate the steering vector for the center angle θ of that region according to the microphone array topology. The input to the enhancement network is the concatenation of "the magnitude spectrum of the first channel + angle features of three microphone pairs" along the frequency axis, where IPD is taken from three microphone pairs with indices (1,4), (2,5), (3,6); STFT frame length is 32 ms, frame shift is 16 ms. The network is trained for 20 epochs with the Adam optimizer, initial learning rate 0.001, halved if the development set shows no improvement.

Two easily overlooked but crucial sub-designs. **First, soft prior rather than hard selection.** The paper specifically compares fixed beamforming (BF: executing fixed beamforming after estimating the direction of arrival from lip coordinates) with this method in the ablation study (Table 1): BF drops the total score on the Eval set from 14.39 to 13.70 (Table 1), but on the Dev set, the FRR actually worsens from 6.89 to 8.51—once a hard beam is selected incorrectly (lip region judgment error, or speaker happens to be at the region boundary), it is irreversible, and fixed beamforming cannot adapt to the environment; whereas angle features are just one evidence stream concatenated into the network input, allowing the network to learn "when to trust it." This method further reduces the score on Eval to 12.64 (Table 1), a 7.59% reduction relative to fixed beamforming (a 24.12% reduction on the Dev side, Section 3.5 of the original text). **Second, the source of training supervision signals.** The enhancement network takes far-field data as input and **near-field data as the target** (Section 3.2). Why not use clean recordings as the target? Because the data is recorded synchronously in real homes, where noise is present on-site; the near-field microphone (closer to the speaker, much higher SNR) is the best approximation available under realistic conditions—this is equivalent to a "pseudo-clean" supervision without manual alignment, and also realizes the dividend of the MISP multi-device synchronous recording design.

### Key Technical Innovation 2: Cross-Modal Attention and Bilinear Pooling Fusion

The starting point of the argument in Section 2.2.2 is an asymmetric reliability judgment (original text): in quiet environments, the audio modality is more reliable than vision (audio systems generally outperform visual systems), while in noisy environments, audio may be very poor while vision is unaffected by acoustic noise. This leads to the design principle for the fusion mechanism—**it cannot be unidirectional or statically weighted; it must allow the two modalities to see each other during their respective modeling stages.**

In implementation, the Transformer layer for each modality contains two attention modules: self-attention models **intra-modal** dependencies (relationships between time steps within the same modality), while cross-attention captures **inter-modal** relationships by using the **representation of the other modality as the query vector** (original text: self-attention models intra-modal dependencies, cross-attention captures inter-modal relationships using other modality representations as queries). Note that this is bidirectional and symmetric: the audio tower enhances audio representations using vision as the query, and the vision tower enhances vision representations using audio as the query—insisting on bidirectionality is necessary because reliability switching is bidirectional (vision falls back for audio when entering noise, audio dominates vision when returning to quiet); unidirectional enhancement would inject errors into the "target modality" when the "source modality" fails.

On top of the enhanced representations produced by cross-attention, bilinear pooling completes explicit fusion (mathematical principles section details formulas 7 to 10). The direct opponents of this fusion structure are two classic approaches: feature-level concatenation (concatenating single-modality encoder outputs into a fully connected layer) and decision-level averaging (averaging scores from each single-modality system). The results in Table 3 prove that this combination of "cross-attention + bilinear + residual projection" is significantly stronger: on the Dev set, the score reduction relative to decision fusion is 44.31%; on the Eval set, it is 33.10% (original Section 3.5, calculated from Table 3's 6.50→3.62 and 8.58→5.74).

Details of input construction also reflect engineering considerations (Section 3.3): the audio side takes 80-dim log-Mel FBank (frame length 25 ms, frame shift 10 ms). Since the video frame length is about 4 times that of the audio frame, a window of 80 frames with a stride of 4 is slid along the time axis to cut out feature block sequences, with shape (T, 80, 80, 1)—aligned temporally with video blocks, treating the 80 (frequency) × 80 (time) time-frequency map as a single-channel image; the visual side crops the lip region according to ROI, scales it to 112×112, 3-channel RGB, with shape (T, 112, 112, 3). The two inputs are unified in form into "image block sequences," matching the isomorphic ResNet3D.

### Key Technical Innovation 3: Two-Stage Training Strategy and Augmentation for Modality Pairing

Joint training (Section 3.4) adopts a "pre-training + frozen fine-tuning" two-stage scheme: the single-modality parts are initialized with pre-trained models, and the fusion module is randomly initialized; all other settings are the same as single-modality pre-training, except the initial learning rate is reduced to 1e-4 (1e-3 for pre-training, ten times smaller). Why is this necessary? The randomly initialized fusion layer will produce unstructured gradients in the early stages of training. If the learning rate remains at the same level as pre-training, these gradients will crash the already learned single-modality representations—small learning rate essentially gives the fusion layer a "slowly learn not to harm" buffer period, which is a standard risk control in the multi-modal pre-training-finetuning paradigm.

Single-modality pre-training itself (Section 3.3) also has two designs targeting task characteristics. The first is **handling class imbalance**: weighted cross-entropy (negative:positive = 1:5) combined with label smoothing (preventing the model from being overconfident in hard labels). The training set has approximately nine times more negative samples than positive samples; without weighting, the model would bias towards "always predicting negative" to lower the loss, directly increasing the missed wake-up rate. The second is **modality-specific data augmentation**: audio side uses speed perturbation, pitch shifting, and noise augmentation; visual side uses frame-by-frame rotation, horizontal flipping, and color transformation. Table 2 shows that visual modality alone, relying on data augmentation, pulled the Eval score from 31.21 to 26.37 and Dev from 22.74 to 15.59, representing the largest single-item gain for the visual track.

There is also an augmentation targeting "scarce audio-video pairing" in the joint training stage (Section 3.4): **randomly replace the current video with another video of the same category** to simulate more audio-video input pairs. The principle is: over 5,000 positive samples mean the model can easily memorize the binding relationship of "this speaker's lip shape matches this voice timbre." Same-category replacement breaks the speaker binding while keeping the label unchanged (both are wake-word class), equivalent to a cheap multi-speaker augmentation, forcing the model to learn the true audio-visual content correspondence rather than speaker identity shortcuts.

### Technical Differences with Existing Methods

Comparing with three reference systems clarifies the position of this paper. **Compared with previous audio-visual KWS works ([11][13][14])**: They all belong to the "exclusive encoding + post-hoc fusion" paradigm—[11] calculates inter-modal similarity maps, [13] aggregates single-modality intermediate features at different levels, [14] performs feature concatenation and score averaging; the fusion action occurs after representations are learned. VE-KWS, however, moves interaction to the representation learning stage (cross-attention within the Transformer layer) and replaces first-order concatenation with second-order bilinear interaction, while being the only system using vision in front-end multi-channel enhancement. **Compared with the audio single-modality robustness route ([7] MLENet, [8] ConvMixer)**: The latter performs enhancement and curriculum learning within the audio modality, does not touch multi-channel beamforming, and has no visual information; Table 1 ablation of VE-KWS shows that just the front-end enhancement (visual-assisted MVDR plus data augmentation) reduces the audio single-modality Eval score from 14.39 to 9.78. **Compared with this paper's own fixed beam ablation (the +BF row in Table 1)**: Using the same lip position information, the gap between hard-selected beam (BF) and soft angle features (this method) shows that "how visual information is injected" is as important as "whether it is injected"—Eval scores 13.70 vs. 12.64, Dev scores 12.52 vs. 9.50. In terms of fusion structure, the bilinear plus residual projection approach is borrowed from cross-modal work in emotion recognition (reference [9]), but this paper adapts it to binary KWS decision and串联s it with cross-attention, forming a new combination.

## Experimental Results

### Dataset Used and Its Scale

All experiments are conducted on the audio-visual wake-word detection (WWS) database released by the MISP2021 challenge (Section 3.1): approximately **125 hours** of audio-visual data, recorded in **over 30 real rooms**. Three subsets: Training set with over 47,000 negative samples and over 5,000 positive samples; Development set with over 2,000 negative samples and over 600 positive samples; Evaluation set with over 8,000 samples (the positive-negative split of the evaluation set is not reported in the paper). Acquisition devices cover three distance levels of audio and two levels of video: six-channel far-field microphone array, two-channel mid-field microphone array, single-channel near-field microphone, mid-field HD camera, and far-field wide-angle camera. The wake word is "Xiao T, Xiao T," with samples containing this wake word labeled as positive, otherwise negative. The visual modality pre-training additionally uses CAS-VSR-W1k (i.e., LRW-1000, a large-scale Chinese natural scene lip-reading benchmark, reference [25], the +Pre-train row in Table 2).

The value of this dataset lies in it being the first publicly available large-scale real-home audio-visual KWS testbed—previous audio-visual KWS research (such as reference [11]) was long constrained by the lack of public datasets, forcing proxy tasks on lip-reading word list data. Multi-device synchronous recording (far/mid/near three levels of microphones recording simultaneously) also directly supports this paper's "near-field supervising far-field" enhancement network training strategy.

### Definition and Rationale for Evaluation Metrics

Following the official settings of the MISP challenge (Section 3.1, formula 11): Total Score = FRR + FAR = N_FR/N_wake + N_FA/N_non-wake, i.e., Missed Wake-up Rate (number of samples containing the wake word but not detected divided by the total number of samples containing the wake word) plus False Alarm Rate (number of samples not containing the wake word but falsely detected divided by the total number of samples not containing the wake word), where lower scores are better.

The rationale for selection can be understood from three levels. First, this is the official challenge metric, directly comparable with all participating systems (Table 5). Second, the two types of errors in KWS are fatal to user experience: missed wake-ups mean the assistant is unresponsive, while false wake-ups mean being randomly woken up by TV noise late at night; neither allows sacrificing one for the other, and equal weighting forces this balance. Third, the base numbers of positive and negative samples differ significantly (approximately 9:1); normalizing them separately before adding avoids the original error counts being drowned out by the negative sample base. It should be noted that how the decision threshold is selected, and whether it is tuned on the development set, is not reported in the paper—this has a non-negligible impact on the FRR/FAR balance point.

### Detailed Comparison with Baseline and SOTA Methods

**Audio Single-Modality Track (Table 1).** Baseline (no extra tricks) Dev/Eval total score 14.96/14.39; +Fixed Beamforming (BF) 12.52/13.70; +Pure Audio MVDR 11.49/13.13; +This Paper's Visual-Assisted MVDR 9.50/12.64; after adding data augmentation, Dev/Eval is 9.85/**9.78**. The relative reductions given in the original text: Visual-Assisted MVDR reduces by 36.50% (Dev) and 12.02% (Eval) relative to the baseline, and by 24.12% (Dev) and 7.59% (Eval) relative to fixed beamforming. Looking closely at the decomposition of FRR/FAR is very informative: all three enhancement methods significantly suppress FAR (Baseline Dev FAR 8.07 → This Paper's MVDR 3.41; Eval FAR 5.48 → 3.22), because beamforming suppresses false wake-ups caused by TV noise; but this paper's MVDR actually increases FRR on Eval to 9.42 (Baseline 8.91), indicating that enhancement brings some distortion or missed enhancement while suppressing noise—until data augmentation (speed/pitch/noise perturbation) pulls FRR back to 5.82, indicating that this distortion can be covered by expanding the training distribution. Another notable phenomenon is that the +Data Aug row slightly worsens on Dev (9.50 → 9.85) but significantly improves on Eval (12.64 → 9.78); the paper reports this truthfully without explaining the reason, hypothesizing it may be related to the augmentation noise distribution matching the real noise of the Eval set more closely (the paper does not report the specific source and configuration of the augmentation noise).

**Visual Single-Modality Track (Table 2).** Baseline Dev/Eval total score 22.74/31.21; +Data Augmentation 15.59/26.37; using CAS-VSR-W1k pre-training 13.41/26.01. Two conclusions: Data augmentation is the single item with the largest gain for the visual modality; lip-reading pre-training continues to significantly improve on Dev (15.59 → 13.41) but hardly moves on Eval (26.37 → 26.01)—the domain gap (viewpoint, resolution, lighting) between the Chinese lip-reading benchmark and the home far-field occlusion scenario limits the transfer of pre-training gains. The huge gap between visual single-modality and audio single-modality (Eval 9.78 vs. 26.01) reversely validates the judgment in Section 2.2.2 that "the audio modality is generally more reliable," and also indicates that the value of vision must be realized through fusion, not by carrying the task independently.

**Multi-Modal Fusion Track (Table 3).** Decision-level fusion (averaging scores of three single-modality systems) Dev/Eval 6.50/8.58; Feature-level fusion (concatenating single-modality outputs into a fully connected layer) 4.98/6.65; This Paper's VE-KWS **3.62/5.74**. Relative to decision fusion, this paper reduces scores by 44.31% on Dev and 33.10% on Eval (original Section 3.5). The ordering of the three numbers (Cross-Attention Bilinear > Concatenation > Averaging) cleanly proves the core argument that "interactive fusion is superior to post-hoc combination"; another detail is that VE-KWS's Dev FRR is as low as 1.60%, tied for the lowest with decision fusion, while FAR is compressed from 4.90% in decision fusion to 2.02%—the fusion mechanism mainly eats up false wake-ups, which is precisely the most painful type of error in the home TV scenario.

**Performance under Different Noise Conditions (Table 4).** The Eval set is divided into three subsets: Noisy/Medium/Clean based on the BAK component (background noise component) of Dnsmos (a non-intrusive objective speech quality metric, reference [26]), and scores are calculated separately: Audio single-modality scores 12.29, 8.77, 8.31 on Noisy, Medium, Clean respectively, with noise causing a performance drop of about 4 points; Visual single-modality scores for the three subsets are 23.78/28.41/26.00, almost unaffected by noise (even the Noisy subset has the lowest score, which the paper attributes to vision being unaffected by acoustic noise, but the difference between Noisy and Medium is not further explained by the paper, and the sample size of each subset is not reported); VE-KWS scores for the three subsets are 7.18/5.61/4.47—on the Noisy subset, the fusion system (7.18) is significantly better than the audio single-modality (12.29), a reduction of about 41.6%, while not sacrificing performance on the Clean subset (4.47 is better than audio's 8.31). This table is direct evidence for the "complementarity" argument: the visual modality provides an effective fallback in the noise interval where audio collapses.

**Comparison with Top Challenge Systems (Table 5).** The top three systems in the MISP2021 challenge: First place CNN-TDNNF+MMI (challenge baseline paper [12]) Dev/Test 4.1/5.8; Second place ResNet+HMA (DKU system [13]) 7.27/7.1; Third place Transformer+Fusion [14] did not report Dev / Test 9.1. This paper's VE-KWS achieves Dev **3.62** / Test **5.74**, outperforming the first place system in both metrics (3.62 < 4.1, 5.74 < 5.8), establishing a new SOTA. The final system's performance, according to the abstract and conclusion, is: Eval set FRR 2.79%, FAR 2.95%.

### Findings from Ablation Experiments

The paper's ablation covers front-end enhancement, single-modality training techniques, and fusion structure,串ing together five findings.

**Finding 1: Visual-Assisted MVDR is the correct form for front-end enhancement, and "soft injection" is superior to "hard selection".** Eval total score ordering: Baseline 14.39 > Fixed Beam 13.70 > Pure Audio MVDR 13.13 > Visual-Assisted MVDR 12.64 (Table 1). Three pieces of information: Lip prior is effective (visual-assisted is better than pure audio MVDR); neural mask-style beamforming is better than fixed beamforming; the main gains of all enhancement methods are reflected in FAR. On the Dev side, Visual-Assisted MVDR's 9.50 reduces by 36.50% relative to the baseline 14.96, and by 24.12% relative to the fixed beam 12.52 (original Section 3.5).

**Finding 2: Data augmentation is one of the single items with the largest gain for both modalities.** Audio side reduces Eval from 12.64 to 9.78 (last row of Table 1); Visual side pulls Dev from 22.74 to 15.59 and Eval from 31.21 to 26.37 (Table 2). Audio augmentation (speed, pitch, noise) also incidentally repairs the FRR worsening caused by beam enhancement (9.42 → 5.82), indicating that the enhancement distortion problem can be alleviated from the training data distribution level.

**Finding 3: The gains of lip-reading pre-training are limited by domain gaps.** CAS-VSR-W1k pre-training improves visual Dev from 15.59 to 13.41, but Eval only moves from 26.37 to 26.01 (Table 2), with limited cross-scenario transfer.

**Finding 4: The ordering of fusion mechanisms is "Interactive > Concatenation > Averaging".** Decision averaging 6.50/8.58, Feature concatenation 4.98/6.65, This Paper's method 3.62/5.74 (Table 3), with relative reductions of up to 44.31%/33.10%. This is the most direct evidence supporting the paper's core claim (that modality relationships should participate in respective modeling).

**Finding 5: The environment-dependence of modality reliability is quantitatively confirmed.** Audio collapses on the Noisy subset (12.29 vs. Clean 8.31), vision is stable across subsets (23.78–28.41), and the fused system is no worse than any single modality on all subsets (7.18/5.61/4.47, Table 4).

Ablation gaps to point out: The cross-attention and bilinear pooling within the fusion module were not ablated separately—Table 3 only compares the overall method with two classic fusion methods, making it impossible to distinguish the attribution of fusion gains between "cross-attention" and "bilinear" from existing experiments; designs such as the bidirectionality of cross-attention and the bilinear residual branch also do not have their own ablation rows.

## Main Contributions

1. **Introducing the visual modality into multi-channel speech enhancement**: This is the hardest contribution point of the paper. Previously, visual information in audio-visual KWS was only used for backend discrimination. This paper proves that lip region coordinates can be injected as spatial priors into mask-based MVDR, bypassing the deadlock of unreliable audio DOA estimation in home noise; and avoids the irreversible errors of hard-selected beams in the form of "soft injection of angle features" (Table 1: Eval 12.64 vs. fixed beam 13.70, pure audio MVDR 13.13).
2. **Proposing a fusion framework of cross-modal attention plus bilinear pooling**: Self-attention manages intra-modal dependencies, cross-attention manages inter-modal relationships using the other modality as query, and bilinear pooling plus residual projection completes second-order fusion, moving modality interaction from "after representations are learned" to "during representation learning," reducing scores by 44.31%/33.10% relative to decision fusion on Dev/Eval (Table 3).
3. **Establishing a new SOTA on MISP2021**: Eval set FRR 2.79%, FAR 2.95%, total score 5.74, Dev total score 3.62, outperforming the original first-place challenge system (4.1/5.8, Table 5) in both sets.
4. **A complete reproducible engineering recipe**: Near-field supervising far-field enhancement training, same-category video replacement augmentation, 1:5 weighted cross-entropy plus label smoothing, pre-training loading plus ten-times smaller learning rate for joint training; these details have direct reference value for subsequent audio-visual KWS work.

## Limitations and Future Work

### Technical Limitations of the Method

**Strong dependence on ROI quality.** Lip coordinates are provided by the challenge organizer's internal tool (Section 3.2); in actual deployment, self-built lip detection and tracking are required. Although the far-field wide-angle camera has a wide field of view, its resolution is limited; side profiles, looking down, occlusions, and backlighting will all degrade coordinate quality; how the system degrades when the speaker is not within the 120-degree field of view or happens to fall on the 20-degree region boundary is not discussed by the paper. A sharper scenario risk is: in the home TV scenario, there are already faces and lips on the TV screen itself; the possibility and consequences of the detector locking onto the TV face (beam pointing to the TV, visual representation taken from the TV face) are completely not involved in the paper.

**Limited granularity and dimension of directional priors.** Only horizontal angle, only 6 regions of 20 degrees, no elevation angle, and no sub-region interpolation; when multiple people are in the field of view simultaneously (a home norm), whose lip's region number is taken is not specified. The 20-degree granularity is a coarse alignment sufficient for the physical aperture of the microphone array, but it means the ceiling of prior information is low—this explains why angle features are only auxiliary evidence rather than a decision.

**Computational overhead is not reported and is likely not light.** The system includes: six-channel STFT and per-frequency-point covariance inversion (MVDR), dual-tower five-block ResNet3D (max 256 channels), two levels of attention, bilinear projection. Parameters, model size, and Real-Time Factor (RTF) are not reported in the paper. The authors themselves admit in the conclusion: although the challenge does not limit RTF and model size, small size and real-time processing are essential conditions for practical KWS, and they list it as future work—this is equivalent to admitting that the current form is far from edge-side deployment. For comparison, pure audio small-size KWS models are usually compressed to within megabyte-level parameters, while this system's dual-tower video encoder is clearly not on the same scale.

**The boundary of "end-to-end" needs clarification.** The "end-to-end" in the title refers to the backend's end-to-end from raw features to 0/1 decision; the front-end visual-assisted MVDR is trained independently with IRM plus MSE, and is not jointly optimized with the KWS decision loss. The optimization target of the enhancement network is to reconstruct near-field speech, not to maximize keyword detection—these two are not always consistent (enhancement distortion may恰好 damage discriminative phonemes), and the worsening of FRR on Eval for this paper's MVDR in Table 1 (8.91 → 9.42) is a shadow of this cascading risk.

**No fallback mechanism for modality failure scenarios.** The visual single-modality is very weak (Eval 26.01, Table 4); how the fusion system performs in scenarios where vision completely fails, such as darkness or complete occlusion, is not tested with modality dropout experiments; while same-category video replacement augmentation enhances pairing diversity, the replaced video and the original audio only share the same label (wake-word class), and their content details do not strictly correspond, introducing a certain degree of pseudo-pairing noise.

### Deficiencies in Experimental Design

- **Single dataset validation**: All conclusions come from one dataset, MISP2021; generalization across languages (wake words) and scenarios (in-car, office) is unknown.
- **Insufficient ablation granularity**: The fusion module was not split for ablation (as mentioned above); sensitivity of hyperparameters such as dual-tower isomorphic vs. heterogeneous, Transformer layer count, and audio "image-ization" window length (80 frames, stride 4) was not tested.
- **Missing key experimental details**: The method of selecting the decision threshold, the sample size of the three noise subsets, the noise sources used for data augmentation, and whether the training data caliber was consistent when comparing with challenge participating systems (the challenge has rules on data usage) are not reported in the paper—among these, the threshold and data caliber items directly affect the strictness of the Table 5 comparison.
- **Blank in phenomenon explanation**: Counter-intuitive phenomena such as the audio track +Data Aug row having opposite directions on Dev and Eval (9.85 vs. 9.78), and the visual track performing best on the Noisy subset (23.78 lower than Clean 26.00) are only reported without analysis.
- **No statistical significance testing**: Differences of 0.06 to 0.5 score levels between systems (e.g., Test 5.74 vs. 5.8) are not supported by variance or significance tests.

### Possible Future Improvement Directions

- **Lightweight and Real-time** (the next step stated by the authors): Replace ResNet3D with lightweight encoders, knowledge distillation, model quantization, compressing the system into edge-side budgets; streaming transformation to support permanent listening.
- **Joint Optimization of Front-end and Decision**: Use differentiable MVDR (or neural beamformer) and KWS loss for end-to-end joint training, eliminating the cascading risk of "inconsistent enhancement and discrimination targets."
- **More Precise Visual Localization**: Upgrade from 6-region classification to continuous angle regression, or introduce temporal smoothing and confidence gating of lip tracking, while explicitly modeling TV face interference.
- **Modality Dropout Robustness**: Add modality dropout during training and dynamically route based on modality confidence during inference, ensuring graceful degradation in dark/occluded scenarios.
- **Personalization Extension**: Combine face recognition or voiceprint to perform target-speaker personalized KWS, further reducing false wake-ups caused by others saying the wake word.
- **Cross-Corpus and Cross-Language Validation**: Validate the fusion mechanism (its architecture design itself is modality-agnostic) on more audio-visual corpora, and test performance beyond Chinese wake words.
