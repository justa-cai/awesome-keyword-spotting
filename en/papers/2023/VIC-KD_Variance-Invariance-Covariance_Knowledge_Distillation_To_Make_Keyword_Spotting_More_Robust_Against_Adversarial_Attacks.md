# VIC-KD: Variance-Invariance-Covariance Knowledge Distillation to Make Keyword Spotting More Robust Against Adversarial Attacks

- **Keywords**: keyword spotting, adversarial robustness, knowledge distillation, robust distillation, VICReg, self-supervised speech representations, model compression

## Problem Statement

### Problem Background and Domain Pain Points
Keyword Spotting (KWS) systems serve as the first entry point for virtual assistants, responsible for recognizing preset wake-up words (e.g., "Hey Siri") in continuous audio streams to activate devices. Edge-side speech processing, represented by Google Now and Alexa, is becoming widespread, but this brings threefold pressure: energy efficiency (devices listen constantly with limited battery budgets), real-time processing (wake-up latency directly determines interaction experience), and user privacy (edge processing keeps private data local without uploading to the cloud or third-party servers). The paper explicitly points out that edge processing itself is a layer of security protection—provided the model can run on resource-constrained hardware.

In recent years, accuracy improvements in KWS have largely come from Self-Supervised Speech Representation Learning (S3RL), with representative models being Wav2Vec 2.0 and WavLM, which jointly learn acoustic and language models on continuous audio inputs. The problem lies in model scale: according to the data in Table 1 of the paper, the two teacher models both have 94.4M parameters, with inference computational costs of 6160.9 MMACs (Wav2Vec 2.0) and 6132.7 MMACs (WavLM) per inference, respectively. This magnitude is completely unfeasible for microcontroller-level edge hardware. In contrast, the student models selected in the paper must have fewer than 96K parameters and 3 MMACs—calculated this way, the parameter count needs to be compressed by approximately 1174 times (from 94.4M to 80.4K for XVector) to about 1394 times (to 67.7K for TC-ResNet), and the computational cost needs to be compressed by approximately 2115 to 3851 times (converted from the MMACs data in Table 1).

Meanwhile, adversarial attacks on speech technology are increasing. The goal of adversarial attacks is to design a tiny perturbation $\delta$ that, when added to the test signal, forces the system to fail. Over-the-air adversarial attacks have proven that adding imperceptible noise to user speech can cause misclassification (UTIO attack in citation [8]); the author team's previous work (citation [7]) also demonstrated that KWS systems based on self-supervised representations are vulnerable to white-box adversarial attacks during inference. Thus, edge KWS faces a dual requirement: the model must be small enough to fit into edge hardware and robust enough to resist adversarial attacks. Previous work either only handled compression (standard knowledge distillation) or only handled robustness (adversarial training), rarely solving both problems in the same recipe—this is the problem space of this paper.

### Specific Shortcomings of Existing Methods
The paper divides existing defenses into two levels and points out the gaps one by one:

- **Standard Knowledge Distillation (KD, Hinton et al.) only transfers generalization ability, not robustness.** KD uses teacher logits to guide the student, allowing the student to approach the teacher's accuracy on clean data (GSC-12 clean accuracy reaches up to 96.91 in the KD row of Table 2), but under AutoAttack, the student's robust accuracy is only 4.49–9.29, close to or even below the 12-class random guessing level (approx. 8.3%). More critical evidence is: even if KD is equipped with a robust teacher trained via adversarial training, the student's accuracy after attack is only 4.49–7.19 (Robust Teacher column in Table 2), while the robust teacher's own robust accuracy is as high as 93.80–94.79 (Table 1). The student's robustness does not automatically appear as the teacher becomes robust; this is an experimental fact repeatedly emphasized in the paper.
- **ARD (Adversarial Robust Distillation, AAAI 2020) combines adversarial training and KD, emphasizing minimizing the KL divergence between student and teacher logits.** It does indeed pull robust accuracy from single digits to the 74–80 range, but in 7 out of 8 configurations (Teacher/Student × Standard/Robust Teacher) in Table 2, it is still lower than the baseline of the same-architecture student directly trained with TRADES (a gap of 0.4 to 6.3 percentage points). That is, spending significant cost on robust distillation yields a student worse than directly doing adversarial training on a small model.
- **RSLAD (Robust Soft Label Adversarial Distillation, ICCV 2021) uses the teacher's soft labels instead of hard labels in the adversarial training loop.** Empirically superior to ARD, but the paper's introduction explicitly states that both ARD and RSLAD "fail to consistently surpass the accuracy of a smaller model obtained via adversarial training." Details in Table 2 support this judgment: when TC-ResNet is the student, all four RSLAD configurations are 1.2–3.0 percentage points lower than the baseline; only when the slightly larger capacity XVector is the student does it exceed the baseline by 0.4–1.7 percentage points.
- **Common structural gap: These methods only constrain the output layer** (KL divergence of logits or soft labels), completely ignoring the geometric shape of the intermediate representation space. Why is this an essential gap rather than an implementation detail? Because the mechanism of adversarial vulnerability is that tiny perturbations at the input are amplified layer by layer in the representation space into large displacements, eventually crossing the decision boundary; aligning probability distributions only at the output layer is equivalent to only correcting the national borders on a map without fixing the terrain—many vastly different representation geometries can produce the same logits on the training distribution, but their behavior under perturbation is completely different. Moreover, the student model has extremely limited capacity (67.7K–80.4K parameters), and every dimension of representation capacity is worth calculating carefully; supervision signals at the output layer cannot tell the student "what information each dimension should hold."
- Additionally, the role of multi-view inputs in distillation has previously only been studied for its contribution to environmental robustness (citations [13][14], including the author team's own RobustDistiller work); its impact on **robust** distillation is unknown—the paper lists this as an open question to be answered.

### Key Challenges This Paper Aims to Solve
How to design a distillation recipe that compresses a 94.4M parameter S3RL teacher into a student with fewer than 96K parameters and 3 MMACs (thousand-fold compression), while simultaneously satisfying three conditions that previous methods could not achieve together: (1) Robust accuracy under AutoAttack white-box ensemble attacks stably exceeds the baseline of the same-architecture student directly trained with TRADES—neither ARD nor RSLAD can achieve this; (2) Clean accuracy does not collapse significantly, maintaining clean accuracy comparable to standard KD; (3) Zero additional overhead during inference—all new mechanisms can only exist in the training phase, because what runs during deployment is just that "primary school student" model.

## Methodology

### Overall Architecture Design and Design Motivation
The overall process of VIC-KD (Paper Fig. 1) is as follows: Sample an audio clip $x$ from the training set, randomly extract two different transformations $t$ and $t'$ ($t \neq t'$, both from transformation set $T$). The teacher branch receives $t(x)$, extracting representation $Z$ through a fine-tuned frozen S3RL encoder—specifically, by taking a weighted sum of intermediate representations from all Transformer layers and aggregating along the time dimension, obtaining a $d$-dimensional sentence-level embedding. The student branch receives the other view $t'(x)$, on which adversarial perturbation is added, first passing through the student encoder to obtain intermediate representation $H'$; $H'$ connects to two heads: a classification head generating logits $Y'$ for predicting the command word, and a projection head generating $Z'$, whose dimension is the same as the teacher's $Z$. The training loss is a convex combination of the TRADES loss and the VICReg loss, controlled by hyperparameter $\alpha$. At the test stage, only the student encoder and classification head are retained; the projection head and the entire teacher branch are discarded.

The "whys" behind several design choices:

- **Why use full-layer weighted sum plus time aggregation for teacher representations**: Different layers of Transformers encode information at different levels (lower layers偏向 acoustic, higher layers偏向 language); layer-by-layer weighted sum allows a learnable scalar combination to automatically select the most suitable mixed ratio for the KWS task; aggregating along the time dimension is because KWS classifies the entire 1-second sentence, and sentence-level embeddings naturally remove the time axis, making $Z$ a compact anchor.
- **Why the projection head**: The projection head decouples the "space constrained by geometric regularization" from the "classification feature space." Geometric regularization is merely scaffolding during training; the entire thing is discarded at test time, thus producing no inference overhead—this directly serves the challenge constraint of "zero inference overhead." This approach inherits from the convention of projection heads in self-supervised learning series like BYOL/SimSAM.
- **Why adversarial perturbation is only added to the student branch, keeping the teacher with natural input**: The teacher acts as an "anchor of clean geometry" in the recipe; the student is trained to map attacked inputs to the same region where the teacher maps clean inputs—robustness is written into the training signal itself, not relying on whether the teacher itself is robust. This choice is verified in reverse in experiments (see ablation section: standard teacher is sufficient).
- **Why require $t \neq t'$**: If both branches see the exact same input, the invariance loss would be trivially satisfied, and regularization would fail; two different views force the student to learn view-stable features.
- **Why these two students**: TC-ResNet (Interspeech 2019, a temporal convolutional network for real-time KWS on mobile devices) has 67.7K parameters, 1.6 MMACs; XVector modified version (reducing TDNN layers, shrinking convolutional kernels to fit resource constraints) has 80.4K parameters, 2.9 MMACs (Table 1). Both satisfy the hard constraint of "within 96K parameters and 3 MMACs," and represent two different edge architecture lineages (temporal convolution vs. TDNN statistical embeddings from speaker verification).

### Mathematical Principles of Core Algorithms
Let $Z = [z_1, z_2, \dots, z_n]$ be the $d$-dimensional latent representations produced by the teacher model on natural (transformed) inputs for a batch of $n$ sentences, and $Z' = [z'_1, z'_2, \dots, z'_n]$ be the same $d$-dimensional representations produced by the student model on adversarially perturbed inputs. The VICReg loss (Paper Equation 1) is:

$$L_{VICReg} = Var(Z') + Inv(Z, Z') + Cov(Z')$$

The roles and "whys" of the three terms:

- **Invariance term $Inv(Z, Z')$**: Mean squared error between the two representation sets. Note that its anchoring direction is **cross-model, cross-view, cross-natural/attacked**—the teacher sees natural view $t(x)$, the student sees attacked view $t'(x)+\delta$; the loss requires the student to pull the attacked input to the position where the teacher processes the clean input. This is the most core force in the entire recipe: adversarial robustness is directly regularized for the first time not on output probabilities but on representation geometry.
- **Variance term $Var(Z')$**: Constrains the standard deviation of each embedding dimension within the batch to remain above a threshold. Why it is necessary: when only the invariance loss exists, there is a trivial solution—$Z'$ collapses into a constant vector, loss is zero but the model is useless. The variance lower bound is an explicit anti-collapse defense line (implementation details follow the hinge form in the original VICReg paper [17]; specific threshold coefficients are not reported in the paper).
- **Covariance term $Cov(Z')$**: Presses the off-diagonal elements of the representation covariance matrix toward zero, i.e., decorrelation. Why this is particularly important for KWS students: student capacity is only 67.7K–80.4K parameters; if multiple dimensions encode highly similar features, it equals wasting already scarce representation capacity; decorrelation forces each dimension to carry different information, effectively subsidizing the small model at the information density level.
- The paper explicitly states that the three terms contribute equally to the final loss; specific implementation details point to the original VICReg paper [17].

The final VIC-KD loss is a convex combination of the TRADES loss and the VICReg loss, with weights controlled by hyperparameter $\alpha$ (specific value of $\alpha$ is not reported in the main text, marked in Fig. 1). Why combine them this way: TRADES handles the trade-off between clean accuracy and robustness at the output level (minimizing classification loss on natural samples while constraining the logit distribution difference between natural and adversarial samples), while VICReg shapes geometry at the representation level; the convex combination ensures that neither constraint is completely suppressed by the other.

### Key Technical Innovation 1: Transferring Geometric Priors from Self-Supervised Learning to Robust Distillation
Joint Embedding Architectures (JEA) have proven effective in self-supervised learning (BYOL, Barlow Twins, VICReg), among which VICReg's unique property is: its defense against representation collapse is entirely composed of explicit loss terms (variance + covariance), requiring no negative sample pairs, no stop-gradient, and no asymmetric network structures. This determines its portability—it can be directly plugged into a supervised distillation pipeline without disrupting existing training dynamics. The migration done by the paper is: rewriting the structure in SSL where "two augmented views act as anchors for each other" into a structure where "teacher natural representations act as anchors, student attacked representations act as targets," thereby turning tools for learning invariance in SSL into tools for learning perturbation resistance in robust distillation. The paper explicitly states in related work that multi-view inputs for (environmental) robust distillation have precedents (citations [13][14]), but using geometric regularization for robust distillation is the new combination proposed in this paper, the effect of which is exactly what this paper aims to answer.

### Key Technical Innovation 2: Superposition of Multi-View Inputs and Adversarial Perturbation on Student Branch
The transformation set $T$ contains six types: clean data, noisy, reverberant, noisy+reverberant, wave chunk dropping, speed perturbation. The teacher and student each receive a random view, and adversarial perturbation generated by a 10-step PGD-like attack ($\epsilon = 1.5 \times 10^{-3}$, step size $3 \times 10^{-4}$, consistent with the attack budget during evaluation) is superimposed on the student view. The motivation for superimposing environmental augmentation and adversarial perturbation is: both "perturbation sources" manifest as noise factors at the representation level; features learned by the student under the triple pressure of two views + adversarial noise are forced to strip away non-speech factors and retain only discriminative speech structures. The paper gives two hypotheses for the gain brought by multi-view (Section 4.2): first, multi-view induces the student to learn perturbation-invariant features, better decoupling noise factors from speech features, improving generalization; second, drawing on existing discussions in self-supervised learning, adopting multi-view methods forces maximizing the mutual information between the two view representations; the authors speculate that distillation scenarios can be analogized—but the authors explicitly mark this hypothesis as "requiring more research."

### Key Technical Innovation 3: Robustness Comes from Geometric Priors, Not Robust Teachers
Table 2 reports both "Standard Teacher" and "Robust Teacher" columns, forming an internal ablation. The conclusion is: for robust distillation methods, using a robust teacher generally does not improve the student's final performance. For example, in the Wav2Vec 2.0/XVector combination for VIC-KD, the standard teacher robust accuracy is 86.12, while the robust teacher drops to 85.33; for the WavLM/XVector combination, the two are basically持平 (86.08 vs 86.31). Combined with the fact from KD experiments that "robustness of robust teachers does not transfer through soft labels," the engineering conclusion given by the paper is: on this GSC use case, unless you already have a ready-made robust teacher, it is not worth spending extra computation to adversarially train a standard teacher into a robust teacher before doing robust distillation—using a fine-tuned standard teacher directly is sufficient. The deeper implication of this conclusion is: VIC-KD needs the **clean geometric anchor** provided by the teacher, not the teacher's robust behavior.

### Technical Differences with Existing Methods
Four methods form a clear spectrum on the dimension of "location of robustness signal injection": Standard KD places the signal on logits of natural data (no robustness); ARD places the signal on the KL divergence of logits of adversarial data (output layer robustness); RSLAD places the signal on the teacher's soft labels in the adversarial training loop (still output layer, but labels are softer and finer); VIC-KD sinks the signal into the representation space—directly shaping the geometry of student embeddings using the three terms of variance, invariance, and covariance, while the robustness constraint at the output layer continues to be borne by the TRADES term, with the two forces complementing each other. Another difference axis is "whether a robust teacher is needed": the native design of ARD/RSLAD is naturally compatible with robust teachers, while VIC-KD experiments prove that a standard teacher is sufficient. The third difference axis is training cost: VIC-KD training time is four times that of RSLAD (explicitly reported in Section 4.1 of the paper), but inference time is exactly the same for all distillation recipes, because inference depends only on the student architecture.

## Experimental Results

### Datasets Used and Their Scale
The experiment uses the Google Speech Commands v0.02 (GSC) dataset: approx. 100k 1-second English speech clips, 35 command words, 16 kHz sampling. The paper adopts two versions: one is all 35 classes; the other is a 12-class version, including {yes, no, up, down, left, right, on, off, stop, go, unknown, silence}—where the silence class is composed of background noise without speech, and the unknown class is uniformly sampled and stitched from the remaining unused classes. Data splitting follows the SpeechBrain recipe, divided into training, validation, and test sets at 80%/10%/10%.

Model configurations (Table 1): Teachers are Wav2Vec 2.0 (94.4M parameters, 6160.9 MMACs) and WavLM (94.4M parameters, 6132.7 MMACs), both fine-tuned for 10 epochs on the KWS task, with learning rate scheduled from $5 \times 10^{-4}$ to $5 \times 10^{-5}$; the teacher structure is an S3RL encoder plus the aforementioned weighted sum aggregation mechanism plus a linear classification layer (in the VIC-KD scenario, this linear layer can be discarded because only latent representations are needed). Baseline models are trained for 100 epochs, batch size 32, Adam optimizer, with learning rate linearly decaying from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-4}$. Three distillation baselines (KD, ARD, RSLAD) and VIC-KD are all trained for 250 epochs, with hyperparameters aligned with small baseline models.

### Definition and Rationale for Evaluation Metrics
The paper reports two accuracies: clean accuracy (accuracy on test set without attack) and robust accuracy (accuracy under AutoAttack). The attack uses AutoAttack—an ensemble of APGD, APGD-T, and FAB attacks, constrained to an $\ell_\infty$ norm ball with radius $\epsilon = 1.5 \times 10^{-3}$. Rationale for selection (combined with community consensus): AutoAttack is a parameter-free attack ensemble, currently the reliable standard for adversarial robustness evaluation, avoiding the inflation caused by "self-proving robustness with a weak attack"; simultaneously reporting both clean and robust numbers exposes the core contradiction in the adversarial training field: the clean-robust trade-off. The efficiency dimension is measured by parameter count and MMACs. Notably, the $\epsilon$ used to generate adversarial perturbations during training is consistent with the evaluation $\epsilon$ (both $1.5 \times 10^{-3}$); the paper does not report performance when the two are inconsistent.

### Detailed Comparison with Baseline Methods and SOTA
**Baseline Panorama (Table 1)**: Naturally trained models collapse comprehensively under attack—TC-ResNet robust accuracy is only 0.09% on 35 classes, XVector only 1.36%, and the two 94.4M large models only reach 0.64–9.40%; TRADES adversarial training pulls small models up: TC-ResNet reaches 79.25% (12 classes) / 77.30% (35 classes), XVector reaches 80.33% / 78.27%, and teacher-level models reach 90.63–94.79%. These numbers establish the "opponent to beat": robust distillation methods must defeat these same-architecture direct adversarial training baselines to be meaningful.

**12-Class Main Experiment (Table 2)**: VIC-KD exceeds the corresponding baseline in all 8 configurations (2 Teachers × 2 Students × Standard/Robust Teacher), with margins of +4.1 to +6.0 percentage points (numbers in parentheses in Table 2 are percentage point differences relative to their respective TRADES baselines). The best combinations are WavLM/XVector + Robust Teacher (86.31%) and Wav2Vec 2.0/XVector + Standard Teacher (86.12%). The performance of control groups is clearly stratified: KD has the highest clean accuracy overall (96.91) but robust accuracy of 4.49–9.29, resembling an undefended state; ARD is lower than the baseline in 7 out of 8 configurations (-0.4 to -6.3 percentage points); RSLAD is slightly higher than the baseline only on the larger capacity XVector (+0.5 to +1.7), and still 1.2–3.0 lower on TC-ResNet. The relative improvement numbers given in the main text: on the Wav2Vec 2.0/XVector combination, VIC-KD's robust accuracy improves by 7.2% relative to the baseline (86.12 vs 80.33, converted from Table 2), and by 8.0% and 6.6% relative to ARD and RSLAD respectively (86.12 vs 79.74 and 80.79). The abstract claims improvements of 12% and 8% for ARD and RSLAD, which are not completely consistent with the 8.0%/6.6% calculable from the main text; the abstract numbers do not specify the configuration, so Table 2 should be used for reference.

**Trade-off Quality (Converted from Table 2 and Fig. 3)**: The clean-robust gap is key to measuring the trade-off. 12 classes, WavLM/XVector, Standard Teacher: KD gap is 88.91 percentage points, ARD 16.94, RSLAD 14.04, TRADES baseline 14.37, VIC-KD is only 9.84—VIC-KD is the only recipe that compresses the gap into the single-digit close range, while clean accuracy 95.92 is not significantly sacrificed.

**35-Class Extension (Fig. 3, WavLM/XVector combination)**: KD clean 94.95/attack 4.43; ARD 94.22/68.67; RSLAD 93.78/78.25; VIC-KD 94.11/80.6; Supervised baseline XVector TRADES is 93.56/78.27 (35-class robust training column in Table 1). VIC-KD's robust accuracy exceeds the baseline by 2.33 percentage points, while the clean-robust gap is the smallest: VIC-KD 13.51 < RSLAD 15.53 < ARD 25.55 ≪ KD 90.52. ARD drops the hardest when the number of classes increases (about 9.6 percentage points lower than the baseline), indicating that robustness constraints at the pure logits layer fail first on more difficult classification boundaries; VIC-KD's advantage remains after class number expansion. Fig. 3 does not explicitly state whether a standard or robust teacher was used.

**Cost**: Training time for VIC-KD is four times that of RSLAD (due to multi-view forward pass, 10-step PGD attack generation, and teacher forward pass); inference time is exactly the same for all distillation recipes, depending only on the student architecture—this guarantees the promise of zero deployment overhead.

### Findings from Ablation Experiments
**Multi-view Ablation (Fig. 2, WavLM/TC-ResNet combination, red dashed line is TC-ResNet TRADES baseline 79.25)**: Without multi-view, KD 9.29, ARD 74.13, RSLAD 77.17, VIC-KD 80.75; with multi-view, KD 10.38, ARD 80.03, RSLAD 81.93, VIC-KD 83.3. Three findings are worth expanding: first, multi-view brings gains to all distillation methods; ARD and RSLAD were originally below the baseline, and only crossed the baseline line after adding multi-view—this means the robustness advantages claimed by these existing methods actually rely on the "multi-view" add-on, which is not their methodological contribution; second, VIC-KD is the only method that exceeds the baseline without multi-view (80.75 vs 79.25), indicating that the geometric prior itself covers part of the invariance provided by multi-view; third, multi-view still brings an additional gain of 2.55 percentage points to VIC-KD (from 80.75 to 83.3); the combined effect of the two mechanisms is best, but the marginal gain is smaller than for ARD/RSLAD (which increased by 5.90 and 4.76 percentage points respectively), indirectly confirming that the roles of geometric regularization and multi-view overlap.

**Teacher Type Ablation (Comparison of two columns in Table 2)**: As described in Innovation 3, robust teachers are generally not advantageous. The most extreme example is the KD row—the student distilled from a robust teacher (itself with robust accuracy 94.79) only achieves 5.11 after attack, proving that robustness cannot be automatically inherited through logits supervision; and robust distillation methods using robust teachers sometimes see declines (e.g., ARD drops from 78.33 to 74.03 on WavLM/XVector). The paper thus gives a clear engineering suggestion: unless a robust teacher is already available, it is not worth spending extra computation to create a robust teacher first.

**Student Capacity Ablation (Vertical comparison in Table 2)**: The slightly larger capacity XVector (80.4K) shows the phenomenon of "exceeding the baseline" earlier than TC-ResNet (67.7K)—RSLAD can only do so on XVector, indicating that at the 70K parameter level, output layer supervision is already insufficient; the more constrained the capacity, the greater the value of guidance at the representation level (VIC-KD); and VIC-KD stably exceeds the baseline on both students.

**Class Number Expansion (Fig. 3)**: Can also be viewed as a task difficulty ablation; the conclusion is the same—after difficulty increases, output layer methods (ARD) degrade fastest, while representation layer methods maintain their advantage.

Ablations not reported by the paper: Item-by-item ablation of the three VICReg terms (variance/invariance/covariance), sensitivity analysis of hyperparameter $\alpha$, and the impact of the projection head structure are not present in the main text.

## Main Contributions
1. **Methodological Level**: First time transferring the VICReg geometric regularization (Variance-Invariance-Covariance) from self-supervised joint embedding learning to adversarial robust distillation, proposing the use of geometric priors in the representation space to replace/supplement traditional output layer (logits/soft labels) robustness supervision, and verifying its effectiveness in the KWS scenario—filling the gap where "ARD/RSLAD cannot consistently surpass AT small model baselines."
2. **Recipe Level**: Proposing a training recipe of multi-view inputs superimposed with adversarial perturbations on the student branch, and systematically proving that multi-view brings gains to all four distillation methods (KD, ARD, RSLAD, VIC-KD), and revealing that the robustness advantages of existing methods partially depend on the unattributed component of multi-view.
3. **Experimental Evidence Level**: Under two settings of GSC 12-class and 35-class, and 8 configurations of dual teachers and dual students, robust accuracy exceeds the TRADES baseline by +4.1 to +6.0 percentage points in all cases (Table 2, Fig. 3), with the smallest clean-robust gap overall, while maintaining zero inference overhead; the abstract claims robust accuracy improvements of 12% and 8% for ARD/RSLAD (calculable from the main text as 8.0% and 6.6% for the Wav2Vec 2.0/XVector combination).
4. **Engineering Conclusion Level**: Proving that a standard fine-tuned teacher is sufficient to support robust distillation, eliminating the need to first adversarially train the teacher into a robust teacher, saving a whole round of teacher-level AT computation; combined with the explicit 4x training time cost, providing an honest cost account for subsequent deployment.

## Limitations and Future Work

### Technical Limitations of the Method
- **High Training Cost**: VIC-KD training time is four times that of RSLAD (Section 4.1). The additional forward pass brought by multi-view, the 10-step PGD-like attack generation per batch, and the teacher model forward pass are superimposed. Although inference has zero overhead, this is a practical obstacle for scenarios requiring frequent retraining (custom wake-up words).
- **Robust Accuracy Ceiling Still Exists**: The best student robust accuracy is 86.31 (Table 2), still nearly 10 percentage points away from its own clean accuracy of 95.92, and about 8.5 percentage points away from the robust teacher's own 94.79 (Table 1)—the distillation process still loses a considerable portion of robustness, and the paper does not explain where this loss goes.
- **Key Hyperparameters Not Disclosed**: The weight $\alpha$ for the convex combination of TRADES and VICReg is only mentioned at the Fig. 1 caption level, with values not reported in the main text; the hinge coefficients for the variance and covariance terms of VICReg are also not reported (pointing to original paper [17]); the structure of the projection head (number of layers, dimensions, non-linearity) is also not explained in the main text. Reproduction requires reverse engineering or contacting the authors.
- **Narrow Threat Model**: Only evaluated white-box AutoAttack (ensemble of APGD+APGD-T+FAB) with $\ell_\infty$ norm, $\epsilon = 1.5 \times 10^{-3}$. Training and evaluation used the same $\epsilon$; performance under mismatched budgets is not reported; perturbation imperceptibility is not quantified with metrics like SNR/PESQ; the over-the-air physical channel attack (UTIO) used to establish the premise in the introduction completely does not appear in experiments—while channel effects of air transmission (playback distortion, environmental reverberation) are exactly the real attack paths.
- **Multi-view Mechanism Remains Hypothetical**: The authors themselves mark the explanation that "mutual information maximization can be analogized to distillation" as requiring more research (Section 4.2); the mechanistic argument is not closed.

### Shortcomings in Experimental Design
- **Single Dataset, Single Language**: Only GSC v0.02 (approx. 100k 1-second English command words), no far-field data, streaming long audio, other languages, or real wake-up words (multi-syllable words at the "Hey Siri" level) for verification; the 1-second clip setting differs from real constant-listening scenarios.
- **No Statistical Significance Reported**: All numbers are results from single runs; the paper does not report multi-seed variance or confidence intervals, while several key gaps (e.g., RSLAD 78.25 vs baseline 78.27, Fig. 3) are small enough to potentially fall into the random fluctuation range.
- **Single Attack Type**: No black-box transfer attacks, no adaptive attacks designed specifically for VICReg geometric regularization—an attacker who knows the defense mechanism can directly optimize the invariance term; such adaptive threats are not tested.
- **Sparse Model Spectrum**: Only two students (67.7K/80.4K) and two teachers, no scanning of intermediate capacities, unable to answer the key question of "how the gain of geometric priors changes with student capacity" (can only extrapolate from two points).
- **Deployment Metrics are Proxies, Not Measured**: Only parameter count and MMACs are used as efficiency evidence; no actual measurements of latency, memory, and power consumption on real microcontrollers/DSPs; the paper also does not report discussions on the feasibility of running AutoAttack on edge devices.
- **Inconsistency Between Abstract and Main Text**: The abstract's 12%/8% and the main text's 8.0%/6.6% are not unified in口径, and the abstract numbers cannot be recalculated from a single configuration in Table 2, affecting readability.

### Possible Directions for Future Improvement
- **Threat Model Expansion**: Supplement over-the-air physical attack evaluation (replay channel, speaker distortion, environmental noise superposition), experiments with larger $\epsilon$ and mismatched budgets, attacks under perceptual constraints (limiting perturbation SNR), and stress testing with adaptive attacks targeting geometric regularization; this directly determines the credibility of the method in real speech security scenarios.
- **Mechanism Research**: Verify the mutual information hypothesis proposed by the authors, quantify the theoretical link between the invariance term and adversarial robustness (e.g., correlation between representation Lipschitz constant and robust accuracy), upgrading "geometric priors are effective" from empirical observation to interpretable laws.
- **Training Cost Reduction**: Cache and reuse adversarial samples, replace 10-step PGD with cheaper first-order attacks, offline pre-extraction of teacher representations (teacher output is invariant per epoch, so calculate once), asynchronous/low-frequency sampling of multi-views, aiming to compress the 4x overhead back to the RSLAD level.
- **Orthogonal Combination with Edge Compression Techniques**: The combination of VIC-KD with quantization, pruning, and binarization has not been explored—true MCU deployment requires the superposition of both, and quantization itself changes robustness; the robust accuracy after combination is an open question.
- **Hyperparameter and Component Ablation**: Supplement item-by-item ablation of the three VICReg terms, $\alpha$ sensitivity curves, and projection head structure ablation, clarifying how much robustness each of the three geometric terms contributes.
- **Scenario Expansion**: Streaming detection (frame-level decision rather than sentence classification), few-shot fine-tuning for custom wake-up words (the 4x training cost problem is most painful here), and generalization verification on multi-language and far-field reverberation data.
