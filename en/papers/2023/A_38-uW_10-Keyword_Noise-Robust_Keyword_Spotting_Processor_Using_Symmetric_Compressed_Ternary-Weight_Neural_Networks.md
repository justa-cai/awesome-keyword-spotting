## Problem Statement

### Problem Background and Domain Pain Points

Keyword Spotting (KWS) processors serve as the human-machine voice interface for battery-powered products such as wearable devices and mobile terminals. They must operate in an "always-on" listening mode year-round, compressing the power budget to the microwatt level. A typical digital Domain Deep Neural Network (DNN) KWS system consists of three stages (Fig. 1(a)): microphone pickup, Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC) feature extraction, and neural network classification to output keywords. In hardware implementation, MFCC computation, data and weight storage, and neural network operations account for the vast majority of energy consumption.

One of the most valuable observations in this paper is the quantitative breakdown of the energy structure: FFT computation accounts for 62.1% of the power consumption of the MFCC module, while MFCC itself accounts for 33.8% of the total system power consumption (cited from [23], which is statistical data from the authors' previous generation 22nm chip). These two numbers directly indicate the priority of optimization directions—if you want to reduce system power consumption, you must first reduce FFT, then reduce neural network computation and storage.

The second background pain point is the complexity of real-world usage scenarios. The paper explicitly sets the design goal as "diverse application scenarios": it must not only support up to 10 keywords (plus silence and unknown classes, totaling 12 output classes) but also maintain high recognition rates across a wide Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) range from 5dB to clean speech, under 11 different background noises. This is the key difference from many prior works that only tested clean speech, and it is also the root cause of the difficulty in power control—noise robustness usually requires deeper networks, finer weight granularity, and noise-augmented training to achieve, all of which increase power consumption and storage.

The third pain point comes from standby behavior: in the always-on listening input stream, the vast majority of speech frames are redundant frames containing no keywords. Performing complete MFCC and neural network computations on these redundant frames is a pure waste of energy. However, skipping these frames in streaming hardware without missing detections requires coordinated design of Voice Activity Detection (VAD) and the data path.

### Specific Shortcomings of Existing Methods

The paper reviews low-power KWS chips from that era using specific power consumption and keyword counts, pointing out their shortcomings one by one. First, K. Kim's ISSCC'22 work [11] uses a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) with a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) structure as the classifier, with a digital backend power consumption of 13.6μW, which is relatively high. Second, Laika (ESSCIRC'18) [1] uses one layer of LSTM plus one fully connected layer, with a classifier power consumption of 5μW, but it is only suitable for near-field microphone scenarios, and this digital number does not include MFCC power consumption; its noise resistance and keyword count are also limited. Third, VoCell (JSSC'20) [5] achieved 10 keywords at 10.6μW, but it was only tested on clean speech (Table II shows its noise support is limited to Clean). Fourth, Shan's ISSCC'20 work [14] used a binarized Depthwise Separable Convolutional Neural Network (DSCNN) to reduce power consumption to 0.51μW, representing the extreme of low power, but it could only support one or two keywords, with category capabilities severely limited by binarization.

At the methodological level, the paper further points out two deeper shortcomings. The first is the dilemma between quantization granularity and storage: Binarized Weight Networks (BWN) compress weights to 1 bit and eliminate multiplication, but suffer from severe information loss—the paper's own comparative experiments (Fig. 1(c)) show that under the same network skeleton, BWN achieves only 87.9% recognition rate on clean speech and drops to 80.8% at 5dB noise, whereas Ternary-Weight Networks (TWN) reach 90.6% and 85.4%, respectively, with gaps of 2.6 to 4.6 percentage points. Ternary quantization (Equation (1)) uses a scaling factor $a$ combined with three values +1, 0, and −1 to represent weights, offering expressive power close to finer quantization. The 0 value does not participate in multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations, naturally bringing sparsity, but each weight requires 2 bits of storage, directly doubling the storage compared to BWN—thus, "how to obtain the precision benefits of ternary values without paying the storage cost" remains an unresolved issue. The second is the lack of compensation for approximate computing: DNNs are naturally fault-tolerant, and using approximate adders/multipliers to trade off for power is a common approach, but existing works are basically "single-unit designs," i.e., isolating the optimization of accuracy-area trade-offs for a single approximate unit, ignoring the system-level opportunity that errors from different approximate units in an adder tree can cancel each other out (Fig. 1(d) demonstrates using the truth table of two 1-bit approximate half-adders that erroneous results when both inputs are 1 can be corrected to exact values through mutual compensation). Error compensation is essentially a probabilistic event; achieving complete compensation naturally is difficult, requiring a systematic circuit design method for compensation, which was a blank space at the time.

Furthermore, FFT implementation itself involves粗放 (rough) waste: in serial pipeline FFTs, the output data bit-width of butterflies at each stage grows stepwise (Fig. 1(b) illustrates the difference between the maximum and mean values at each stage using the speech "left" as an example). If all stages uniformly use 16 bits as is conventional practice, the earlier stages will be over-designed, yet these earlier stages occupy the most hardware resources.

### Key Challenges to be Solved by This Paper

In summary, this paper aims to tackle three hard bones simultaneously: First, at the feature extraction end, how to precisely determine the data bit-width for each stage of the serial pipeline FFT based on the varying data scale characteristics, cutting redundant hardware in the earlier stages while keeping SNR degradation controllable; Second, at the network end, how to maintain high precision for ternary-weight networks in multi-keyword, wide-SNR scenarios while pushing back the storage and computation overhead brought by 2-bit weights, even reverse-utilizing the inherent repetitive weight phenomenon of ternarization for algorithm-level compression and architecture-level data reuse; Third, at the circuit end, how to further save power using configurable approximate adder trees in the new landscape where multiplication has been eliminated by ternarization and addition has become the main source of computational power consumption, and ensure that errors from different approximate units cancel each other out according to design intent rather than stacking up. These three tasks correspond to the paper's three core technologies (SBSA, SKT, CGP error mutual compensation adder tree), and must be jointly optimized end-to-end to compress real-time 10-keyword recognition into 3.8μW under 22nm process, 0.54V, and 250kHz conditions.

## Methodology

### Overall Architecture Design and Design Motivation

The overall system architecture (Fig. 3(a)) follows the frame-length adaptive framework proposed by the authors previously [27], consisting of three cascaded modules: SNR-based Voice Activity Detection (VAD) unit, MFCC extraction module based on optimized FFT, and TWN accelerator. Input speech is sampled at 8kHz, framed with a 40ms frame length and 20ms frame shift, cutting 1 second of speech into 49 frames; each frame extracts 26 Mel coefficients, forming a 26×49 feature map fed into the network (Fig. 4).

The network model is a hybrid structure of fully convolutional and fully connected layers (Fig. 4): Input 26×49, passes through 4 3×3 convolutional layers (channel numbers annotated in the figure are 28@24×24, 24@22×22, 16@20×10, 12@9×8 respectively, with different stride combinations for time and frequency dimensions to shrink the feature map layer by layer), followed by 4 fully connected layers (neuron counts 30, 16, 16, finally outputting 12 classes), with Batch Normalization (BN) and ReLU inserted between layers. The paper specifically explains why it adopts more fully connected layers than other KWS processors: the purpose is to improve classification accuracy and noise robustness—a clear design decision of "trading network capacity for robustness." The astronomical figure of 4930K operations per frame in Table II is the direct consequence of this decision. Model structure information also reveals a key optimization target: Conv2 and Conv3 layers account for 75.58% and 19.83% of all operations, totaling over 95%, and are accompanied by massive storage accesses. Therefore, all subsequent optimizations targeting network layers (symmetric kernel training, data reuse) fall precisely on these two layers. This engineering approach of "choosing the battlefield based on operation proportion" runs through the entire paper.

In terms of design methodology, the biggest characteristic of this paper is the three-layer linkage: algorithm layer (sparse-controlled ternarization plus symmetric kernel training), architecture layer (weight compression coding, dedicated data flow, and PE array), and circuit layer (approximate adder tree) interlock with each other. Symmetry is an algorithmic property learned during training, but its existence is almost entirely for hardware—weight symmetry allows storage to keep only half and allows the PE array to reuse partial sums. This approach of "training the network for hardware properties" is fundamentally different from model compression research on the pure algorithm side.

### Mathematical Principles of Core Algorithms

The ternary quantization function (Equation (1)) is defined as a piecewise mapping: when weight $W$ is greater than threshold $\delta$, it is quantized to $+a$; when less than $-\delta$, it is quantized to $-a$; when the absolute value does not exceed $\delta$, it is quantized to 0, where $\delta$ is the threshold and $a$ is the scaling factor. The scaling factor multiplied by ±1, 0 restores the weight magnitude.

FFT bit-width optimization uses Signal-to-Quantization-Noise Ratio (SQNR, Equation (2)) as the constraint metric: first, calculate the exact N-point FFT result $X(k)$ using double-precision floating point, compute the Mean Squared Error (MSE) with the approximate result after bit-width truncation, and define SQNR as ten times the logarithm of the ratio of the square of the maximum amplitude of the result to the MSE. A larger SQNR indicates smaller error introduced by truncation. Using it as the criterion for whether the bit-width of each stage can be reduced puts "how much hardware is saved" and "how much signal is damaged" into the same computable dimension.

Error modeling for approximate adders uses Error Distance (ED) and Sum of Error Distances (SED): ED is defined as the absolute value of the difference between the exact result and the approximate result (the Error definition in Equation (4)), and SED is the cumulative sum of ED under all input combinations. For a 4-bit adder, there are 16×16 = 256 input combinations for the two addends, which can be enumerated. The fitness function (Equation (3)) for CGP search is Fitness = $SED_{CA} - 2 \times SED_{ORA} + Cost$, where $SED_{ORA}$ is the SED obtained by exhaustive simulation of the OR-gate approximate adder used as a baseline, $SED_{CA}$ is the SED of the compensation adder to be generated, and Cost (Equation (5)) is a hard constraint—taking 0 only when the generated circuit has non-negative errors under all input combinations, and taking infinity if any negative error appears. This design directly writes two engineering intents into the search objective: "the total error magnitude of the compensation adder should be approximately equal to twice the OR adder error" (explained by the paper as the inherent error of the two addends) and "the compensation direction must be constant," forcing evolution to generate circuits that can cancel out errors with the OR adder.

### Key Technical Innovation 1: Stepwise Bit-Width Selection Algorithm (SBSA) for Serial Pipeline FFT

FFT structure selection itself carries power considerations: parallel structures are fast but hardware-heavy, storage-based cyclic structures save logic but have high memory access, and serial pipeline (here using 512-point R2SDF-FFT, i.e., Radix-2 Single-Path Delay Feedback structure) offers the best balance between hardware resources and throughput, suitable for lightweight KWS. The core observation is: a complete N-point Radix-2 FFT decomposes into $\log_2 N$ stages of butterflies, where butterfly operations at each stage do not interfere with each other, so each stage can use different data bit-widths for calculation—this opens the door to customizing bit-widths on demand. A deeper insight is the misalignment between resources and error sensitivity: in serial pipeline structures, earlier stages occupy more hardware resources (longer delay feedback chains) but are more tolerant to errors, so the optimization space and benefit of earlier stages are greater than later stages; later stages have data amplitudes growing stepwise (Fig. 1(b)), so their bit-width requirements are naturally higher. The correct posture for optimization is "aggressive early, stable late."

The specific process of SBSA (Algorithm 1) is a greedy stepwise downward search: initially, the bit-width of each stage is set to 16 (fixed-point format, 9 decimal places, inherited from the authors' previous work [27]), and a tolerable error boundary SQNR bound is set. Starting from the first stage, while keeping the bit-widths of other stages unchanged, gradually reduce the bit-width of the current stage and calculate SQNR. Once it drops below the boundary, stop and set the bit-width of that stage to the last feasible value. Then, set the lower bound of the next stage's bit-width to the current stage's bit-width (theoretically, the minimum bit-width of the k-th stage is the bit-width of the previous stage's output, because data width can only grow stepwise), continue optimizing the next stage, until the ninth stage.

The greedy result for a single speech cannot be directly generalized to the entire dataset. Therefore, the paper randomly extracts 1000 original speeches of different keywords from GSCD and runs SBSA on each, statistically analyzing the distribution of bit-width selection for each stage (Fig. 2, SQNR bound set to 50dB). The results show that the bit-width requirements for the first two stages are low and highly consistent, diverging from the third stage—speeches of different complexities have different bit-width requirements for each stage; by the ninth stage, 25% of speeches can use 14 bits, 65% can use 15 bits, and the remaining 10% must use 16 bits to maintain precision. To turn this distribution into a single implementation, the paper introduces a bit-width threshold T: if the proportion of speeches choosing the highest bit-width $m$ at the k-th stage is not less than T, that stage takes $m$, otherwise it takes $m-1$. Combining the judgment that "earlier stages have greater benefits and are less sensitive to errors," T is set to 0.1 for the first five stages (more aggressive, tolerating 90% of speeches being reduced by one bit-width) and 0 for the last four stages (most conservative, taking the highest bit-width if even one speech requires it). The final bit-width sequence is [10, 10, 11, 12, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16]—compared to the uniform design of all 16 bits, the first two stages directly cut 6 bits, and precisely these earlier stages occupy the most registers and computational resources. The engineering judgment reflected here is: bit-width is not a precision parameter but a statistical parameter, and should be selected based on dataset distribution, not worst-case scenarios.

Above bit-width optimization, a frame-level gating layer is stacked (Fig. 3): The SNR-based VAD unit uses short-term energy and dual thresholds (Threshold1/Threshold2 in Fig. 3(b)) to predict whether the input frame contains speech. Frames with energy below the threshold are judged as non-speech frames, directly outputting 0 to the TWN accelerator, bypassing MFCC calculation entirely. The skip-0 unit in the control module further allows the neural network classifier to skip calculations for all-zero frames, and operations with massive 0 weights can also be skipped (this directly benefits from the 33.6% weight sparsity brought by ternarization). The effect (annotated in Fig. 3(b)) is that only about 40ms of effective working time is needed per second, spreading the average power consumption of always-on listening by duty cycle.

### Key Technical Innovation 2: Sparse-Controlled Ternarization and Symmetric Kernel Training (SKT) and Its Accelerator

The first step on the training side is to transform ternary quantization itself. In traditional TWN, the threshold $\delta$ and scaling factor $a$ are not trainable, making it impossible to obtain a high-sparsity weight matrix by tuning the threshold; Fan et al.'s Truncated Gaussian Approximation (TGA) [28] was the first to achieve joint training of quantization thresholds and weights, minimizing precision loss caused by model compression, but sparsity is uncontrollable. This paper introduces a tunable sparsity parameter $Sl$ to scale the threshold $\delta$ on the basis of TGA. Quantization updates proceed in two steps (Fig. 5(a)): Step 1 calculates the ternary weight threshold based on the current layer's weights and sparsity parameter; Step 2 updates weights according to the new threshold. The transformed quantization scheme increases weight sparsity to 33.6%, which is 6.5 times that of the full-precision model—note the causality here: sparsity is not a byproduct of training but a design variable explicitly controlled by $Sl$. 0 weights do not generate MAC operations or memory accesses in hardware, so sparsity is directly exchanged for computation volume.

The second step is Symmetric Kernel Training (SKT, Fig. 5(b)). The motivation is direct: increasing repetitive weights is an effective way to improve DNN accelerator energy efficiency, and in ternary networks, the phenomenon of repetitive weights is already significant—a 3×3 convolution kernel naturally has 33.3% repetitive weights when weights satisfy a uniform distribution; after symmetrization, weights at two symmetric positions only need to store one, and symmetric kernels can trigger dedicated data reuse data flows. The SKT training loop is: at epoch 0, initialize weights, thresholds, and scaling factors using a pre-trained network; at epoch t, perform weight symmetrization first, then ternarization based on the structure from the previous round. Symmetrization can be implemented using two operators: taking the maximum or taking the average. After experimental comparison, the average is selected—taking the average of weights at symmetric positions yields higher precision. The ternarization threshold is calculated based on the mean $\mu_l$, variance $\sigma_l$, and sparsity parameter $s_l$ of all weights in each layer. Another detail that completely removes multiplication from the data path: the scaling factor $a$ of convolutional and fully connected layers is extracted and folded into the BN layer, so that multiplication between weights and activations only has three cases: ±1 and 0, which can be completed in hardware using NAND gates and adders. The overall cost of SKT is a precision loss of less than 1% under different background noises.

Symmetry is not one but three modes (Fig. 6): horizontal symmetry, vertical symmetry, and diagonal symmetry, each further divided into single-weight symmetry (only two corresponding position weights are equal) and group-weight symmetry (multiple consecutive weights are identical). Since single-weight symmetry can produce very little data reuse in hardware, the paper focuses on group-weight symmetry. Ablation data (Fig. 6) provides three key decision bases: First, the natural TWN without SKT has 34.59% single-weight and 6.17% group-weight symmetry, with a recognition rate of 92.2%; applying SKT to Conv2 and Conv3 increases the group-weight symmetry rate from 6.17% to 66.46%, and single-weight symmetry from 34.59% to 89.48%, with only a 0.5% drop in recognition rate—this trade is very worthwhile. Second, if SKT is applied to Conv1 simultaneously, the symmetry rate increases only slightly while the precision loss is as high as 4.3%, so SKT is only used on Conv2 and Conv3—this echoes the earlier targeted judgment that "Conv2/Conv3 account for 95.41% of operations": paying the precision cost of symmetrization only on the two heaviest layers. Third, horizontal symmetry has the highest precision of 91.9%, which is 0.7% higher than vertical (91.2%) and 1.2% higher than diagonal (90.7%), so all symmetric kernels uniformly adopt horizontal symmetry.

The differences in hardware data reuse among the three symmetry modes (Fig. 7) explain why horizontal wins: Diagonal symmetry of convolution weights is symmetric in both time and frequency domains, making it difficult to generate a large amount of reusable data during convolution, making it unsuitable for hardware deployment; output reuse of vertical symmetry kernels only occurs when the filter moves along the time domain (i.e., different input frames), making reuse opportunities difficult to capture; horizontal symmetry kernels, however, when the frame sequence is fed into the PE array for convolution in FIFO order, the first row of weights can explicitly reuse the third row of weights (frequency domain weight reuse) in one convolution, and whenever the kernel moves twice in the frequency domain, the previously calculated output can be reused—both weight and output reuse can be stably realized within the pipeline.

The accelerator architecture (Fig. 8(a)) is customized around these reuse opportunities: 30 PEs form a 5×6 array, equipped with a main control unit to schedule weight and data loading/storage, a weight decoding unit to decompress encoded weights, a post-processing unit to calculate BN and ReLU, and on-chip SRAM—Data SRAM 8.96KB (16-bit width), Weight SRAM 4.29KB, Bias SRAM 0.39KB, totaling 14KB. Weight compression uses a combination of Huffman-Run-Length Coding (H-RLC) (Fig. 8(b)): 1-bit Flag indicates whether the weight is encoded, 2 bits store the ternary weight itself (encoding mapping 0→2'b00, +1→2'b01, −1→2'b11), and the repetition count is represented by the Coding Table of Huffman coding—Run-Length Coding is good at long strings of repetition, and sparsification and symmetrization happen to create massive strings of 0s and repetitions, while Huffman further compresses the overhead of "how many times repeated." The decoding end only uses a finite state machine plus a counter to sequentially output repeated weights and valid signals (Fig. 8(c)), with extremely low decompression complexity. This coding saves 2.74KB of weight storage, and SKT brings an additional compression of 1.55KB. Architecturally, a 1-bit "weights info" flag bit is attached after each 3×3 convolution kernel, indicating whether the kernel is symmetric; symmetric kernels only load one set of weights, further improving memory access efficiency. The comprehensive effect is: the power consumption of the TWN accelerator optimized based on SKT is 18.6% lower than the version without SKT.

The data flow design (Fig. 9) realizes the reuse of horizontal symmetry at the cycle level: without symmetry, one PE processes three weights and data per clock cycle, and a 3×3 convolution is completed by a group of 3 PEs in one clock cycle; at clock cycle t+1, the weights remain unchanged, the input data of PE1 is passed to PE0, the input of PE2 is passed to PE1, and PE2 gets new data, forming a data-shifting pipeline; with horizontal symmetry, in convolutions with a frequency domain step of 1, PE0 will have a repeated partial sum every two data movements, so at clock cycle t+2, PE0 does not calculate directly, and the output partial sum is taken from the value stored in the "reuse output" register of PE2 at clock cycle t—exchanging one clock cycle of calculation for one register read. In terms of blocking strategy, the 30 PEs are divided into 10 groups of three, processing 10 output channels simultaneously. Data is broadcast to the 10 groups, and input channels are processed sequentially within groups, using time-division multiplexing to digest convolutions with large numbers of input and output channels.

### Key Technical Innovation 3: CGP Error Mutual Compensation Approximate Adder Tree

After ternarization eliminates multiplication, addition operations become the main source of computational power consumption, constituting the motivation for approximate adder design. The paper first provides a statistical insight: in DNNs, positive and negative errors following a uniform distribution are more likely to cancel each other out during computation, thereby achieving higher precision—meaning that the shape of the error distribution of the approximate circuit is more worth designing than the magnitude of the error itself.

The structure of the approximate adder tree (Fig. 10(a)): The 9 partial sums of a 3×3 convolution kernel form a two-level adder tree. The compensation block in each level consists of two different approximate adders, mutually compensating for each other's errors. To verify feasibility, the paper first uses a 32-bit low-order OR-gate adder (LOA) [30] to accumulate 106 random data, evaluating with Error Distance ED (Fig. 10(c)). The result is that the ED of the adder tree is lower than that of the traditional accumulator, and the ED of the error mutual compensation adder tree is the lowest—proving that tree organization plus heterogeneous combination indeed statistically cancels errors. The implemented 16-bit adder (Fig. 10(b)) uses a full adder for the high 8 bits to ensure high-bit correctness, and the low 8 bits are divided into blocks of 4 bits, configurable as exact or approximate mode (controlled by Approximate_EN, retaining flexibility for precision fallback); among them, approximate block 1 consists of 4 OR-gate approximate adders without internal carry but with external carry, and approximate block 2 is the compensation adder (CA) searched out by CGP—extremely simple structure: 4 wires connect input A directly to output Sum, plus a fixed external carry of 1. Compared to the full adder, the OR-gate adder and CA have fewer transistors and shorter delay.

The generation of CA uses Cartesian Genetic Programming (CGP): each chromosome is a 2D graph structure, with node functions modeled as Boolean functions, which has been proven to generate high-quality approximate circuits [31]. The search process involves simulating the error distribution of the 4-bit OR-gate adder block and feeding it to the fitness function, while taking the target error characteristics as search parameters. The fitness drives $SED_{CA}$ to approach $2 \times SED_{ORA}$ according to Equation (3), and the Cost term strictly excludes any candidate circuit with negative errors, ensuring that the error distribution of the compensation adder is entirely positive, pointing exactly in the compensation direction opposite to the OR adder. The generated heterogeneous adder tree's error distribution is closer to a uniform distribution than the original approximate adder, achieving simultaneous optimization of computational precision and hardware efficiency. Circuit-level evaluation is conducted under TSMC 22nm process, 0.54V voltage, and 250kHz clock: the approximate adder tree brings a 17.3% power consumption reduction to the TWN accelerator (from 3.24μW to 2.68μW, Fig. 11), with a precision loss of 0.5% (clean speech) to 1.3% (5dB)—on the software baseline, the recognition rate of the exact adder version is 91.7%/90.5%/87.4% (Clean/15dB/5dB), and the approximate version is 91.2%/89.8%/86.1% (Fig. 11). A notable detail is that the heavier the noise, the greater the loss, indicating that approximate errors and noise errors overlap in the direction of degradation, which is also one of the reasons for choosing to approximate only the low 8 bits.

### Technical Differences with Existing Methods

Compared to the binarization route [14], the choice here is "ternary for precision": on the task of 12-class multi-keyword plus wide SNR, the information bottleneck of BWN is directly reflected in the 2.6 to 4.6 percentage point precision gap in Fig. 1(c); while the 0 value of ternary creates sparsity without the overhead of doubling storage like extra precision bits, combined with SKT and H-RLC, it actually compresses storage to 4.29KB weight SRAM. Compared to the floating-point or high-bit-width LSTM route [1][5][11], this paper uses 2-bit weights, approximate addition, and 0.54V near-threshold voltage to squeeze a larger network with million-level operations per frame into 3.8μW—the direction of cost and benefit is completely different: the former uses small networks (43K~53K ops/frame) with high bit-width, while this paper uses a large network (4930K ops/frame, Table II) with extremely low bit-width and approximate circuits. Essentially, it buys noise robustness with network depth and the fine-grained adjustment capability of ternary weights, and then pays the power bill through circuit-algorithm协同 (collaborative) optimization. The third difference is symmetric kernel training itself: letting the network converge towards "weight symmetry," a pure hardware property, during the training phase, locking model compression, data reuse, and memory access optimization at the algorithm level at once, rather than doing post-pruning or compression after training; the fourth difference is that approximate computing moves from "single-unit optimal" to "system compensation optimal," using CGP to automatically design a compensator with hard constraints on error direction, pairing it with existing approximate units, which has no precedent in previous KWS chip designs.

## Experimental Results

### Datasets Used and Their Scale

Training and validation use the Google Speech Command Dataset (GSCD), containing 105K audio clips of 1-second length, covering 35 keywords. The noise robustness evaluation for chip testing uses all 11 background noises from the NoiseX-92 corpus (Babble, Buccaneer1, Buccaneer2, Destroyerops, Factory1, Factory2, M109, Machinegun, Pink, Volvo, White, Table I), combined with three SNR intensity levels of 5dB, 10dB, 15dB, plus clean speech, totaling four conditions. The bit-width statistical experiment for SBSA randomly extracted 1000 original speeches of different keywords from GSCD separately. It should be pointed out that the paper also mentions that to improve noise robustness, speech feature extraction under each background noise was mixed into the training set—meaning that robustness partly comes from noise-augmented training data, not solely from the architecture.

### Definition and Rationale of Evaluation Metrics

Recognition accuracy is reported by noise type and SNR level, which directly corresponds to the design goal of "11 noises, wide range from 5dB~Clean," and is the most core acceptance metric of this paper. FFT bit-width optimization uses SQNR (Equation (2)) to measure quantization noise. The reason for selection is that it puts the impact of bit-width truncation into the signal quality dimension, establishing a computable boundary between hardware overhead and precision damage. Approximate adders are measured using ED/SED (Equation (4)). The reason is that adder errors can be precisely characterized after enumerating the input space, making them suitable as fitness for CGP search. Chip-level metrics include power consumption, area, delay, and normalized energy efficiency. Normalized energy efficiency is defined as (delay × power) / baseline (Table II note, baseline taken as JSSC'20 [5]). The reason for choosing this composite metric is to fairly compare different chips across processes and voltages—looking only at power consumption ignores response speed differences, and looking only at delay ignores power consumption differences; the product form binds the two together to measure "how much energy is burned and how long one waits to complete one recognition."

### Detailed Comparison with Baseline Methods and SOTA

The chip is taped out in TSMC 22nm Ultra-Low-Leakage (ULL) process, using High Threshold Voltage (HVT) transistors. The test platform includes power supply, oscilloscope, microphone, PC, and test board with chip socket: the PC writes weight parameters to the chip via UART, and an external digital silicon microphone SPH0645 collects speech and sends it to the chip via I2S interface (Fig. 12). The paper tested a total of 30 chips, with an average power consumption of 3.8μW~150μW within the operating range of 0.54V~0.9V and 250kHz~10MHz; the prototype system operating point is set at 0.54V, 250kHz, achieving minimum power consumption of 3.8μW, on-chip storage of 14KB, core area of 0.99×0.61mm², die area of 1mm², and MFCC part synthesized into 580,000 NAND2 equivalent logic gates (Fig. 12).

Measured accuracy (Table I): 90.6% on clean speech; under 11 noises, all exceed 89.1% at 15dB, all exceed 88% at 10dB, and all exceed 85% at 5dB. The fluctuation range at 5dB is 85.0% (Destroyerops, Machinegun) to 86.4% (Pink, Volvo), with an overall average of about 85.4% (abstract口径). Compared to the software baseline, the chip measurement has a slight precision loss of no more than 1%, attributed by the paper to subtle differences between the test environment and the software simulation environment.

Comparison with SOTA (Table II): This paper (22nm, TWN, data/weight 16/2 bits, 4930K ops/frame, 250kHz, delay 16ms, 0.54V, layout area 0.60mm², 14KB storage, 3.8μW) compared with Laika (65nm, LSTM, 43K ops, 5μW but excluding MFCC power, only 4 words, Clean 91.2%), VoCell (65nm, LSTM, 4(8)/8 bits, 53K ops, 10.6μW, 10 words, Clean 90.9%), Shan ISSCC'20 (28nm, binarized DSCNN, 1/1 bits, 150K ops, 0.41V, 0.51μW, only 1~2 words, Clean 98.0%, 5dB 90.7%), Kim ISSCC'22 (65nm, RNN, 14/8 bits, 48K ops, 13.6μW, 10 words, Clean 86.0%). In terms of normalized energy efficiency, this paper reaches 2.79× (10 words), higher than Laika's 2.12× (4 words), VoCell's 1× (10 words), and Kim's 1.01× (10 words); the only higher one is Shan's 5.20×, but it only supports 1~2 keywords. In terms of noise support dimension, only this paper and Shan support 5dB~Clean, while Shan is only valid under single-word vocabulary; among works with 10 keywords, this paper is the only one to simultaneously achieve wide SNR support and minimum power consumption (3.8μW). The paper attributes high robustness to two points: more layers brought by a deeper network architecture, and finer-grained weight adjustment capability provided by ternary weights; plus training with a training set mixed with feature extraction under each background noise.

Power and Area Breakdown (Fig. 13, Fig. 14, post-layout simulation at 0.54V): At the processor level, TWN accelerator accounts for 70.42% power and 58.00% area, MFCC accounts for 18.25% power and 24.10% area, top-level control accounts for 7.25%/9.27%, and the rest accounts for 4.31%/8.63%; the ratio of leakage to dynamic power for the entire processor is 42.7%/57.2%. Inside the TWN accelerator, data storage is the largest power source (38.84%), weight storage accounts for 21.38%, PE array accounts for 20.45%, activation unit 11.07%, control 8.26%; while in area, the PE array is largest (55.67%), data storage 20.51%, weight storage 12.58%, control 5.90%, activation 5.34%; leakage/dynamic inside the accelerator is 40.9%/59.1%. The cross-interpretation of these two sets of numbers is very informative: SBSA compresses the MFCC power proportion to 18.25% (compared to the statistic that MFCC accounted for 33.8% of the system in previous works), SKT compression makes both the area and power of weight storage significantly lower than data storage, while data storage, because it needs to cache calculation results layer by layer, becomes the new power head—this points the direction for subsequent optimization.

### Findings from Ablation Experiments

The paper's ablation chain covers the switch cost of every technology. Network level: BWN compared to TWN (Fig. 1(c)), clean speech 87.9% vs 90.6%, 5dB 80.8% vs 85.4%, establishing the necessity of the ternary route. SKT layer ablation (Fig. 6): baseline recognition rate 92.2% with natural symmetry rates of 34.59% (single) / 6.17% (group); applying SKT only to Conv2/Conv3 increases symmetry rates to 89.48%/66.46%, with a precision loss of 0.5%; including Conv1 expands the precision loss to 4.3% with no significant increase in symmetry rate—thereby locking the optimal layer combination of Conv2/Conv3. This ablation also explains "why not all layers": symmetrization is a strong constraint added to the weight distribution, and feature extraction undertaken by weights in shallow layers (Conv1) is more sensitive to perturbations. Symmetry mode ablation (Fig. 6): horizontal 91.9%, vertical 91.2%, diagonal 90.7%; after superimposing hardware reuse feasibility analysis, horizontal is selected. Approximate adder tree ablation (Fig. 11): TWN accelerator power drops from 3.24μW to 2.68μW (−17.3%), with precision loss increasing from 0.5% on clean speech to 1.3% at 5dB. SKT architecture benefits: accelerator power reduced by 18.6%, H-RLC saves 2.74KB, SKT extra compression 1.55KB. The final level of ablation is chip measurement vs software baseline, with a gap of less than 1%, verifying the fidelity from algorithm simulation to silicon.

## Main Contributions

First, this is the first taped-out KWS processor claimed by the paper to achieve high-precision real-time recognition of 10 keywords (12 classes) at 3.8μW power consumption, simultaneously supporting 11 background noises and a wide SNR range of 5dB~Clean, satisfying the three previously mutually restrictive requirements of "multi-keyword," "noise resistance," and "ultra-low power" for the first time. Second, it proposes the Stepwise Bit-Width Selection Algorithm (SBSA) for serial pipeline FFT, using SQNR boundary plus stepwise greedy plus dataset statistical distribution (1000 GSCD speeches, 50dB boundary, T=0.1 for first five stages, T=0 for last four stages) to customize the 512-point FFT bit-width from uniform 16 bits to [10,10,11,12,12,13,14,15,16], directly hitting the soft spot that FFT accounts for 62.1% of MFCC power. Third, it proposes a lossy configurable compressed ternary-weight network with sparse control (33.6% sparsity, 6.5 times that of full-precision model) plus symmetric kernel training (SKT), combined with H-RLC coding and dedicated data reuse data flow, reducing accelerator power by 18.6% and significantly compressing weight storage under the premise of precision loss less than 1%. Fourth, it pioneers a CGP-based error mutual compensation scheme, using a fitness function with hard constraints of all-positive errors to automatically search for compensation adders, pairing them with OR-gate approximate adders to form heterogeneous approximate adder trees, further reducing power by 17.3% while controlling precision loss within 0.5%~1.3%. Fifth, 22nm taped-out measurement shows normalized energy efficiency reaching 2.79×, exceeding all previous 10-keyword works.

## Limitations and Future Work

### Technical Limitations of the Method

The most obvious limitation is the computational scale: 4930K operations per frame (Table II) is about 93 times that of VoCell (53K) and about 115 times that of Laika (43K). This number indicates that the noise robustness of this paper is largely built up using network capacity, and the power advantage relies on four means: ternary weights eliminating multiplication, approximate addition, 0.54V near-threshold voltage, and 250kHz low frequency, making each single operation extremely cheap. Once the task is upgraded or voltage/frequency is restricted, the extrapolability of this path is questionable. Secondly, absolute precision is not dominant: clean speech 90.6% is lower than Shan's 98.0% (although it only has 1~2 words) and Laika's 91.2% (4 words), and 5dB 85.4% is also lower than Shan's 90.7%—the precision tax of ternarization plus two levels of lossy compression is clearly visible in the comparison table. Thirdly, lossy links are superimposed: SKT takes 0.5%, approximate adder tree takes 0.5%~1.3%, and chip measurement takes another ~1%. All three layers of loss are pressed onto the final precision, and Fig. 11 shows that the heavier the noise, the greater the approximate loss, meaning that approximate errors and noise errors are not independent. Fourthly, among the three symmetry modes, only horizontal is truly implemented; diagonal symmetry is abandoned because dual-domain symmetry is difficult to schedule, and vertical symmetry is difficult to realize because output reuse only occurs across frames, narrowing the freedom of algorithm design to a single point in the face of hardware reality. Fifthly, the operating point of 0.54V near-threshold plus HVT devices is naturally sensitive to process-voltage-temperature (PVT) fluctuations, and the paper does not report precision drift under fluctuations. Sixthly, VAD relies on preset energy dual thresholds, posing risks of false triggering and missed detection in non-stationary noise or low SNR environments, and the paper does not report any false alarm rate or ROC-like metrics, leaving a key piece of evidence missing for the actual usability of always-on systems.

### Shortcomings in Experimental Design

Although noise evaluation covers 11 noises, they all come from NoiseX-92, a corpus recorded decades ago, which has spectral characteristic gaps with contemporary real-world home, street, and office noises. On the speech side, the 8kHz narrowband sampling plus near-field setting of external silicon microphones does not cover far-field, reverberation, or multi-speaker scenarios. Regarding dataset scope, the paper does not list the final selected 10 keywords, nor does it report the confusion matrix for unknown and silence classes, making it impossible for readers to judge where the errors at 85.4%@5dB specifically fall. Training details are almost entirely absent: the number of training epochs, learning rate, value of sparsity parameter $Sl$, population size and time-consuming of CGP search are not reported, resulting in high cost for method reproduction. Although the definition of normalized energy efficiency (delay × power / baseline) provides an anchor for cross-design comparison, it does not perform process normalization (the advantage of 22nm over 65nm is directly counted in),打折 (discounting) the fairness of cross-generation comparison. The test covers 30 chips, giving a range of 3.8~150μW, but does not provide inter-chip distribution statistics, making the yield availability of the minimum power point unclear.

### Possible Directions for Future Improvement

Looking forward along the paper's own power breakdown, the most direct next step is data storage: 38.84% of accelerator power comes from data SRAM (weight storage has another 21.38%), totaling about 60% storage power, meaning that Processing-in-Memory (PIM) architectures (one of the corresponding author Fan's research directions) or deeper data reuse scheduling still have large space. The framework of approximate computing can be extrapolated: currently, compensation is only done in the low 8 bits of the 16-bit adder and the two levels of the adder tree. Extending the CGP compensation scheme to higher bit segments and extending to other operators (such as BN post-processing, activation) is a natural extension; combined with the existing configurable mechanism of Approximate_EN, runtime precision-energy adaptation can be achieved, opening exact mode in quiet environments and switching as needed in noisy environments. On the algorithm side, sparsity parameter $Sl$ and symmetry mode are already training-phase variables; further, they can be made configurable deployment items, achieving one training for multiple deployment levels. On the evaluation side, adding false alarm rate, modern noise corpora, and far-field reverberation tests is necessary to ground the claim of "noise robustness" into the acceptance standards of always-on products. Finally, the large network route of 4930K ops/frame and the small network route of 0.51μW [14] are not the end points. Performing Pareto search on keyword count-robustness-power between the two—such as replacing ordinary convolution with more efficient architectures (binarized depthwise separable convolution)—may be the main battlefield for next-generation designs.
